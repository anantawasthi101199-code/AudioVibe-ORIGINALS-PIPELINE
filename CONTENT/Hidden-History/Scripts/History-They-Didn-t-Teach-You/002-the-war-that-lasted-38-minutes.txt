## hook

[warmly] The shortest war ever recorded started at two minutes past nine in the morning, on the 27th of August 1896, and it was over before ten. This is the story of the war between Britain and Zanzibar, an island off the coast of East Africa.

## story

[measured] Zanzibar had a sultan, but it was under British protection, and Britain expected to choose who sat on the throne. On the 25th of August, the sultan, Hamad, died suddenly. Within hours, his young cousin Khalid had walked into the palace and declared himself ruler. Britain wanted a different man, Hamud, who was friendlier to its interests.

[steady] Khalid gathered about three thousand men around the palace, with some old cannons, and a small royal ship anchored just offshore, called the Glasgow. Across the harbour, the British brought up five warships, and their guns were aimed at the palace, which was built largely of wood. On the 26th, the British consul sent an ultimatum: lower the flag and leave by nine o'clock the next morning.

[thoughtful] Khalid sent a message at eight, asking for talks. The British answer was plain. They did not want to fire, it said, but they would if he refused. Nine o'clock came. Nobody moved. At two minutes past, the ships opened fire.

[steady] The palace collapsed in flames, and its guns were silenced quickly. The Glasgow fired back at a British cruiser with its small guns, and it was sunk where it lay. Its crew were rescued in boats flying the British flag. By about twenty minutes to ten, the flag over the palace had been shot down, and the firing stopped. Around five hundred of the men on the sultan's side had been killed or wounded. On the British side, one sailor was hurt.

## close

[warmly] Khalid slipped out through a back door and took shelter in the German consulate, and the Germans got him away to mainland Africa. Hamud became sultan that same afternoon. Then Britain sent the Zanzibar government the bill for the shells it had used. Thirty-eight minutes of war, and the losers paid for the ammunition.
