## cold_open

[steady] "Gold," whispered someone in the front row. "His eyes went gold. For her?"

Kael Blackthorn had already turned away. He said something short to the priest, and the priest shut the book, and that was the end of the wedding. Three hundred wolves in velvet stood up at once and watched the new queen of Blackthorn walk back down the aisle on her own.

[warmly] Last time, Mira Hale, the stepdaughter nobody wanted, was pushed into a wedding dress meant for her stepsister Celeste, and married the wolf king of Blackthorn in front of his whole pack. Celeste called her the flower girl. Kael called her the only one who came. And then his eyes turned gold, which in Blackthorn means one thing only. The wolf inside him had chosen his true mate.

Nobody had told Mira what any of that meant. She had a feeling she was about to find out.

## story

[steady] An hour later she sat in Kael's study, still in the dress, across a long oak table covered in maps. He sat at the far end. Between them lay a single sheet of paper, and a pen.

"One year," said Kael. "You live here as my queen. In name only. You'll have your own rooms, and a key, and nobody will touch you, me least of all. When the year is up, you walk out of that gate free, with enough money to buy a shop of your own."

Mira looked at the paper. She looked at him. "Why?"

"The pack needs a queen at my side," he said. "It doesn't need to be a real one."

"And the gold eyes?"

Kael rubbed the scar through his eyebrow. "My wolf has opinions," he said. "I don't take orders from him."

[warmly] Mira read the contract twice, slowly, the way she read invoices for her stepmother. Then she picked up the pen, and stopped.

"One condition," said Mira. "Nobody in this castle ever calls me the flower girl again."

Kael looked at her for a long moment. Then he took the pen from her hand, wrote a line at the bottom in small black letters, and signed under it. She signed under that.

"Done," said Kael. "Anyone who says it answers to me."

[steady] The next morning she found out how much the pack thought of the arrangement. In the long gallery two noblewomen stopped talking when she passed, and started again, louder, the moment she was behind them. "A shop girl." "He'll be rid of her by spring." A footman bowed to her so slowly it was an insult. Only one person was kind. He found her lost on the east stairs, a tall, golden-haired man with a lovely smile and a velvet coat the colour of wine.

"Lucian Blackthorn," he said, and kissed her hand. "Kael's cousin. The family's spare. Don't let them frighten you, my lady. They're wolves, and wolves sniff at anything new."

He walked her all the way to the library. He laughed at everything she said, even the things that were not funny. He kept her hand a moment longer than he needed to. She liked him. She was almost sure she liked him.

[warmly] That afternoon, in the courtyard, the Hale carriage was finally leaving. Vivian was already inside. Celeste stood by the step, pulling on her gloves, while the castle steward handed over the papers for the bride price. Celeste glanced at the top sheet, out of boredom. Then she read it properly. It was the settlement for the queen's household: her allowance, for one year. The figure was larger than the whole of Thornfield.

Celeste read it a third time. Her pretty face did something complicated. She climbed into the carriage and did not say a single word, and Mira, watching from a window above, had never once in eleven years seen her stepsister lost for words.

[steady] Then Rowan came to find her. Rowan was Kael's second-in-command, broad and cheerful, the man who had held the rings. He had the contract in his hand.

"He'll kill me for this," said Rowan, "but you ought to know what you signed." He laid it on the windowsill and tapped a clause near the bottom, written in Kael's hand, the night before, after she'd gone to bed. If anything happened to the king before the year was out, every coin of the money, and the shop, went to Mira anyway. No conditions. "He wrote that after you'd signed," said Rowan. "He wants you safe whatever happens to him."

Mira looked at the small black letters for a long time. A man who wanted rid of her by spring did not write that.

[warmly] Dinner was in the great hall, under iron chandeliers, with garlands of flowers hung all along the table. An old woman sat on Mira's left, so small she almost vanished into her shawl. This was Grandmother Ysolde, the pack's seer, who was said to be older than the castle. All through the soup she said nothing. Then, as the plates were cleared, she reached out and took Mira's gloved hand in her dry little fingers.

"The bride who was not chosen," Ysolde whispered. She patted the hand once, and went back to her bread as if she had said nothing at all.

[steady] Across the table sat Lady Corvina, the noblewoman from the gallery, in grey silk and a great many pearls. She had watched Mira all evening, the way a cat watches a bird with a bad wing.

"You must find all this very grand," said Lady Corvina, loudly enough for half the table. "I suppose where you come from you eat with your fingers. Do they even have forks in Thornfield? Or only flowers?"

A few people laughed. Lucian, three seats down, gave Mira a sympathetic little shrug. Kael, at the head of the table, put down his knife.

[warmly] But Mira had spent eleven years with flowers, and these garlands were full of them. She looked along the table, quite calmly.

"We have forks," said Mira. "And we have flowers. Those are white lilies, and sweet peas, and the little blue ones are larkspur." She nodded at the garland that hung right over Lady Corvina's plate. "The purple one there is wolfsbane. It's lovely. But I wouldn't let it drip in your wine, my lady. It's poison to wolves, and you've been sitting under it since the soup."

Nobody laughed. Lady Corvina looked up at the purple flowers. Then, without a word, she picked up her plate and her glass, and moved two seats down the table.

Rowan made a sound into his napkin that might have been a sneeze. Somebody at the far end began to clap and thought better of it. And at the head of the table Kael Blackthorn looked at his wife across all those candles, and for the first time since she had met him, he smiled.

## cliffhanger

[steady] Two nights later the moon came up full and white over the forest, and the whole castle went quiet. Servants hurried along the corridors without speaking. Doors were bolted. Somebody let down the iron gate with a long, grinding crash.

There was a knock at Mira's door. She opened it, and Kael was standing there in his shirtsleeves. His face was grey. He held out her key, and she saw that his hands were shaking.

"Lock it," said Kael. "Whatever you hear tonight, don't open it. Not for anyone. Not for me."

[pause] He was gone before she could answer. She turned the key, and sat down on the bed, and listened to his footsteps running away down the stairs.

Then, somewhere below her, the howling started.
