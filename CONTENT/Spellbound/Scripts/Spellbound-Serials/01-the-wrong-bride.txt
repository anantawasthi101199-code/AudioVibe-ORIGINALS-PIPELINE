## cold_open

[warmly] "You owe us for every meal you have ever eaten," said Vivian Hale, and she pushed the wedding dress into Mira's arms.

It was still warm from Celeste. Mira's stepsister had worn it for exactly four minutes that morning, looked out of the carriage window at Blackthorn Castle, and announced that she would not marry a monster for any money in the world. Then she had taken off the dress, sat down in her petticoat, and asked for a cup of tea.

[steady] Mira Hale was twenty-four. She had spent eleven years working without pay in her stepmother's flower shop in Thornfield, and all she wanted was a shop of her own, with her own name over the door. Today she had come to carry the bouquet. Now the Blackthorn pack had paid a fortune for a Hale bride, the fortune was spent, and there was only one Hale left in the carriage.

"Nobody will see your face until it's over," Vivian said, and dropped the veil.

## story

[warmly] Mira had one thing to do before she got out. She found a pair of lace gloves in the bottom of the bouquet box and pulled the left one on, up past the wrist, over the small silver crescent on her skin. She had hidden that mark every day of her life. Her mother had told her to, and had never told her why.

The chapel at Blackthorn Castle was stone, and cold, and full of wolves. They looked like people, in dark velvet and old silver, but they all turned their heads at the same moment when she came through the door. Three hundred faces. Nobody smiled.

[steady] At the far end stood the king. Kael Blackthorn was taller than the stories said. Nobody had seen him in Thornfield for ten years, and the stories said he had claws, and a second row of teeth, and had eaten a tax collector. What he had was a plain black coat, a tired face and a scar through one eyebrow. He watched her walk all the way up the aisle, and he did not look like a man at his wedding. He looked like a man waiting for a dentist.

Beside him stood a broad, cheerful man holding the rings. That was Rowan, his second-in-command, and he gave Mira a small nod, the kind you give somebody on their way to the gallows.

[warmly] The old priest read the words. Mira said hers in a voice that came out smaller than she had planned. Her hands shook. One tear slid under the veil, off her chin, and fell on the chapel floor. She heard it land. Nobody else looked down. So nobody saw the crack between two stones, or the white rose that quietly opened there.

"You may lift the veil," said the priest.

[pause] Kael lifted it.

The chapel gasped, all three hundred of them, because it was the wrong face. Everybody had seen the portrait of Celeste. Mira stood there with her plain brown hair and her flower-stained fingers, and in the front row, in a borrowed blue dress, with her tea finished and her courage back, sat Celeste Hale. She had decided to come and watch.

Celeste stood up. She laughed, a pretty little laugh that carried to the rafters.

"Oh, don't worry, Your Majesty," said Celeste. "That's not a bride. She's just the flower girl."

[steady] Somebody in the third row sniggered, and then a few more did. Mira felt the old heat climb her neck. She knew this feeling very well. She had eleven years of practice. She looked at the floor and waited for it to be over.

Kael did not look at Celeste. He kept looking at Mira, at the gloved hand that was shaking and the chin she was trying to hold up.

"The contract says a daughter of the Hale house would stand at this altar today," said Kael. His voice was quiet, and it reached every corner of the room. "I count one woman at this altar. You, I understand, sent word this morning that you would rather die."

Celeste's mouth stayed slightly open.

"So she's the flower girl," said Kael. "Then she's the only one here who came."

[warmly] Nobody sniggered after that. Rowan coughed into his fist, and it was not really a cough. Celeste sat down very slowly, and turned the colour of a boiled ham. And Mira, who had not had one person take her side since she was thirteen years old, found she had forgotten how to breathe.

## cliffhanger

[steady] The crowd began to shuffle. The priest was looking for his place in the book. Mira thought that was all. She thought she would be led off somewhere, and fed, and forgotten, like always.

Kael stepped closer. He bent his head towards her neck, the way you might lean over a flower, and he breathed in once, slowly.

Then he went very still.

[pause] When he lifted his head, the tired grey had gone out of his eyes. They had turned gold, bright as a coin, and from the front row an old woman's stick hit the floor.

"You're not the woman I was promised," Kael said quietly. "But you're the one my wolf just chose."
