## setup

[warmly] The landline in Mia Lewis's hallway had been disconnected since before she was born, and on the first night of March it rang.

She'd only kept the old green telephone because it came with her father's house. She picked it up as a joke. "Hello?"

"Oh, thank goodness," said a young woman, laughing. "I've been dialling my sister for ten minutes. Who's this?"

Her name was Jo. It was a Friday, she said, and she was eating chips on the stairs, and it was the first of March 1995. Mia thought it was a prank, until Jo described the song on her radio and the news that night, and it all checked out. The line opened at nine, every night, for as long as a lamp stayed on in the hall. It had one rule. Neither of them could ever say a full name. The moment they tried, it went dead for a day.

Jo had a laugh that started high and fell down the stairs. Mia was sure she knew it from somewhere.

## turn

[steady] They talked every night. It became the best part of the day. Mia was thirty-one, and new to being alone. Jo was twenty-six, and pregnant with her first baby, and terrified.

"What's the future like?" said Jo. "Tell me it's good."

"The phones are smaller," said Mia. "We're still all sad on them."

Jo laughed that falling laugh. She told Mia about the cot she was painting yellow, and about the husband who sang flat in the bath. She told her she was scared she'd be no good at this. Mia told her about her dad, who had brought her up on his own and never talked about before.

Then, one night in June, Jo sounded tired. The doctors had found something, she said. It wasn't good, but she didn't want to talk about it. She was just glad the baby was on the way.

"The line's getting faint," said Jo. "Have you noticed?"

Mia had noticed. She went up to the attic to look for the old phone bills, for anything that might explain it. She found a shoebox instead. On top was a photograph of a young woman on a staircase, eating chips, laughing with her whole face. On the back, in her father's writing, it said: Jo. March 1995. A pot of yellow paint stood behind her on the step.

## twist

[pause] Mia ran down the stairs. The green phone was already ringing. It sounded very far away.

"Hello, it's only me," said her mother. "I think this might be the last one. I just wish I knew she'd be all right."

Mia sat down on the same stair and held the phone in both hands.

"She's fine," said Mia. "She's going to be so fine. Your daughter turned out fine, I promise you."

The line was quiet. Then, very faint, came a laugh that started high and fell all the way down the stairs.

"Oh," said Jo. "I thought it might be you."
