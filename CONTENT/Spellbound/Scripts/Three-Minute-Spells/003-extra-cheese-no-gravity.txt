## setup

[warmly] Every Friday at nine, Sam Okafor delivered two large pizzas to an empty field, and every Friday somebody paid him in gold.

It had been going on for a year. The order came through to Marco's Pizza, in the town of Dunmore, with no name and no phone number. It only had a grid reference and a note: extra cheese, no gravity. Marco thought it was a joke from the students. But when Sam rode out on his scooter, there was a steel box waiting in the long grass, with one small gold coin inside, and a jeweller on the high street paid him two hundred pounds for each one. Sam left the pizzas on the box and rode home, and by the time he looked back, they had gone.

He didn't ask. He was twenty-two, he had rent to pay, and the rule of the job seemed to be that nobody looked too hard.

## turn

[steady] But it was a long ride, and a man starts to think.

"Who has pizza in a field?" said Sam.

"Who pays in gold?" said Marco, counting it. "Take the extra garlic bread. Be nice."

So Sam started leaving a little something on top. The first week it was garlic bread. The next he left a handwritten note: Try the chilli oil. The Friday after that, the box held two gold coins and a square of grey paper covered in very careful capital letters. YOU WERE CORRECT ABOUT THE CHILLI OIL.

[warmly] After that they wrote every week. Sam told them about the time he'd dropped a whole order on the ring road. They asked what a ring road was. He told them the best pizza was the one you ate at midnight, straight from the box, standing up. They wrote back: WE HAVE BEEN EATING IT STANDING ON THE CEILING. IS THIS ACCEPTABLE? He laughed so hard he nearly fell off the scooter. He assumed it was the students after all, and that he'd made some very odd friends.

Then, one Friday in October, the order was different. It asked for two hundred large pizzas. There was only one line in the note. BRING THE RECIPE.

Marco looked at it for some time. Then he took the old book down from the shelf and put it into Sam's hands.

## twist

[pause] The field was not empty. Above the long grass, something the size of a shopping centre hung in the air without a sound, and the pizzas lifted gently off the back of Sam's scooter and floated up into a square of warm yellow light. His own feet were about an inch off the ground.

Extra cheese, no gravity. It had never been a joke. It was a cooking instruction.

A folded square of grey paper drifted down into his hand. FIRST BRANCH, it said. ALPHA CENTAURI. WE NEED A MANAGER. THERE IS A SCOOTER.

Sam looked back at the lights of Dunmore, and then he got out his order pad.

"Do you lot deliver?" he said.
