## setup

[warmly] Theo Marsh had been dead since 1891, and for the last eleven years he had worked as a night porter at St Agnes Hospital, pushing trolleys from nine at night until six in the morning.

It suited him. Sunlight would turn him to ash in about a minute, so the night was all he had. The blood bank threw out its out-of-date bags every Thursday, and Theo took them home in a cool box, and nobody ever got hurt. He was the most polite vampire in the north of England.

[steady] Then, in October, a new nurse started on Ward Six. Her name was Dana Reyes. She had a loud laugh, a cardigan with holes in both elbows, and the steadiest hands on the night shift. She only ever worked nights. She told him so on her first evening, at three in the morning, in the staff room, while everybody else attacked the tin of hospital biscuits.

"Days aren't for me," said Dana. "I keep the blinds shut at noon and sleep like a bat."

Theo noticed she never touched the biscuits. He liked that about her. He couldn't eat them either.

## turn

[warmly] By December they took their break together every night. They sat on the fire escape, under the one light that worked, and she talked about her patients and he talked about the city as if he remembered it a hundred years ago, which he did.

"You talk like my granddad," said Dana. "Are you secretly ninety?"

"Older," said Theo. She laughed, and he let her.

He was falling for her, badly, and he knew exactly how it ended. One day she would want breakfast. A walk on the beach. A holiday somewhere hot. She would want a whole life with the sun in it, and he would have to tell her he couldn't stand in a doorway at noon.

[steady] On Christmas Eve, at half past five in the morning, she asked him.

"Come to mine for breakfast," said Dana. "I'll do pancakes. Proper ones."

The sky over the car park was already going grey. Theo looked at it, and then at her, and decided he would rather she knew.

"Dana," he said, "I can't come for breakfast. I can't ever come for breakfast. I'm not ill, and I'm not married. I'm a vampire. I've been one since 1891. And I understand completely if you'd like me to leave."

## twist

[pause] Dana looked at him for a long moment. Then she reached past him into the staff room, picked up the tin of hospital biscuits, and held it out.

"Why do you think I never eat these?" said Dana.

She smiled, properly, for the first time, and there they were, two small, neat, very sharp teeth. She'd taken the night shift for exactly the same reason he had.

"The pancakes were a test," said Dana. "Mine are made of nothing. Come round anyway. I've got very good blinds."
