## setup

[warmly] Hannah Clarke had got married forty times, and every single time she woke up the next morning at six o'clock, in her mother's spare room, on her wedding day.

It worked like this. The moment she said "I do", the whole day folded up like a paper fan, and she was back in bed with the alarm going off and the dress hanging on the wardrobe door. Every time, something went wrong. A storm. A fire alarm. The vicar's hiccups. Once, a goose.

[steady] The groom was Simon. Simon was kind and tidy and had a very good pension. He checked his phone a lot during the vows, but everybody had flaws.

Some things never changed, loop after loop. Her mother cried at the car. The best man lost the rings. And at the reception, just as the four-tier cake began to tip off its stand, the caterer always caught it. Every time, he was there one second before it fell. He was a tall, untidy man called Leo, with icing on his sleeve.

## turn

[warmly] On the forty-first morning, Hannah decided to be clever. She made the vicar drink water before the service. She locked the goose in the boot of a car. The sun shone, the alarm stayed quiet, and she stood at the altar beside Simon and said "I do" in her best voice.

[steady] Six o'clock. The alarm. The dress on the wardrobe door.

By the fiftieth loop she had tried everything. She had married Simon in a field, in a registry office, at midnight. Nothing stopped the day coming round again. On the fiftieth reception she sat alone at the cake table in her dress, too tired to cry, and watched the cake start to tip, and watched Leo catch it.

"How do you always know?" said Hannah.

Leo set the cake down very carefully. He looked at her, and he looked more tired than she was.

"Because I've caught it fifty times," said Leo. "Same as you. I've been trying to tell you something since the first one, and every time I open my mouth you get called away for a photograph."

## twist

[pause] The fifty-first morning, Hannah didn't go to the church. She went straight to the kitchen tent, still in her dressing gown, and found Leo icing roses.

"Tell me," said Hannah. "I'm listening."

"You're marrying the wrong man," said Leo. "He's been texting your cousin through the vows. Fifty times I've watched it. And I know because I've been looking at you, and not at the cake."

Simon, when she rang him, said "Oh, thank God," and that was the end of that.

At six o'clock the next morning, Hannah woke up. It was Sunday. There was cake for breakfast, and the caterer was asleep on the sofa with icing on his sleeve.
