## setup

[warmly] The genie came out of the lamp on a Tuesday, and the first thing he did was apologise.

Nina Shah had only been cleaning it. She'd found it at a car boot sale, the week after Daniel left her with half a lease and a note on the fridge. Now a thin blue man sat on her kitchen counter, swinging his legs. He explained the rule. One wish, whatever she asked, and it would cost her nothing but the asking.

He looked around, put the kettle on, and made her a cup of tea: milk first, half a sugar, the bag left in. She drank it without thinking.

## turn

[steady] "I wish Daniel would regret leaving me," said Nina. "I want him sorry. I want him sorry every single day."

The genie looked at his blue hands. "Let's not rush into it," he said.

So she waited. On Wednesday she said it again, and he said the stars were wrong. On Thursday she said it again, and he offered her a very nice car instead.

"You're the worst genie in the world," said Nina.

"I know," he said. "Are you eating? You always stop eating when you're sad."

She stared at him. "How would you know that?"

[warmly] He didn't answer. He made toast. He cut it into triangles and set it in front of her. By Saturday she had stopped crying at the fridge. By Sunday, they were doing the crossword together, and she was laughing at his terrible guesses.

On Monday she picked up the lamp and turned it over in her hands. "It's the wish," she said slowly. "I've been wasting it on him, haven't I?"

The genie went very quiet. "I can't tell you what to wish for," he said. "That's the one rule I'm not allowed to bend."

Nina thought about it for a long time. Then she put down the lamp.

"I wish," she said, "to be happy. Whatever that takes. With him or without him."

## twist

[pause] The blue ran out of him like water out of a bath. The thin man on the counter got shorter, and paler, and grew a chin she knew. Daniel stood barefoot on her kitchen floor. He had been in that lamp since the day he wrote the note. He had been sorry every single day.

He knew how she took her tea, of course. He'd made it for six years.

"Thank you," said Daniel. He bowed, the way the genie had, and he picked up his shoes, and he walked out of her life for good.

Nina put the kettle on, and for the first time in months, she made it for one.
