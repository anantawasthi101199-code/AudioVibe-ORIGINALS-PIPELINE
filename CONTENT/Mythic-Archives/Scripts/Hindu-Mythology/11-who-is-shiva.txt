## opening

[warmly] Shiva is one of the greatest gods in Hinduism, and the strangest. He lives alone on a snowy mountain. He smears his body with ash from funeral fires, and wears a snake around his neck. [curious] He is the destroyer of the universe, and the gentlest of fathers. He can sit still for thousands of years, and dance so wildly the worlds shake.

[thoughtful] Today you'll hear who he is: what he looks like, what each of his signs means, and why he seems to break every rule. And you'll hear one story from the Puranas, the old books of stories, about the day he walked into a forest full of proud holy men, and what they tried to do to him.

[softly] So let's go to his mountain first, and meet him properly.

## story

[with quiet awe] Far in the north, above the clouds of the Himalayas, stands Mount Kailash, a great peak of snow and rock. That is where Shiva lives. [thoughtful] There is no palace. He sits on a tiger skin, on bare rock, in deep meditation, his eyes half closed, as still as the mountain itself.

[curious] Look at him closely. His skin is pale, because it is covered in ash. His hair is long and matted, piled up on his head in thick coils. [warmly] In his hair, a thin crescent moon shines. And from the top of his head, a river pours out, the holy river Ganga, which he caught in his hair when she fell from heaven, so her force would not shatter the earth. [hushed] On his forehead is a third eye, usually closed. When it opens, it burns whatever it looks at to ashes.

[thoughtful] His throat is blue. Once, when the gods and demons churned the great ocean, a deadly poison rose up that could have destroyed every living thing, and Shiva swallowed it, and held it in his throat to save the world. [curious] Around his neck, a cobra. In one hand, a trident, a three-pronged spear. In another, a small drum shaped like an hourglass, called a damaru. When he beats it, creation begins in rhythm.

[warmly] And at the foot of the mountain waits his companion, Nandi, a great white bull, who carries him wherever he goes. [tender] Shiva also has a family. His wife is the goddess Parvati, daughter of the mountains, and their sons are Ganesha, with the head of an elephant, and Kartikeya, a young warrior god.

[thoughtful] The oldest hymns knew him as Rudra, the howler, a wild and frightening god of storms and sickness, who was asked, again and again, to keep his arrows away. [with quiet awe] But later, in a book of teachings called the Svetasvatara Upanishad, Rudra becomes much more. He is called the one god, who made the worlds and protects them and draws them back in at the end, the one who lives in every heart. The name Shiva means kind, auspicious, the one who brings good. [knowingly] Fierce and kind, both at once. That is who he is.

[curious] Now, the story. In the Puranas, there was once a beautiful pine forest, called Daruvana, where a great group of sages lived with their wives. They were famous for their rituals. They performed every ceremony exactly right, every day. [serious] And they had become very proud of it. They believed that perfect ritual was all that mattered, and that it made them the most powerful beings in the world.

[thoughtful] So one day, Shiva decided to visit them. He did not come as a god. He came as a wandering beggar, young, naked but for the ash on his skin, with matted hair, carrying a bowl made from a skull, singing and dancing as he walked.

[with a smile] The sages' wives saw him, and they were enchanted. They left their cooking and their chores, and followed the strange, beautiful beggar through the trees, laughing and dancing. [darkly] The sages were furious. Who was this filthy, shameless man, ruining their perfect order? [serious] They cursed him. Nothing happened. So they used their power to destroy him.

[grave] They lit a great fire, chanted spells, and out of the flames sprang a tiger, huge and snarling. It leapt at Shiva. [hushed] He caught it, killed it with one hand, peeled off its skin, and wrapped it around his waist, like a cloth. That is why he wears a tiger skin.

[building] They sent snakes, hissing and writhing. Shiva picked them up and hung them around his neck and his arms like jewellery. They sent a deer with sharp horns. He caught it, and held it gently in one hand. [darkly] Last of all, they sent a demon, a small, ugly dwarf who stood for forgetfulness and ignorance. Shiva put his foot on its back, and pinned it to the ground. [pause] And then, standing on the dwarf, he began to dance.

## close

[with quiet awe] The ground shook under Shiva's dance. The sages' fire went out. Their chanting stopped. And at last the proud sages understood who was standing in their forest. They fell at his feet. He forgave them, and taught them that their rituals had been empty, because they had forgotten the god the rituals were for.

[thoughtful] For the people who told this story, that was Shiva. He does not live by the rules of polite society, or by the rules of ritual. He lives beyond them. He takes everything that is frightening, the tiger, the snake, the poison, death and ash, and he wears it calmly, because none of it has power over him. [warmly] That is why people pray to him to destroy what is old and stale, so that something new can begin.

[gently] Picture him one last time. A naked beggar in a pine forest, covered in ash, a tiger skin around his waist, snakes around his arms, a deer resting in his hand, dancing.
