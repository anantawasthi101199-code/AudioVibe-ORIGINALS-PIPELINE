## opening

[warmly] This is the story of Sati, the first wife of the god Shiva, and of her father, Daksha, a proud king of creation. It's told in the Shiva Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, two of the great old books of Hindu stories.

[thoughtful] It's a love story between a princess and a strange god who lived on a mountain. And it's the story of a father who could not forgive his daughter's husband for being who he was.

[softly] It does not end happily. It ends in fire, in grief, and in Shiva's terrible anger. So let's go back to the beginning, when Sati was a little girl, and hear it properly.

## story

[thoughtful] First, who's who. Brahma is the creator god, who made the world. One of his sons was Daksha, a great Prajapati, which means a lord of creatures, one of the powerful beings who helped Brahma fill the world with life. [warmly] Daksha had many daughters. The youngest and most loved was Sati. And Shiva is the god who lives alone on a mountain, covered in ash, who destroys the world so it can begin again.

[tender] From the time she was a small girl, Sati loved Shiva. Other children played games. Sati made little images of him out of clay, and worshipped them, and sang his name. [determined] When she grew up, she went into the forest and prayed to him and fasted, eating nothing but leaves, then nothing at all, so that he would notice her.

[with quiet awe] And Shiva noticed. He came down from his mountain, and he asked her to be his wife. [warmly] Brahma himself persuaded Daksha to agree, and the wedding was held. Sati went to live with Shiva on Mount Kailash, among the snow and the rocks, and they were happy. [tender] He, who had always been alone, loved her more than anything.

[serious] But Daksha never liked Shiva. He thought his daughter's husband was a disgrace. A god who smeared himself in the ash of the dead, wandered with ghosts and wild spirits, wore snakes and animal skins, and cared nothing for good manners.

[darkly] Then one day there was a great gathering of the gods and the sages, a sacred ceremony. When Daksha walked in, everyone stood up to honour him. Everyone except Brahma, his father, and Shiva, who sat still, lost in his own thoughts. [grave] Daksha took it as an insult, in front of everyone. He cursed Shiva, and declared that he would never again receive a share of the offerings made to the gods.

[thoughtful] Some time later, Daksha decided to hold a great sacrifice of his own. To understand why that mattered, you need to know what a sacrifice was. It was a huge ceremony, lasting days, around a sacred fire. Priests poured offerings into the flames, butter, grain and more, and each god received a portion. Being left out was a public insult. [serious] Daksha invited every god and every sage in the universe. Every one, except Shiva.

[curious] On Mount Kailash, Sati saw the gods and their wives flying overhead in their chariots, dressed in their finest clothes. She asked where they were going. When she found out, she turned to Shiva. My father is holding a great sacrifice. My sisters will be there. Let's go.

[gently] Shiva said no. He had not been invited, and he told her that going to the house of someone who hates you, even your own father, brings only pain. [softly] She begged him. He still would not go. So Sati went alone.

[grave] At her father's sacrifice, nobody welcomed her except her mother and sisters. Daksha would not even look at her. She saw that there was no portion set aside for Shiva at all. [darkly] And her father, in front of the whole gathering, insulted her husband again.

[determined] Sati stood up. Her voice was steady. She said she could no longer bear to have a body that had come from a man who spoke against Shiva. She would not live as Daksha's daughter. [hushed] She sat down on the ground, facing north, and closed her eyes. She went deep into meditation, and called up the fire that lives inside every body. And the fire came. [softly] In front of all of them, Sati's body burst into flames, and she was gone.

[grave] When the news reached Kailash, Shiva's grief turned into a fury that shook the mountain. He tore a single lock of hair from his head and dashed it onto the ground. [building] From it rose Virabhadra, a terrifying warrior, with a thousand arms and eyes like fire, and an army of Shiva's wild companions behind him.

[grave] They swept down onto Daksha's sacrifice. They broke the sacred pots, put out the fires, and scattered the priests and the gods. [darkly] And Virabhadra seized Daksha, cut off his head, and threw it into the sacrificial fire.

## close

[thoughtful] When it was over, Brahma and the other gods went to Shiva, and bowed down, and asked him to forgive. Shiva's anger cooled. He let the sacrifice be finished. [softly] And he brought Daksha back to life, but his head had burned in the fire, so he was given the head of a goat. Humbled, Daksha bowed to Shiva at last.

[tender] But nothing could bring Sati back. Shiva went to where her body lay, and his grief was beyond anything the gods had seen. [thoughtful] For the people who told this story, it showed that Shiva, the god beyond all rules, could love, and could lose, and could grieve like any husband. And it showed what pride costs. Daksha had a perfect sacrifice, and lost his daughter and his own head to it.

[gently] Picture Sati one last time, as a little girl, shaping a tiny figure of Shiva out of clay, and singing to it.
