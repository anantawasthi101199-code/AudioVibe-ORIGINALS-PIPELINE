## opening

[warmly] In Hindu mythology, the gods and their enemies are always at war. The gods are called devas, the shining ones. Their enemies are called asuras, and they are often called demons. [curious] But here is the surprise. They are not strangers. They are brothers. They have the same father.

[thoughtful] This is a story from the Mahabharata, India's great epic, about why that war never ends. And it is told through one young man, sent by the gods on a dangerous mission into the land of their enemies.

[gently] You'll meet three people. Kacha, a young student. Shukra, the wise teacher of the asuras. And Devayani, Shukra's daughter. [softly] There is a secret at the centre of it, a secret that decides who wins every battle. So let's go back to the beginning, to the family where the war started, and hear it properly.

## story

[thoughtful] Long ago, there was a great sage named Kashyapa, one of the first beings in the universe, a father of many races. He had several wives, and they were sisters. [slowly] One wife, Aditi, gave birth to the devas, the gods of light, Indra, king of the gods, and the sun and others with him. Another wife, Diti, gave birth to the daityas. A third, Danu, gave birth to the danavas. Together the daityas and the danavas are called the asuras. [matter-of-fact] So the gods and the asuras are half-brothers. Same father, different mothers.

[serious] And like brothers who cannot share, they fought over everything. Over heaven. Over the three worlds, earth, sky and everything in between. Over the offerings that people made at their sacred fires. Both sides were strong. Both sides were proud. [darkly] And for a long time, neither side could win for good.

[curious] Each side had a teacher, a guru, who guided them and performed their sacred rites. The guru of the gods was Brihaspati, a sage of great learning. The guru of the asuras was Shukra, also called Shukracharya. And Shukra knew something that nobody else in the universe knew. [hushed] He knew the sanjivani, the secret of bringing the dead back to life.

[grave] You can imagine what that meant in war. When the gods killed asuras in battle, Shukra simply brought them back. They stood up, picked up their weapons, and fought again. But when the asuras killed gods, the gods stayed dead. [pause] The gods were losing. Slowly, but surely.

[thoughtful] So the gods went to Kacha. Kacha was the son of Brihaspati, their own guru. He was young, handsome, good, and very clever. [warmly] They said to him: go to Shukra. Ask him to take you as his student. Serve him faithfully, learn everything you can, and in the end, learn the secret of life. [serious] And they gave him a piece of advice. Shukra has a daughter he loves more than anything. Her name is Devayani. If you please her, Shukra will be pleased with you.

[curious] Kacha travelled to the city of the asuras, where their king, Vrishaparva, ruled and Shukra lived. He bowed before Shukra and said: I am Kacha, the son of Brihaspati. Take me as your student. I will serve you for a thousand years. [thoughtful] In those days, a teacher could not refuse a sincere student, whoever his father was. And Shukra was a generous man. He said yes.

[warmly] So Kacha lived in Shukra's house and served him. He fetched wood for his fire. He looked after his cows. And he was kind to Devayani. He sang for her, he danced for her, he brought her flowers and fruit. And slowly, Devayani fell in love with him.

[darkly] But the asuras were not fools. They knew exactly why the son of the gods' teacher was living among them. And one day, when Kacha was out alone in the forest with the cows, they caught him, killed him, cut his body into pieces, and fed them to wild dogs.

[hushed] That evening, the cows came home without him. Devayani ran to her father in tears. Kacha has not come back, she said. Something terrible has happened. I cannot live without him. [softly] Shukra loved his daughter. So he used his secret. He spoke the words of the sanjivani, and called Kacha. And Kacha came back to life, burst out of the bodies of the dogs, whole again, and walked into the house.

[serious] The asuras tried again. This time, they killed him, ground his body into a paste, and mixed it into the sea. And again Devayani wept, and again Shukra called Kacha back to life.

[darkly] So the third time, the asuras thought of something cleverer. They killed Kacha, burned his body, and mixed the ashes into wine. And they gave the wine to Shukra himself to drink. [pause] Now, they thought, if Shukra brings him back, he must tear his way out of his own teacher's stomach, and Shukra will die.

[hushed] Devayani came again, weeping. Father, Kacha is gone again. [slowly] Shukra sighed. He said, my child, he is dead, and the dead should be allowed to go. But Devayani said she would not eat, she would not drink, she would die herself unless Kacha came back. [softly] So Shukra spoke the words, and called Kacha.

[with quiet awe] And from inside Shukra's stomach, Kacha's voice answered. Master, I am here. The asuras burned me, and you drank me in your wine.

[thoughtful] Now Shukra understood the trap. If he brought Kacha out, he himself would die. If he did not, Kacha stayed dead and his daughter would die of grief. [a beat] So he found the only way out. Inside his stomach, he taught Kacha the secret. The whole sanjivani, the words that bring the dead back to life.

[building] Then Kacha burst out of Shukra's body, alive, shining like the full moon. And his teacher fell dead at his feet. [with quiet awe] Kacha knelt down at once, and used what he had just learned. He spoke the words. And Shukra opened his eyes and stood up.

[serious] Shukra was angry, but not with Kacha. He was angry with the wine that had made him drink his own student without knowing it. And from that day, he made a rule: any priest, any brahmin, who drinks wine will lose all his goodness, and be looked down on in this world and the next.

## close

[warmly] Kacha stayed on with Shukra until his thousand years of study were complete. Then he got ready to go home to the gods, with the secret.

[softly] Devayani came to him. You know I love you, she said. Now marry me. [gently] But Kacha refused. He said, I was born again from your father's body. That makes me your brother. I cannot marry you. [grave] Devayani was heartbroken, and she cursed him: the knowledge you came for will never work for you. [thoughtful] Kacha answered calmly. Then I will teach it to others, and it will work for them. And because her curse was unjust, he cursed her in return, that no son of a sage would ever marry her. Then he went home and taught the secret to the gods.

[thoughtful] So the gods could bring back their dead too, and the war went on, even again, as it always does. For the people who told this story, that was the point. The devas and the asuras are one family. They have the same father, their teachers are both wise, and each side has its good people and its cruel ones. Every age, they fight again. Neither side can wipe the other out.

[tender] Picture it one last time. A teacher, lying dead on the floor of his own house. And his student, kneeling beside him, speaking the words that bring him back.
