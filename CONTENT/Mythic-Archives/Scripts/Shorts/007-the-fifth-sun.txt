## story

[warmly] This is the Aztec story of how our sun was made, and of the humble little god who was brave enough to become it.

[hushed] The Aztecs, who lived in Mexico long ago, believed the world had already had four suns, and each one had been destroyed. When the fourth was gone, the world was completely dark. [serious] So the gods gathered in the darkness, at an ancient city, and asked a question. Who will become the new sun?

[slyly] A rich, proud god called Tecuciztecatl stepped forward at once. I will, he said. He wore fine feathers and gold, and he wanted the glory. [softly] The gods asked who else would try. Nobody spoke. Then they turned to a small god at the edge of the crowd, Nanahuatzin. He was poor and covered in sores, and nobody ever noticed him. Will you? they asked. [gently] And he simply said, yes.

[thoughtful] For four days the two of them prepared. The proud god made offerings of jade and fine feathers. The little god had only reeds and the scabs from his own skin to offer. Meanwhile the other gods built an enormous fire, and it burned for four days until it roared.

[building tension] On the fifth night, the gods formed two lines facing the flames. Tecuciztecatl went first. He ran at the fire. The heat hit him, and he stopped. He tried again, and stopped. Four times he ran at it, and four times he turned back, terrified. [pause] Then it was Nanahuatzin's turn.

## ending

[intense] He did not hesitate. He closed his eyes and jumped straight into the flames. His body crackled and burned. [shamed, quietly] And the proud god, ashamed, finally leapt in after him.

[with wonder] The gods waited in the dark. Slowly, in the east, a glow appeared. The little god rose into the sky as a blazing sun. Then a second sun rose behind him, just as bright, and the gods were worried that the world would burn. So one of them grabbed a rabbit and threw it at the second sun's face. [softly] Its light dimmed, and it became the moon, marked forever with the shape of the rabbit.

[serious] But the new sun would not move. To set him going, the gods gave their own lives, and the wind god blew until he began to travel across the sky.

## close

[warmly] And on a clear night, if you look closely at the moon, you can still see the rabbit.
