## story

[warmly] This is a story from ancient Mesopotamia, in what is now Iraq, about Etana, the first king who ever flew.

[softly] Etana was the king of a city called Kish, one of the first cities ever built. He was a good king, and the gods loved him. [grave] But he and his wife had no children. Without a son, there would be no one to rule after him. [thoughtful] He heard of a plant that grew only in heaven, the plant of birth, which could give a childless couple a son. But how could a man ever reach heaven?

[curious] Meanwhile, far away, an eagle and a serpent lived in the same great tree, the eagle at the top, the serpent at the roots. They had sworn to be friends, and to share their food. [darkly] But one day, while the serpent was out hunting, the eagle swooped down and ate the serpent's young. [intense] The serpent took its revenge. It hid inside the body of a dead wild bull, and when the eagle came to feed, it grabbed him, tore out his feathers, broke his wings, and threw him into a deep pit to die.

[softly] Etana prayed to Shamash, the god of the sun, for help. [knowingly] The sun god told him to go to the pit. You will find an eagle there, he said. He can take you where you need to go.

[tender] Etana found the eagle at the bottom of the pit, starving and broken. For months he fed it, and cared for it, until its feathers grew back and its wings were strong. [pause] Then the eagle said, climb on my back.

## ending

[building tension] Etana held on tight to its feathers, and the eagle rose into the sky. Look down, said the eagle. [with wonder] The land looked like a little hill, and the sea like a pond. They rose higher. The land looked like a garden, and the sea like a trough of water. Higher still, and Etana could not see the land at all.

[intense] He grew afraid, and his grip slipped. The eagle tumbled, and they fell, down and down, until the eagle caught them just above the ground. [determined] But they tried again. This time, Etana did not look down. They rose up through the sky, past the moon, until they reached the gates of heaven.

[reverently] There the gods let Etana take the plant of birth. The eagle carried him home, and soon his wife gave birth to a son.

## close

[softly] And for thousands of years afterwards, the scribes of that land wrote down Etana's name in the list of kings as the shepherd who went up to heaven.
