## story

[warmly] This is the Aztec story of how their great god of the sun and of war was born, already armed, on the top of a mountain, to save his mother.

[softly] On a mountain called Coatepec, which means Serpent Mountain, lived a goddess called Coatlicue. She wore a skirt made of woven snakes, and she was the mother of the moon and the stars. [lightly] One day, as she was sweeping the temple at the top of the mountain, a ball of soft feathers drifted down from the sky. She picked it up and tucked it into her waistband, to keep it.

[with wonder] When she looked for it later, it was gone. And she was pregnant.

[darkly] Her children were furious. Her daughter, Coyolxauhqui, was a fierce warrior goddess, and she had four hundred brothers, the gods of the southern stars. They thought their mother's strange pregnancy had shamed them all. [ominous] So Coyolxauhqui called her brothers together, and told them they must kill their mother.

[building tension] They put on their armour and painted their faces for war, and began to march up the mountain. [hushed] Coatlicue was terrified. But from inside her body, her unborn child spoke to her. Do not be afraid, he said. I know what to do.

[intense] One of the four hundred brothers felt sorry for her, and ran ahead to warn the child. They are coming. They are close. Now they are at the top. [pause] And at that moment, the child was born.

## ending

[intense] He came into the world fully grown, and fully armed. He was Huitzilopochtli. His face and limbs were painted blue, his shield was decorated with eagle feathers, and in his hand he held a serpent made of fire. [darkly] With it, he struck down Coyolxauhqui. Her body tumbled down the mountainside, breaking apart as it fell, and he threw her head up into the sky, where it became the moon.

[building tension] Then he chased the four hundred brothers down the mountain, round and round it, and scattered them across the sky, where they became the stars of the south.

[reverently] And every morning since, the sun rises and chases the moon and the stars away.

## close

[softly] In 1978, workers digging under Mexico City found a huge round stone, carved with the broken body of the moon goddess, lying at the foot of the Aztecs' great temple, exactly where she fell.
