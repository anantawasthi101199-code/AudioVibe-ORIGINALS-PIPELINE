## story

[warmly] This is the story of how a small boy in Ireland earned the name of its greatest hero, all because of a feast, a game, and a very big dog.

[lightly] The boy was called Sétanta. He lived long ago in Ulster, in the north of Ireland, and he was the nephew of the king, Conchobar. [with a gentle smile] He was small for his age, but he was fast, fearless, and better with a hurley than anyone. A hurley is a curved wooden stick, used in an old Irish game, to strike a small hard ball.

[curious] One day the king was invited to a feast at the house of Culann, the finest metalworker in the land. On his way out, the king passed the playing field and saw Sétanta beating the older boys at their game. Come with me, the king said. [brightly] Sétanta said he would follow as soon as the game was finished.

[thoughtful] So the king rode on, and he forgot all about the boy. At the feast, Culann asked if all his guests had arrived, because now he would set his hound loose. [ominous] This was no ordinary dog. It was enormous, so fierce it needed three chains to hold it, and it guarded the house all night. The king said yes, everyone is here. [pause] And the hound was let loose.

[building tension] Night had fallen when Sétanta came running up the road, tapping his ball along with his hurley. The hound smelled him. It came out of the dark, snarling, its jaws wide open, running straight at him.

## ending

[intense] Sétanta did not run. He struck the ball with all his strength, and it shot straight into the hound's open mouth. Then he grabbed the great dog and dashed it against a standing stone. [slowly] The guests rushed out, expecting to find the boy dead, and found him standing beside the body of the hound.

[softly] But Culann was heartbroken. That dog had protected his home, his flocks, and his family. [tender] Sétanta saw it, and he made a promise. I will raise you a puppy from the same family, he said. And until it is grown, I will guard your house myself. [reverently] The king's wise man, a druid, laughed with delight, and gave the boy a new name: Cú Chulainn, the Hound of Culann.

## close

[warmly] And for the rest of his life, Ireland's greatest warrior was named after the dog he had killed, and the promise he made to make it right.
