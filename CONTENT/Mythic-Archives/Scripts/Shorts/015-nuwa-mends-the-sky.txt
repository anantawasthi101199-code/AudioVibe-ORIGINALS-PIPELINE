## story

[warmly] This is the Chinese story of Nüwa, the goddess who made the first people, and then had to save them when the sky itself fell apart.

[softly] After heaven and earth had been made, the world was beautiful. There were mountains and rivers, flowers and animals. But Nüwa wandered through it, and she was lonely. There was nobody to talk to.

[with wonder] So she knelt beside a river, scooped up a handful of yellow mud, and shaped it into a small figure, a little person that looked like her. She set it down on the ground. And it came to life. It jumped up, laughed, and danced around her. [delighted] Nüwa was overjoyed. She made another, and another.

[lightly] But making each person by hand was slow work, and the world was huge. So she took a long rope, dipped it in the mud, and swung it through the air. Drops of mud flew everywhere, and every drop that landed became a person. [warmly] Soon the land was full of people.

[darkly] Then disaster came. Far away, two gods went to war: the god of water, called Gonggong, and the god of fire. Gonggong lost. [intense] In his fury, he ran at a great mountain and smashed his head against it. That mountain was one of the pillars that held up the sky. [ominous] It cracked, and broke. And the sky tore open.

[building tension] Through the hole poured fire that would not go out, and water that would not stop. Floods swept across the land. Wild beasts came out of the forests, and huge birds swooped down to snatch people away. [pause] The people Nüwa had made were dying.

## ending

[determined] So Nüwa went to work. She gathered stones of five colours from the riverbeds, and melted them down in a great fire until they glowed like liquid. [slowly] Then she flew up to the broken sky, and painted it closed with the molten stone, smoothing it over until the hole was gone.

[intense] To hold the sky up, she needed new pillars. So she cut off the four legs of a giant turtle, and stood them up at the four corners of the earth. She killed the black dragon that was causing the floods. And she burned tall reeds, and piled up their ashes to soak up the water.

[softly] The fires died. The floods drained away. The animals went back to the forests, and people were safe.

## close

[gently] But the sky never sat quite straight again, and so the sun and stars still slide towards the west, and China's rivers still run east to the sea.
