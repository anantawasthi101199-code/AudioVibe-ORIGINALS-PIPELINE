## story

[warmly] This is the Japanese story of how Susanoo, the wild storm god, killed a monster with eight heads, and found the most famous sword in Japan inside its tail.

[serious] Susanoo had behaved so badly in heaven that the other gods threw him out. He came down to earth, beside a river in a land called Izumo. [curious] And floating down that river, he saw a pair of chopsticks. That meant people lived upstream. So he walked upriver to find them.

[softly] He found an old man and an old woman, weeping, with a young girl sitting between them. Susanoo asked why they were crying. [grave] We once had eight daughters, the old man said. But every year, a monster comes and eats one of them. Now only this one is left, and it is nearly time for it to come again.

[ominous] The monster was called Orochi. It had eight heads and eight tails. Its eyes were as red as cherries. Its body was so long it stretched across eight valleys and eight hills, and moss and trees grew on its back. Its belly was always swollen and running with blood.

[determined] Susanoo asked if he could marry the girl, whose name was Kushinada. The parents agreed. [with wonder] So, to keep her safe, he turned her into a comb, and tucked her into his hair.

[slyly] Then he told the old couple to brew rice wine, the strongest they had ever made, brewed eight times over. He had them build a fence with eight gates, and at each gate, a platform with a huge bowl of the wine. [pause] Then they waited, in the dark.

## ending

[ominous] The ground shook. Orochi came sliding through the hills. It smelled the wine. Each of its eight heads pushed through a gate, and plunged into a bowl, and drank. [slowly] The wine was so strong that, one by one, the heads drooped, and the monster fell into a deep sleep.

[intense] Susanoo drew his sword and cut Orochi to pieces. The river ran red with its blood. [curious] But when he struck the middle tail, his blade broke with a clang against something hard. He cut the tail open, and found a sword inside, gleaming.

[reverently] He knew it was too precious to keep. So he sent it up to heaven as a gift for his sister, the sun goddess, Amaterasu.

## close

[softly] And that sword, which came out of the serpent's tail, became one of the three sacred treasures of the emperors of Japan.
