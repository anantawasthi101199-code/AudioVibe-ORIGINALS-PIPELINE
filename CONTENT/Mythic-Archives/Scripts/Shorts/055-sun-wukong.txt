## story

[warmly] This is the Chinese story of Sun Wukong, the Monkey King, the cheekiest creature who ever lived, and the day he took on heaven itself.

[with wonder] On top of a mountain stood a magic stone. One day it cracked open, and out jumped a monkey, made of stone, with eyes that flashed like lightning. [playfully] He was brave and clever. He led the other monkeys through a waterfall to a secret cave behind it, and they made him their king.

[thoughtful] But one day the Monkey King realised that one day he would grow old and die. So he travelled far to find a wise teacher, and learned magic. He learned to change into seventy-two different shapes. He learned to leap onto a cloud and somersault thousands of miles in one jump. [mischievously] He took a magic iron staff from the Dragon King of the sea, which could grow as tall as a mountain or shrink to the size of a needle, to keep behind his ear. He even crossed out his own name from the book of the dead.

[serious] The Jade Emperor, the ruler of heaven, decided to keep the troublemaker close. He gave him a job in heaven, looking after the gods' gardens of peaches, the magic peaches that made them immortal. [slyly] The Monkey King ate them. He gate-crashed the gods' great feast and ate the food and drank the wine. He gobbled up the pills of immortality too.

[intense] Heaven sent its armies to catch him. He beat them all. They put him in a furnace for forty-nine days, and he jumped out with eyes of fire. [pause] At last, the Jade Emperor sent for the Buddha.

## ending

[gently] The Buddha smiled at the Monkey King. Let us make a bet, he said. If you can jump out of my hand, you can rule heaven. [mischievously] The Monkey King laughed, jumped into the Buddha's open palm, and leapt. He somersaulted thousands of miles, to the very edge of the world, where five great pink pillars stood. He wrote his name on one of them, to prove he had been there, and leapt back.

[slyly] The Buddha lifted up his hand. There, written on one of his fingers, was the Monkey King's name. [with wonder] The five pillars had been the Buddha's own fingers. He had never left his hand at all.

[intense] Before the Monkey King could move, the Buddha turned his hand over, and it became a mountain, and pressed down on top of him.

## close

[softly] And there the Monkey King stayed, trapped under the mountain with only his head sticking out, for five hundred years.
