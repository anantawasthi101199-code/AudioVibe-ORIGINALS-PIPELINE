## story

[warmly] This is the end of the oldest story ever written down, about a king who lost his best friend, and set out to beat death itself.

[softly] Gilgamesh was the king of Uruk, a great walled city in ancient Iraq. He was part god, and stronger than any man. His dearest friend was Enkidu, a wild man the gods had made, who had fought beside him on every adventure. [grave] But the gods were angry with the two friends for killing their monsters, and they decided that one of them must die. Enkidu fell ill, and after twelve days, he died in Gilgamesh's arms.

[slowly] Gilgamesh would not let them bury him. He sat beside the body for seven days and seven nights. Then, at last, he saw that his friend was truly gone. [hushed] And a cold new fear took hold of him. One day, I will die too.

[determined] He had heard of one man who never died: Utnapishtim, who had survived a great flood long ago, and been given everlasting life by the gods. So Gilgamesh set off to find him. He dressed in lion skins and walked to the edge of the world. [with wonder] At the mountain where the sun goes down, half-human scorpion guards let him into a tunnel. For hours he ran through total darkness, and came out in a garden where the trees grew jewels instead of fruit.

[building tension] Then he reached the sea, and the Waters of Death. A single drop on your skin would kill you. A boatman ferried him across, using long poles so their hands never touched the water. [pause] And on the far shore, an old man was waiting.

## ending

[thoughtful] Utnapishtim listened, and set Gilgamesh a test. If you want to live forever, stay awake for seven nights. [softly] Gilgamesh sat down, and fell asleep at once. He slept for seven days. [tender] Then, as Gilgamesh was leaving in despair, the old man told him a secret. At the bottom of the sea grows a thorny plant, he said. Whoever eats it becomes young again.

[intense] Gilgamesh tied heavy stones to his feet and sank down into the deep water. He grabbed the plant, though its thorns tore his hands, cut the stones loose, and burst back up to the surface. [slowly] On his way home, he stopped to bathe in a cool pool. And while he was in the water, a snake smelled the plant, slid out of the reeds, and ate it. [softly] As it slipped away, it shed its old skin.

## close

[tender] Gilgamesh sat down and wept. Then he went home to Uruk, and looked up at its great walls, the one thing he had built that would outlast him.
