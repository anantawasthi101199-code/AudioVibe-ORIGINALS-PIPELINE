## story

[warmly] This is a story from the Shahnameh, the great Persian Book of Kings, about a prince called Zahhak, and the devil who made him a monster.

[softly] Zahhak was the son of a good king, in a land to the west of Persia. He was brave, but foolish, and easily flattered. [darkly] The devil, the spirit of evil, saw that, and came to him in disguise, as a friendly young man. [slyly] With sweet words, he tempted Zahhak to want his father's throne. And then he helped him kill his father, by digging a pit in the garden path the old king walked each morning.

[ominous] So Zahhak became king. And the devil changed his shape again, and came to the palace as a cook. [thoughtful] In those days, people did not eat meat. They ate only plants and grains. The devil cooked Zahhak eggs, then birds, then lamb, and each meal was more delicious than the last. [delighted] Zahhak was thrilled. What reward would you like? he asked.

[softly] Only one small thing, said the cook. Let me kiss your shoulders. [pause] Zahhak laughed, and let him.

## ending

[intense] As the cook's lips touched him, he vanished. And from each of Zahhak's shoulders, a black snake grew, writhing and hissing. Zahhak cut them off. They grew straight back. [darkly] Then the devil returned, disguised as a doctor. There is only one cure, he said. Feed the snakes human brains, every day, and they will be quiet.

[grave] Zahhak went on to rule Persia for a thousand years, a cruel tyrant. Every day, two young men were killed to feed his snakes. [tender] But two brave cooks in his kitchen came up with a secret plan. Each day, they saved one of the two young men, mixing a sheep's brains with the other, and sent the survivor to hide in the mountains.

[firmly] At last a young hero rose up against the tyrant, captured him, and chained him deep inside a cave in the highest mountain in Persia, where he could hurt no one ever again.

## close

[softly] And the young men the cooks had saved lived on in the mountains, and their children's children are said to be the people who live there still.
