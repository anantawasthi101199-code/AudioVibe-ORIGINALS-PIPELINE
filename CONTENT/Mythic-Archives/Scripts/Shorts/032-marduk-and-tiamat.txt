## story

[warmly] This is the Babylonian story of how the world was made, from the body of a dragon of the sea.

[hushed] In the beginning, there was only water. Fresh water, which was a god called Apsu, and salt water, which was a goddess called Tiamat. Their waters mingled together, and from them, the first gods were born.

[lightly] But the young gods were noisy. They laughed and danced and stamped, and the noise kept their great-grandfather awake. [darkly] So Apsu decided to kill them all. Before he could, the wisest of the young gods cast a spell, put him to sleep, and killed him instead.

[ominous] Tiamat was furious. She was the sea itself, vast and deep, and she began to make an army. Monsters, serpents with poison for blood, dragons, scorpion men, raging storm demons. At their head she put her new champion, Kingu, and hung on his chest the Tablet of Destinies, which gave power over everything.

[serious] The young gods were terrified. One after another, the bravest went out to face her, and came back running. [determined] Then a young god stepped forward. His name was Marduk. Tall, shining, with four eyes and four ears that saw and heard everything. I will fight her, he said. [pause] But if I win, you must make me king of all the gods.

## ending

[building tension] They agreed. Marduk armed himself with a bow and a mace, a great net, and the four winds. He rode out in a chariot pulled by storms. [intense] Tiamat opened her huge mouth to swallow him. Marduk sent a raging wind straight into it, so she could not close her jaws. Her body swelled. And he shot an arrow down her throat, through her heart.

[slowly] Then he stood over her body, and split it in two, like a shellfish. The top half he lifted up, to make the sky, and he set the stars in it. The bottom half he laid down, to make the earth. [with wonder] From her two eyes, he made the great rivers, the Tigris and the Euphrates, flow. He took the Tablet of Destinies from Kingu, and from Kingu's blood he made the first humans, to work the land.

## close

[reverently] Then the grateful gods built Marduk a city on the earth for his home, and called it Babylon.
