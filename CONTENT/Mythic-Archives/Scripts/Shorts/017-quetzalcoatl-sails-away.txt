## story

[warmly] This is the story of Quetzalcoatl, the feathered serpent of ancient Mexico, a gentle god-king who was tricked by his greatest rival into leaving his people forever.

[with wonder] Long ago, before the Aztecs, a people called the Toltecs had a beautiful city named Tollan. Its king was Quetzalcoatl, a god in human form, wise and kind. [softly] He taught his people to grow maize, to work gold and jade, to weave fine cloth, and to count the days of the year. Under him, the maize grew so huge a man could barely carry one cob. He asked his people to offer him only flowers, butterflies and snakes, and never human blood.

[darkly] But another god hated him. His name was Tezcatlipoca, which means Smoking Mirror, a god of night and trickery, who carried a magic mirror of black stone that showed the truth.

[slyly] One day Tezcatlipoca came to the palace disguised as a wrinkled old man, and asked to see the king. He held up his black mirror. [hushed] Quetzalcoatl looked in it, and for the first time he saw himself as old. His face was lined. His eyes were sunken. He was horrified. If my people see me like this, he said, they will run from me.

[knowingly] The old man had an answer. Drink this, he said, and you will feel young again. He offered a strong drink made from the cactus plant. [building tension] The king refused. Then he took just a taste. Then he drank more. And more. [pause] And soon, Quetzalcoatl, the wise and holy king, was completely drunk.

## ending

[grave] That night, he forgot every sacred duty. He forgot his prayers and his offerings. He sang and stumbled through the palace. [slowly] In the morning he woke up, and remembered everything, and was filled with shame.

[softly] He could not stay. He burned his beautiful palaces. He buried his treasures in the mountains. He turned his cocoa trees into plain desert bushes, and sent the bright birds of Tollan flying away before him. [reverently] Then he walked east, all the way to the sea. There he made a raft of woven serpents, stepped onto it, and sailed away over the water, towards the rising sun.

## close

[hushed] And before he left, he promised his people that one day, from across that same eastern sea, he would come back.
