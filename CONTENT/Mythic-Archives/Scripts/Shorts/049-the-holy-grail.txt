## story

[warmly] This is the story of the greatest quest of King Arthur's knights, the search for the Holy Grail.

[with wonder] One spring, at Camelot, Arthur's castle, the knights were sitting down to a feast. Suddenly there was a crack like thunder. A beam of light, brighter than the sun, filled the hall. [hushed] And floating through the air, covered by a cloth of white silk, came the Holy Grail, the cup from which Jesus had drunk at his last supper. As it passed, every knight found his favourite food and drink in front of him. Then it was gone.

[determined] The knights stood up and swore an oath. They would search for the Grail for a year and a day, until they had seen it clearly. [softly] Arthur wept, because he knew many of them would never come back.

[thoughtful] They rode out in every direction. Most found nothing, or lost their way, or died. Even Lancelot, the greatest knight of all, came close, but he was not pure enough. When he tried to enter the room where the Grail was kept, he was struck down, and lay as if dead for twenty-four days.

[serious] Far away stood a castle called Carbonek. In it lived a king who had been wounded long ago, and could not heal. [grave] Because the king was wounded, his whole land was dying around him. Fields lay bare. Rivers were dry. Nothing grew. [pause] He was waiting for one knight in particular.

## ending

[reverently] That knight was Galahad, Lancelot's young son, the purest knight who ever lived. He came to Carbonek with two companions. That night, the Grail appeared to them, and they were filled with joy. [gently] Galahad touched the wounded king with the blood of a holy spear. And the king was healed. Across his land, the fields turned green again.

[with wonder] Galahad carried the Grail across the sea to a holy city. There, kneeling before it, he was allowed to see what was inside. He saw wonders no one could describe. [softly] And he asked to leave the world at once. His soul rose up to heaven, and a hand reached down from the sky and took the Grail up after him.

## close

[softly] One of the companions sailed home, to tell Arthur's court that the Grail was found, and that it would never be seen on earth again.
