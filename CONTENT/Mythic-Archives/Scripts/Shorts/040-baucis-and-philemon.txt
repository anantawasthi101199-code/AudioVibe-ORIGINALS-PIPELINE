## story

[warmly] This is a Greek story about a poor old couple who shared everything they had with two strangers, and the reward they asked for.

[curious] One day, Jupiter, the king of the gods, and his son Mercury, the messenger god, wanted to see how kind people really were. So they dressed as ragged, tired travellers, and walked into a village in the hills of Phrygia, in what is now Turkey. [darkly] They knocked on door after door, a thousand doors, asking for food and a place to rest. Every door was slammed in their faces.

[softly] Then they came to the smallest house in the village. A tiny hut, with a roof made of reeds. Inside lived an old woman called Baucis and her husband Philemon. They had married young, and grown old there together, poor but happy.

[tender] They invited the strangers in at once. Baucis blew on the embers of the fire until it caught, and fed it with dry bark. Philemon cut a piece of bacon from the smoked side hanging from the beam. [gently] The table had one leg too short, so Baucis pushed a piece of broken pot under it to make it steady, and wiped it down with fresh mint. Then they served everything they had. Olives. Pickled cherries. Radishes. Cheese. Eggs cooked in the ashes.

[thoughtful] And then the old couple noticed something strange. [pause] However much wine they poured, the jug never went empty. It filled itself up again.

## ending

[with wonder] They realised their guests were gods. Trembling, they tried to catch their only goose to cook for them, but it ran and hid behind the gods' legs. [warmly] Jupiter laughed, and told them who they were. Come with us, he said, up the hill.

[slowly] When the old couple looked back, the whole village had vanished beneath a lake. Only their hut was left, and it was changing. Its posts became marble columns, and its reed roof became gold. It was a temple.

[gently] Ask for anything, said Jupiter. [tender] They asked to look after the temple for the rest of their lives, and to die at the same moment, so neither would ever have to live without the other. [softly] Years later, standing together outside the temple, they began to change. Leaves sprouted from them. Bark crept up their bodies. They said goodbye as it closed over them.

## close

[softly] And for hundreds of years, an oak and a linden tree stood there side by side, their branches touching.
