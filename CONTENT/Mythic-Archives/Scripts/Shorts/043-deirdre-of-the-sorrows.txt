## story

[warmly] This is the saddest of all the old Irish stories, about Deirdre, and a prophecy made before she was even born.

[serious] At a feast in Ulster, in the north of Ireland, the wife of the king's storyteller was about to give birth. As the feasting went on, a scream rang out from inside her, from the unborn child itself. [hushed] The king's druid, a wise man and seer, placed his hand on her and listened. A girl will be born, he said. She will be the most beautiful woman in Ireland. And because of her, Ulster will suffer, and great warriors will die.

[darkly] The warriors wanted to kill the baby. But King Conchobar had a different idea. No, he said. I will have her raised in secret. And when she is grown, I will marry her myself.

[softly] So Deirdre grew up hidden in a house deep in the forest, with only an old nurse for company. She never saw a young man. [thoughtful] One winter's day, she watched a calf being killed for food, and its blood spilled on the snow, and a black raven came down to drink it. [tender] I would love a man with those three colours, she said. Hair as black as the raven. Cheeks as red as the blood. Skin as white as the snow.

[knowingly] The nurse knew someone like that. Naoise, a young warrior of the king's household. [pause] And she arranged for Deirdre to see him.

## ending

[tender] They fell in love, and ran away together, with Naoise's two brothers, across the sea to Scotland. For years they lived there happily. [slyly] Then King Conchobar sent a message. Come home, all is forgiven. You have my word you will be safe.

[grave] They came home. And at Conchobar's order, Naoise and his brothers were killed. [softly] Deirdre was forced to live with the king. For a year, she never smiled, never raised her head. [darkly] At last he asked who she hated most, after him. The man who killed Naoise, she said. Then you will spend a year with him, said the king.

[slowly] She was put in a chariot between the two men she hated most. And as it raced along, Deirdre threw herself out, and struck her head against a rock, and died.

## close

[softly] And just as the druid had foretold, the warriors of Ulster turned against their king, and the kingdom was torn apart.
