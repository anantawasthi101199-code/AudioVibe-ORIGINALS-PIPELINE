## story

[warmly] This is a story from the San people of southern Africa, who have lived and hunted in the deserts there for thousands of years. It is about Kaggen, their trickster god, and the first eland.

[curious] Kaggen often took the form of a small praying mantis, a green insect with long folded arms. He was clever and mischievous, always getting into trouble, and always getting out of it. [softly] And one day, he decided to make something.

[with wonder] He took a small piece of leather, cut from his son-in-law's shoe, and dropped it into the water at the edge of a reed bed. Then he went away. [lightly] When he came back the next day, a tiny creature was standing in the reeds. It was an eland, a kind of antelope, though no one had ever seen one before.

[tender] Kaggen loved it like a child. Every day he crept away to the reeds, and called to it softly, and it came to him. He fed it honey, and rubbed honey into its sides until its coat shone. And it grew, and grew, until it was huge and beautiful, the largest antelope of all. [slyly] He told nobody. When he came home without honey, the family asked where it had gone. He just smiled.

[thoughtful] But his family began to wonder. [hushed] So one day his grandson, a young mongoose, hid and followed him to the reeds, and watched. He saw Kaggen call out, and the great eland step out to meet him. [pause] The boy ran home, and told the others what he had seen.

## ending

[grave] While Kaggen was away, the family went down to the reeds. They called the eland the way Kaggen did, and it came to them, trusting. And they killed it, and began to cut up its meat.

[intense] Kaggen found them there. When he saw the blood in the reeds, he wept, and shouted at them in fury. [darkly] He struck at the eland's gall, and a thick darkness poured out over everything, so that no one could see.

[softly] Kaggen picked up his shoe, rubbed it in the dust, and threw it high into the sky, where it hung and began to shine. It was the moon. It gave a gentle light in the dark, so that hunters could find their way home.

## close

[reverently] And the eland, which Kaggen loved best of all his creatures, stayed the most precious animal of the San people, and the one their hunters honour above all others.
