## story

[warmly] This is the story of Sir Gawain, one of King Arthur's knights, and a terrifying game that began with a knight losing his head.

[lightly] It was Christmas at Camelot, Arthur's castle, and the hall was full of knights and ladies, feasting. [ominous] Suddenly the doors crashed open, and a giant rode in on horseback. Everything about him was green. His skin, his hair, his beard, his clothes, even his horse. In one hand he held a branch of holly. In the other, an enormous axe.

[slyly] I have come for a Christmas game, he said. Let any knight here strike me once with this axe. And in a year and a day, I will strike him back.

[serious] The hall went silent. Nobody moved. King Arthur himself stood up to accept. [determined] But his young nephew, Gawain, stepped forward and asked to take his place. The Green Knight knelt, and bared his neck. [intense] Gawain swung the axe, and cut off his head. It rolled across the floor.

[hushed] And the Green Knight's body stood up, walked over, picked up its own head, and held it up by the hair. [slowly] The head opened its eyes, and spoke. Find the Green Chapel, it said, one year from now, and take your blow. Then he rode out into the snow.

[thoughtful] A year passed. Gawain set out in the freezing winter to keep his promise. He found shelter in a castle, where the lord was kind to him. [pause] The lord's wife gave Gawain a green silk belt, and said it would protect his life. And he hid it, and told no one.

## ending

[building tension] On New Year's morning, Gawain found the Green Chapel, a dark mound in a valley. The Green Knight was there, sharpening his axe. Gawain knelt. The axe came down, and Gawain flinched. [slowly] The knight laughed at him. The axe came down again, and stopped. [intense] The third time, it came down and just nicked his neck. A little blood fell on the snow.

[softly] Then the Green Knight revealed who he really was. He was the lord of the castle. The test was his, and Gawain had passed it, all except for hiding the green belt. That was why he had cut him, just a little. [tender] Gawain was so ashamed he could barely speak. But the knight forgave him.

## close

[warmly] Gawain wore that green belt for the rest of his life, as a reminder, and the knights of the Round Table all began to wear green belts too, in his honour.
