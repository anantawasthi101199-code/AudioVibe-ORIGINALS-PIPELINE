## story

[warmly] This is a Greek story about a sculptor who carved the woman of his dreams, and then fell in love with her.

[softly] Pygmalion lived on the island of Cyprus, in the eastern Mediterranean Sea. He was a sculptor, a man who carved statues. He lived alone, and he had never married. [thoughtful] He had never met anyone he could love.

[with wonder] So he took a block of white ivory, and began to carve a woman. He worked day after day, with tiny chisels and fine files, smoothing every curve. And when he was finished, she was more beautiful than any woman who had ever lived. [softly] She looked so real that you would think she was about to move, or speak, or breathe.

[tender] Pygmalion fell in love with her. He could not stop touching her, to check whether she was really only ivory. He brought her gifts, the kinds of things girls love: shining shells, smooth pebbles, little birds, flowers, beads of amber. He dressed her in fine clothes, put rings on her fingers, and a necklace around her neck. At night he laid her on soft cushions, and talked to her.

[curious] Then came the great festival of Venus, the goddess of love, the most important day of the year on Cyprus. [softly] Pygmalion brought an offering to her altar. He was too shy to ask for what he really wanted. So he prayed for a wife like his ivory girl. [pause] Venus, who was there in the temple, knew exactly what he meant.

## ending

[with wonder] The flame on the altar leapt up three times. Pygmalion hurried home. He bent down and kissed the statue. [hushed] Her lips felt warm. He touched her arm, and the ivory went soft under his fingers, like wax in the sun. He felt a pulse beating in her wrist.

[tender] She was alive. She opened her eyes, and the first things she saw were the sky through the window, and the man who loved her. She blushed.

[warmly] The goddess Venus herself came to their wedding. And nine months later, they had a daughter, and named her Paphos, and a city on Cyprus was named after her.

## close

[softly] And Pygmalion, who had once carved a woman to keep him company, never again had to live alone.
