## story

[warmly] This is a story from the Kalevala, the great collection of old Finnish songs, about a contest where the weapons were words, and the loser was sung into a swamp.

[softly] In the land of Kalevala lived Väinämöinen, the oldest singer in the world. He had been born at the beginning of time, and his songs were magic. When he sang, the world itself listened and obeyed.

[curious] Far to the north lived a young man called Joukahainen. He was proud, and he had learned some songs too. [slyly] He heard about old Väinämöinen, and he was jealous. I know more than that old man, he said. I will beat him. [lightly] His mother and father begged him not to go. He ignored them, harnessed his horse to his sledge, and drove south.

[building tension] On a narrow road, the two sledges crashed into each other, nose to nose. Neither would move aside. [knowingly] Joukahainen started to boast. He knew all sorts of things, he said. That a bird is a bird. That a pike is a fish. And then, boldest of all, he claimed he had helped to make the world itself, piling up the hills and scooping out the seas.

[firmly] Väinämöinen had been there when the world was made, and he knew that was a lie. You are lying, he said. [intense] And Joukahainen, furious, challenged him to a duel. [pause] So the old man began to sing.

## ending

[with wonder] As Väinämöinen sang, the land changed. Joukahainen's sledge turned into dead wood floating in a pool. His horse became a rock by the shore. His bow became a rainbow over the water. [building tension] And Joukahainen himself began to sink, into a cold, sucking swamp. Up to his waist. Up to his chest. Up to his chin.

[intense] Stop, he begged. I will give you my bow. My boat. My horse. Gold and silver. Väinämöinen kept singing. [slowly] Then Joukahainen offered the only thing left. My sister, Aino. She can be your wife.

[softly] The old man stopped. He sang the song backwards, and Joukahainen was lifted, cold and muddy, out of the swamp. He went home crying to his mother.

## close

[tender] But Aino did not want to marry a man as old as the world, and she ran to the sea, and swam out, and became a fish, swimming free.
