## story

[warmly] This is one of the great love stories of the Middle Ages, about the knight Tristan, the Irish princess Iseult, and a drink that was never meant for them.

[softly] Tristan was the nephew of King Mark of Cornwall, in the south-west of Britain, and the bravest knight of his court. [curious] The king needed a wife, and he chose a princess of Ireland, Iseult, famous for her golden hair. He sent Tristan across the sea to fetch her.

[intense] In Ireland, Tristan killed a dragon that was terrorising the land, and as a reward, the king of Ireland agreed to send Iseult to Cornwall to be married. [thoughtful] But Iseult's mother was a wise woman who knew herbs and magic. She wanted her daughter to be happy in her marriage. [hushed] So she made a love potion, in a little flask. Whoever drank it together would love each other for the rest of their lives. She gave it to Iseult's maid, and told her to give it to the princess and King Mark on their wedding night.

[lightly] The ship sailed for Cornwall. [softly] One afternoon it was very hot, and the wind dropped. Tristan and Iseult were thirsty. A young servant found the little flask, thought it was wine, and poured it for them. [pause] They drank it, both of them.

## ending

[tender] At once, they fell in love, a love so deep it frightened them. But Iseult had to marry King Mark. So they loved each other in secret, for years, and were found out again and again. [grave] Tristan was banished, and went to live in Brittany, across the sea, where he married another woman, also called Iseult.

[intense] Years later, Tristan was wounded by a poisoned blade. Only Iseult of Ireland could heal him. He sent a ship to fetch her. If she is on board, he said, raise a white sail. If not, a black one. [darkly] His jealous wife saw the white sail coming, and told him it was black.

[slowly] Tristan turned his face to the wall, and died. Iseult arrived, too late, lay down beside him, and died of grief.

## close

[softly] They were buried side by side, and from his grave a briar rose grew, and reached across to hers, and no one was ever able to cut it back.
