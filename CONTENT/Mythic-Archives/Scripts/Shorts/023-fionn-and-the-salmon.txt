## story

[warmly] This is an Irish story about how a boy called Fionn burned his thumb, and gained all the knowledge in the world.

[with wonder] By the River Boyne, in Ireland, there was a deep pool. Around it grew nine hazel trees, and their nuts held all the wisdom there is. When the nuts dropped into the water, a salmon ate them. [curious] So that salmon, swimming in the dark pool, knew everything. Anyone who ate its flesh would know everything too.

[thoughtful] An old poet called Finnegas lived beside the river. For seven years he tried to catch that salmon. He fished in sunshine and in rain, in summer and in frost. [softly] There was a prophecy, an old saying, that a man named Finn would one day eat the Salmon of Knowledge. And Finnegas hoped it meant him.

[lightly] One day a boy came to Finnegas to learn poetry. He was young and strong and quick, and he had been raised in hiding in the forest. He said his name was Demne. [gently] Finnegas took him in, and the boy cleaned and cooked and helped with the fishing.

[building tension] Then, at last, after seven long years, the old man felt a great weight on his line. He pulled, and pulled, and out of the pool came the Salmon of Knowledge, silver and shining. [serious] He gave it to the boy. Cook this for me, he said. But do not eat any of it. Not one bite.

[softly] So the boy built a fire, and turned the salmon slowly on a spit. As it cooked, a blister rose on its skin. [pause] Without thinking, he pressed it down with his thumb.

## ending

[intense] It was burning hot. He snatched his hand away and put his thumb in his mouth, to cool it. [slowly] And a little taste of the salmon went in with it.

[thoughtful] When he brought the fish to Finnegas, the old man looked at his face, and saw that something in it had changed. It shone. Did you eat any of the salmon? he asked. [gently] No, said the boy. But I burned my thumb on it, and put my thumb in my mouth. [curious] What is your name? asked the poet. Demne, the boy said. But some people call me Fionn.

[tender] Finnegas sat quietly for a moment. Then he said, the prophecy was for you, not for me. Eat the salmon. And Fionn ate it all.

## close

[with a gentle smile] And for the rest of his life, whenever Fionn needed to know something, he only had to put his thumb in his mouth.
