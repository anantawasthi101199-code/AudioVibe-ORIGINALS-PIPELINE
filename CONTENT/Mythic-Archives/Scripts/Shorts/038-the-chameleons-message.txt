## story

[warmly] This is a story of the Zulu people of southern Africa, about why people die, and the slow little creature they blame for it.

[softly] At the beginning of things, the Zulu say, there was the first ancestor, Unkulunkulu, the very old one, who came out of the reeds and made everything else: people, cattle, and the land itself. [thoughtful] When the people had been made, Unkulunkulu had to decide one more thing. Would they live forever, or would they die?

[gently] He chose life. And he called for the chameleon. Go to the people, he said, and tell them this: let people not die.

[lightly] The chameleon set off. But a chameleon is slow. It lifts one foot, and holds it in the air, and wobbles, and slowly puts it down. Then the next foot. [playfully] Along the way, it came to a bush heavy with ripe purple berries. It stopped, and ate some. Then it ate some more. Then it rested, in the warm sun, changing colour on the branch.

[serious] Time passed. The message did not arrive. [darkly] And Unkulunkulu, for reasons of his own, changed his mind. [pause] He called for a lizard, a quick one.

## ending

[building tension] Go to the people, he said, and tell them this: let people die. [intense] The lizard ran. It dashed through the grass and over the rocks, faster than the wind, and it passed the chameleon still eating berries on its bush. [grave] It reached the people first. Let people die, it said. And the people heard it, and accepted it.

[slowly] Much later, the chameleon finally arrived, slowly lifting one foot and then the other. Let people not die, it said, proudly. [softly] The people looked at it. We have already heard the lizard's message, they said. It is too late. [grave] And so, from that day, people die.

## close

[knowingly] And Zulu children used to chase the slow chameleon wherever they found it, because it took too long to bring the message that mattered most.
