## story

[warmly] This is a story from ancient China, about a great flood, and the man who spent thirteen years of his life fighting it.

[grave] Long ago, the rivers rose and kept rising. Water covered the fields and the villages. It swirled around the hills, and climbed toward the mountaintops. People fled up into the trees and caves, and the land became one huge brown sea. [serious] The old emperor asked his ministers who could stop it. They chose a man named Gun.

[thoughtful] Gun's plan was to build walls, dams and banks, to hold the water back. [hushed] To build them faster, he stole something from heaven: a magic soil that swelled and grew by itself, so that one handful became a hill. He piled it up along the rivers.

[grave] For nine years he built his walls, and for nine years the water broke through them and rose higher. The walls only made the flood angrier. [serious] The next ruler, Shun, a wise man the emperor had chosen to rule after him, judged that Gun had failed. And Gun was put to death on a cold mountain, far away.

[with quiet awe] But his body did not rot. For three years it lay there, unchanged. Then someone cut it open, and out came his son, Yu.

[determined] Shun gave the flood to Yu. And Yu did a different thing from his father. [pause] He did not try to hold the water back. He decided to let it go.

## ending

[building] Yu took up a spade and went out with the workers. He dug channels and cut through hills, so the water could run down along its own paths, to the sea. He walked every river and measured every mountain. A great dragon swept its tail across the ground to show him where to dig, and a black turtle carried the mud on its back.

[tender] He worked so hard the hair wore off his legs, and he walked with a limp. Three times in thirteen years, he passed his own front door. Once he heard his newborn son crying inside. [softly] And he did not go in, because the work was not done.

[warmly] At last the water drained away. The fields came back out of the mud, and people came down from the hills. Yu divided the dry land into nine regions, and Shun handed him the throne.

## close

[gently] Yu became one of the most loved rulers China ever had, the man who dug where his father had built walls, and the door he walked past three times was still waiting for him when he came home.
