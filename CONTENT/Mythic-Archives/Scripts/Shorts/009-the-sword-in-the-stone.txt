## story

[warmly] This is the story of how a teenage boy, who did not even know who his real father was, became King Arthur.

[serious] Long ago, Britain was ruled by a king called Uther. When he had a baby son, the wizard Merlin took the child away in secret, and gave him to a good knight to raise as his own. The boy grew up as Arthur, with no idea that he was a prince. [darkly] Then King Uther died, and left no heir that anyone knew of. The great lords began to fight over the crown, and the whole country fell into chaos.

[curious] So Merlin asked the Archbishop to call every lord in the land to London, at Christmas. On Christmas morning, as the people left the great church, they found something in the churchyard that had not been there before. [with wonder] A huge square block of stone, with an iron anvil on top of it. And driven down through the anvil, into the stone, was a sword. Golden words were written on the stone. Whoever pulls this sword out of this stone and anvil is the rightful king of all England.

[building tension] Every lord tried. They heaved until their faces turned red. Not one of them moved it even an inch.

[lightly] A few days later, at New Year, there was a great tournament outside London. Arthur, now about fifteen, went along as a helper to his older brother, Kay. On the way, Kay realised he had left his sword at home. Arthur rode back to fetch it, but the house was locked. [thoughtful] Then he remembered the sword in the churchyard. [pause] He rode over, took hold of the handle, and pulled.

## ending

[with wonder] It slid out as easily as from a sheath. Arthur took it to Kay. Kay recognised it at once, and claimed he had pulled it out himself. [firmly] But his father made him tell the truth. Then Arthur pushed the sword back into the stone, and drew it out again.

[tender] The old knight dropped to his knees before the boy. Arthur begged him to stand. You are my father, he said. And the old man told him, gently, that he was not. [serious] The lords refused to believe it, and made him do it again and again, all through the year. Every time, only Arthur could. At last the ordinary people of the land shouted that they would have Arthur and no one else. And he was crowned king.

## close

[softly] And the first thing the new king did was make Kay, the brother who had tried to claim the sword, a lord of his household.
