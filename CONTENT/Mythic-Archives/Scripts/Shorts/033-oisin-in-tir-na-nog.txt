## story

[warmly] This is the Irish story of Oisín, who rode away to a land where nobody grows old, and of what happened when he came home.

[lightly] Oisín was a warrior and a poet, the son of Fionn, the great leader of the Fianna, a band of hunters and heroes who guarded Ireland. [with wonder] One misty morning, as the Fianna were hunting beside a lake, a young woman rode out of the west on a white horse. Her hair shone like gold. Her dress was bright as the sky.

[softly] Her name was Niamh. She came from Tír na nÓg, the Land of Youth, beyond the sea. And she had come for Oisín. Come with me, she said, and you will never grow old. [tender] Oisín climbed up behind her, and the white horse galloped straight across the waves.

[with wonder] Tír na nÓg was more beautiful than any place he had imagined. Orchards were always in flower and always in fruit. Nobody grew sick. Nobody died. Oisín married Niamh, and they were happy there.

[thoughtful] But after three years, Oisín began to miss Ireland. He wanted to see his father, and his friends, just once more. [softly] Niamh was afraid. But she gave him the white horse to ride home. [serious] Whatever you do, she said, do not get off the horse. If your foot touches the ground of Ireland, you can never come back to me. [pause] Oisín promised.

## ending

[slowly] He rode across the sea, and came to Ireland. But something was wrong. He rode to his father's great hall, and found nothing but grass and ruins. Nobody had heard of Fionn, except in old stories. [grave] And Oisín understood. Three years in the Land of Youth had been three hundred years in Ireland.

[building tension] Riding on, he came to a valley where men were struggling to lift a heavy stone. They looked small and weak to him. Oisín leaned down from the saddle to help, and lifted the stone with one hand. [intense] But as he did, the strap of the saddle broke, and he fell to the ground.

[slowly] The moment he touched it, three hundred years caught up with him. His hair turned white. His skin wrinkled. His eyes went dim. He was an old, blind man, lying in the grass. And the white horse turned, and galloped back over the sea without him.

## close

[softly] Oisín spent his last days telling the stories of Fionn and the Fianna to anyone who would listen, which is how we still know them.
