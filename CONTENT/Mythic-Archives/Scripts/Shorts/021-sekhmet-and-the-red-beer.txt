## story

[warmly] This is the Egyptian story of the day the gods nearly destroyed the human race, and saved it at the last moment with an ocean of red beer.

[softly] At the very beginning, the sun god Ra ruled on earth as a king, over gods and people alike. But he grew old. His bones turned to silver, his flesh to gold, and his hair to blue stone. [darkly] And the people he ruled noticed. They began to whisper. He is weak now. They plotted against him, out in the desert.

[serious] Ra heard about it. He called the gods together in secret, and asked what he should do. Send your Eye against them, they said. [ominous] The Eye of Ra was his daughter, a goddess of terrible power. And as she went out to punish the people, she changed into Sekhmet, a lioness, with burning breath and claws like knives.

[intense] She hunted the plotters into the desert, and killed them. Then she kept killing. She roared through the land, and wherever she went, the ground ran red. [darkly] She loved it. When night fell, she lay down to sleep, and promised herself she would finish the job in the morning, until not one human was left.

[grave] Ra looked down, and felt sorry. He had wanted to punish them, not wipe them out. But nobody could stop Sekhmet once she had tasted blood. Not even him. [pause] So, that same night, he came up with a trick.

## ending

[building tension] He sent his fastest messengers far up the river Nile, to fetch a red clay that grew in the south. He ordered his servants to brew beer, grinding barley through the night, until they had filled seven thousand jars. Then they mixed the red clay into it, until the beer looked exactly like blood.

[hushed] Before dawn, they poured it over the fields where Sekhmet would hunt, until the land was flooded with it, three hands deep.

[slowly] Sekhmet woke and saw it, shining red in the morning light. She thought it was blood. She bent down, and drank, and drank, and drank. [softly] The beer made her happy and sleepy. She wandered home, gentle now, and forgot all about the humans. They were saved.

## close

[warmly] And for centuries afterwards, the Egyptians held a festival in her honour, where everyone drank beer, sang, and remembered how close the world had come to ending.
