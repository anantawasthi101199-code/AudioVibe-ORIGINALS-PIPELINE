## story

[warmly] This is the Japanese story of the day the sun goddess hid herself away, and the whole world went dark, until the other gods found a way to bring her out.

[with wonder] Amaterasu was the goddess of the sun, the brightest of all the gods, who lit up heaven and earth. Her brother was Susanoo, the god of storms, loud, wild and impossible to control.

[darkly] One day, Susanoo came to visit her in heaven, and he would not behave. He smashed the walls between her rice fields and filled in the water channels. He made a filthy mess in the hall where she held her sacred feasts. [intense] Then he went too far. He tore a hole in the roof of her weaving hall, where her maidens wove clothes for the gods, and dropped a dead horse through it. One of the weavers was so terrified that she hurt herself and died.

[softly] Amaterasu could bear no more. She ran to a cave in the mountains of heaven, went inside, and rolled a great rock across the entrance. [hushed] And the sun was gone. Darkness fell over heaven and earth. Nothing grew. Evil spirits came out and swarmed through the endless night.

[thoughtful] Eight hundred gods gathered beside a river in the dark, to work out what to do. The wisest of them came up with a plan. [lightly] They brought roosters, the birds that crow at dawn, and set them crowing outside the cave. They hung a great mirror and strings of jewels on a tree in front of the entrance. [pause] Then a goddess called Uzume climbed up onto an upturned tub, and began to dance.

## ending

[playfully] She stamped and spun, faster and faster, until her clothes slipped off her shoulders, and all eight hundred gods roared with delight. [curious] Inside the cave, Amaterasu heard the noise. Why are they so happy in the dark? She pushed the rock a tiny bit to one side, and asked.

[slyly] Uzume called back. We are celebrating, because a goddess even brighter than you has come. [with wonder] Amaterasu peeped out, and saw a dazzling light. It was her own reflection, shining in the mirror, but she did not know that. She leaned out to look closer. [intense] And the strongest of the gods, waiting beside the rock, grabbed her hand and pulled her out into the open.

[warmly] At once the light came back to the world. Another god quickly stretched a rope of straw across the cave mouth, so she could never hide in there again.

## close

[softly] And to this day, at shrines across Japan, ropes of twisted straw still hang across sacred doorways.
