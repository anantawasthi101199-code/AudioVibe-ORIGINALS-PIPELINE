## story

[warmly] This is the most famous story from the Shahnameh, the great Persian Book of Kings, about the hero Rostam, and the son he never knew.

[with wonder] Rostam was the greatest warrior in Persia, which is now Iran. Huge and strong, he wore a tiger skin into battle and rode a giant horse called Rakhsh. [curious] One day, hunting near the border, he fell asleep, and Rakhsh was stolen. Rostam followed the tracks to a city called Samangan.

[tender] There, that night, the king's daughter, Tahmineh, came to him. She had heard of his deeds, and she loved him. They were married. [softly] But Rostam could not stay. Before he left, he gave her an armband with a jewel set in it. If we have a son, he said, tie this around his arm, so I will know him.

[thoughtful] They had a son, Sohrab. He grew up incredibly fast and strong, a warrior by the age of ten. When he learned that his father was the great Rostam, he made a bold plan. [determined] He would lead an army into Persia, defeat its king, and put his father on the throne. [grave] So Sohrab joined the enemy army, and marched against Persia.

[serious] The Persian king sent his greatest champion to stop the young warrior. Rostam. [building tension] But Rostam wore no badge and gave no name. Sohrab looked at him and asked, are you Rostam? [pause] And Rostam, not wanting the enemy to know he was there, said no.

## ending

[intense] They fought for three days. On the second day, Sohrab threw Rostam down and sat on his chest, ready to kill him. [slyly] Rostam told him that, by custom, a warrior is never killed the first time he falls. Sohrab believed him, and let him go.

[grave] On the third day, Rostam threw Sohrab to the ground, and drove his dagger into him. [slowly] As he lay dying, Sohrab said, my father Rostam will avenge me. [softly] Rostam tore open the boy's armour, and saw the armband with the jewel.

[tender] He held his son, weeping, and sent riders to beg the king for a magic medicine that could heal any wound. The king delayed. By the time the riders returned, Sohrab was dead.

## close

[softly] Rostam laid his son to rest, and took the armband home to Tahmineh, the only thing either of them had left of him.
