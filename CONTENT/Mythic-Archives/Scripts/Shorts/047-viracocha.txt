## story

[warmly] This is the story the Inca people of the Andes told about Viracocha, the creator of everything, and how he left the world he made.

[hushed] In the beginning, the world was dark. There was no sun, no moon, no stars. [with wonder] Out of the waters of Lake Titicaca, a vast blue lake high in the mountains of South America, rose Viracocha, the creator. He was tall, and pale, and wise.

[thoughtful] First, he made a race of giants out of great blocks of stone, and gave them life in the darkness. [darkly] But the giants would not obey him. They fought, and broke his rules. [intense] So Viracocha sent a great flood that drowned the world, and turned the giants back into stone. Some of them still stand in the high mountains, people said, frozen where they fell.

[with wonder] Then he began again. At the shores of the lake, he called out the sun, and it rose from an island, shining over the world for the first time. Then the moon, and the stars. [gently] Next he made new people out of clay. He painted each group in different clothes, gave each its own language and its own songs, and sent them down into the earth. Then he called them out again, from caves, rivers and hills, all over the land, to be the first people of every nation.

[determined] Viracocha walked among them, disguised as an old man with a staff, teaching them how to live. [serious] But in one village, the people did not recognise him. They threw stones at him, and drove him away. [pause] So he looked up, and called down fire from the sky.

## ending

[intense] Flames poured down onto a hill beside the village, burning the rocks themselves. [softly] The people fell to their knees, terrified, and begged him to stop. Viracocha raised his staff, and the fire went out. The burnt stones lay there, black and light, for people to see for centuries.

[slowly] He walked on, all the way to the edge of the land, where the great ocean began. [reverently] There he said goodbye to the people who had followed him. Then he stepped onto the sea, and walked away across the waves, westwards, and disappeared.

## close

[softly] And the Incas waited, for a long time, for their creator to come walking back across the water.
