## story

[warmly] This is the story of a king of Egypt, the brother who wanted his throne, and the queen who would search the whole world to bring him back.

[softly] The king was Osiris. He was a god who ruled over Egypt, back when gods still walked the land, and he was loved. He taught people to plant wheat, to grow grapes, to live by laws. His wife was Isis, the cleverest of the gods, wise in magic.

[darkly] But Osiris had a brother called Set, god of storms and the red desert. Set looked at his brother's crown, and his brother's love, and he wanted all of it.

[slyly] So Set made a plan. He secretly measured Osiris's body. Then he had a chest built to those exact measurements, a beautiful thing of polished wood, painted and inlaid with gold. And he threw a great feast.

[building tension] At the height of the feast, Set's servants carried in the chest. Every guest gasped at it. With a smile, Set made an offer. Whoever fits inside, he said, can keep it. One by one, the guests climbed in. Too tall. Too short. Too wide. [pause] Then Osiris lay down in it. And he fitted perfectly.

[intense] Before he could sit up, Set and his friends slammed down the lid. They hammered in nails. They poured hot melted lead around the edges so no air could get in. [slowly] Then they carried the chest to the River Nile, and threw it in.

## ending

[hushed] The river carried the chest out to sea, all the way to a far city on another shore, where a tree grew up around it and swallowed it whole. Isis searched every land until she found that tree, cut the chest free, and brought her husband home. [darkly] But Set, out hunting by moonlight, found the body. This time he cut it into fourteen pieces and scattered them across Egypt.

[tender] So Isis searched again. She sailed through the marshes in a little boat made of reeds, and gathered every piece she could find. With her magic, she joined them together and breathed life back into Osiris, just long enough for them to have a son. [reverently] Then Osiris went down to rule the land of the dead, a kind king for every soul who would ever arrive there.

## close

[softly] And every year, when the Nile rose and turned the fields green again, people said it was Osiris, coming back to them.
