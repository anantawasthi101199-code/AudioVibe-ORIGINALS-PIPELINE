## story

[warmly] This is the Egyptian story of what happens after you die, told through one man, a scribe called Ani, whose journey was painted on a scroll and buried with him.

[softly] Ani was a royal scribe, a man who wrote letters and records for the temple. When he died, his body was carefully wrapped and laid in a tomb. [hushed] But Ani himself, his spirit, set off on a long journey through the Duat, the dark land beneath the world. He passed through gates guarded by fierce spirits, and at each one, he spoke the right words, so he could pass.

[with wonder] At last he came to a great hall, the Hall of Two Truths. At the far end sat Osiris, the king of the dead, on his throne. Along the walls sat forty-two judges.

[thoughtful] A god with the head of a jackal, Anubis, took Ani gently by the hand and led him forward. In the middle of the hall stood a tall pair of scales. [serious] Ani had to speak to each of the judges, and swear to them how he had lived. I have done no harm to anyone, he said. I have stolen nothing. I have told no lies.

[ominous] Then Anubis reached into Ani, and took out his heart, because the Egyptians believed the heart remembered everything a person had ever done. He placed it on one side of the scale. On the other side, he placed a single white feather, the feather of truth.

[darkly] Beside the scale crouched a monster called the Devourer. She had the head of a crocodile, the body of a lion and the back legs of a hippopotamus. [pause] If Ani's heart was heavier than the feather, she would eat it.

## ending

[building tension] The scale wobbled. The god of writing, with the head of an ibis bird, stood ready with his reed pen to write down the result. [softly] Ani spoke to his own heart, the way the scroll told him to. Do not stand against me, he whispered. Do not speak against me here.

[slowly] The scale settled. The heart and the feather hung exactly level. [warmly] The god of writing wrote it down: Ani is true of voice. The Devourer turned away, still hungry.

[with wonder] The falcon god, Horus, led Ani to the throne of Osiris. And from there, Ani walked out into the Field of Reeds, a perfect Egypt where the wheat grew taller than a man, and he would live and farm in peace forever.

## close

[softly] And when Egyptians were buried, a carved stone beetle was laid over their heart, with those same words written on it, asking the heart to stay quiet on the scale.
