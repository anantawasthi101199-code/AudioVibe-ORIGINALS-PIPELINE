## story

[warmly] This is a Russian story about a girl sent into the forest to fetch fire from a witch who eats people, with nothing to help her but a tiny doll in her pocket.

[tender] The girl was Vasilisa. When she was eight years old, her mother fell ill. Before she died, she gave Vasilisa a little wooden doll. Keep her with you always, her mother whispered. Feed her, and when you are in trouble, ask her for help.

[darkly] Her father married again, to a woman with two daughters of her own, and all three of them were cruel to Vasilisa. They made her do every job, hoping she would grow thin and ugly. [softly] But every night, Vasilisa gave the doll a little of her food. And the doll helped her with the work.

[ominous] Then her father went away on business. One night the stepmother put out every candle in the house, and sent Vasilisa into the dark forest to fetch fire from Baba Yaga. Everyone knew who Baba Yaga was. A witch, bony and old, who ate people.

[hushed] Vasilisa walked all night. A rider dressed in white galloped past on a white horse, and the dawn came. A rider in red went by, and the sun rose. In the evening a rider in black passed, and it was night again. [building tension] Then she saw the hut. It stood on giant chicken legs. Around it was a fence made of human bones, and on every post sat a skull with eyes that glowed in the dark.

[intense] With a roar, Baba Yaga arrived, flying through the trees in a great stone bowl, steering with a stick and sweeping away her tracks with a broom. [slowly] You may have fire, she said. But first you will work for me. And if you fail, I will eat you.

## ending

[building tension] Baba Yaga set impossible tasks. Sweep the house. Cook her a feast. Sort the bad grain from a whole sack of good. [tender] Each night, Vasilisa fed her doll, and each morning the work was done. At last the witch asked how. My mother's blessing, said Vasilisa. [darkly] Baba Yaga hated blessings. She pushed the girl out of the door, and gave her a skull on a stick, its eyes burning like coals.

[ominous] Vasilisa carried it home. The moment her stepmother and stepsisters stepped near, the skull's blazing eyes stared at them, and kept staring, and by morning nothing was left of them but ash.

## close

[softly] Vasilisa buried the skull in the garden, and kept the little doll in her pocket for the rest of her life.
