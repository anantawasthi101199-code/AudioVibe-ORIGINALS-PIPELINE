## story

[warmly] This is a story from the Ashanti people of Ghana, in West Africa, about how a small, clever spider called Anansi came to own all the stories in the world.

[curious] Long ago, every story belonged to Nyame, the god of the sky. He kept them in a box, and nobody on earth could tell them. [mischievously] Anansi wanted them. So he spun a long thread all the way up to the sky, climbed it, and asked Nyame what it would cost to buy them.

[knowingly] The sky god smiled. Great kings had tried to buy his stories and failed. The price, he said, is four things. Onini the python, who swallows men whole. Osebo the leopard, with teeth like spears. The Mmoboro hornets, who sting like fire. And Mmoatia, the fairy whom no human has ever seen. [lightly] Anansi bowed, and said he would bring them.

[slyly] First, the python. Anansi cut a long palm branch, and walked past Onini, arguing loudly with himself. My wife says the python is longer than this branch, he said. I say she is wrong. [playfully] The python wanted to prove him wrong. So he stretched out along the branch, and while he was stretched out, Anansi tied him to it, tight.

[mischievously] Next, the hornets. Anansi filled a gourd with water and poured some over their nest, and some over himself. It is raining, he called. Quick, shelter in my gourd. The hornets flew in, and Anansi plugged it shut.

[building tension] For the leopard, Anansi dug a deep pit on its path and covered it with branches. Osebo fell in. Anansi lowered a sticky rope, pretending to help him out, and tied him up.

## ending

[slyly] Last was the fairy. Anansi carved a wooden doll, covered it with sticky gum from a tree, and put a bowl of mashed yam in its hands. Mmoatia came, ate the yam, and thanked the doll. [lightly] The doll said nothing. Mmoatia was offended and slapped it, and her hand stuck. She slapped it with the other hand, and that stuck too. Anansi tied her up.

[proudly] Then he carried all four of them up to the sky. Nyame called together his whole court. [warmly] He announced that kings had failed where a spider had succeeded, and from that day, every story would belong to Anansi.

## close

[with a gentle smile] And that is why, in West Africa, people still call their stories Anansi stories, even this one.
