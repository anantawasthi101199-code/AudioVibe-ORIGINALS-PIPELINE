## story

[warmly] This is the Greek story of Phaethon, a boy who wanted to prove who his father was, and borrowed the sun to do it.

[softly] Phaethon's mother had told him that his father was the god of the sun, who drives a blazing chariot across the sky every day. [serious] But one day, a friend laughed at him. You? The son of the sun god? You are making it up. [determined] Phaethon was furious and ashamed. So he decided to go and ask his father himself.

[with wonder] He travelled east, to the edge of the world, to the palace of the sun. Its columns were gold. Its doors were silver. And on a throne of emeralds sat the sun god, so bright that Phaethon had to stop at the door and shade his eyes.

[tender] The god took off his crown of rays so his son could come near. Yes, he said, you are my son. And to prove it, I will give you anything you ask. I swear it by the river of the underworld, an oath even the gods can never break.

[slowly] Phaethon did not hesitate. Let me drive your chariot across the sky, he said, for one day.

[grave] The sun god's face fell. Anything but that, he begged. My horses breathe fire. Even the king of the gods could not hold them. Ask for something else. [determined] But Phaethon would not change his mind. [pause] And an oath is an oath.

## ending

[building tension] At dawn, the gates opened. The four fiery horses leapt into the sky. At once they felt the chariot was too light, the driver too weak. They bolted. [intense] Phaethon dropped the reins. The chariot swung too high, and the earth froze. Then it plunged too low, and the world began to burn. Forests burst into flames. Rivers boiled away. Deserts spread across the land. The great river Nile fled and hid its head, which is why its source was hidden for so long.

[grave] The Earth cried out to the king of the gods for help. He raised a lightning bolt, and threw it. [slowly] Phaethon was struck from the chariot, and fell, blazing like a shooting star, down into a river far below.

## close

[tender] His sisters stood weeping on the riverbank until they turned into poplar trees, and their tears, dropping into the water, hardened into amber.
