## story

[warmly] This is a West African story about Anansi the spider, who tried to keep all the wisdom in the world to himself, and the small boy who taught him a lesson.

[curious] Anansi was clever. Everybody knew it, and Anansi knew it best of all. But he wanted more. He wanted to be the cleverest creature that had ever lived, so that everyone would have to come to him for answers.

[mischievously] So he went around the whole world, collecting every bit of wisdom he could find. He took it from the elders and the hunters. He took it from the farmers and the mothers. And he poured it all into one big clay pot. [with wonder] When he lifted the lid and peered inside, the pot glowed with everything there was to know.

[slyly] Now Anansi was worried. Someone might steal it. He needed to hide it somewhere nobody could ever reach. [thoughtful] And he knew just the place: the top of the tallest tree in the forest, a giant silk-cotton tree whose branches touched the clouds.

[lightly] He tied the heavy pot to a rope, and hung it around his neck, so it sat on his round belly in front of him. Then he started to climb. [playfully] But the pot was in the way. Every time he reached for the trunk, it bumped against the bark and pushed him off. He slid down. He tried again. He scraped his knees and tangled his legs. [wry] He could not climb more than a few feet.

[softly] What he did not know was that his young son, Ntikuma, had followed him from home. The boy was standing at the bottom of the tree, watching all of this. [pause] And at last, he could not stay quiet any longer.

## ending

[gently] Father, he called up. Why don't you tie the pot on your back? Then you could hold on to the tree.

[slowly] Anansi stopped. He hung there, on the side of the trunk, and thought about it. His son was right. [darkly] And then he realised what that meant. He had all the wisdom in the world in a pot around his neck, and a small boy still knew something he did not.

[intense] In a fury, Anansi tore off the pot and flung it to the ground. It shattered against the roots. [with wonder] The wisdom spilled out, and a storm came, and the rain washed it into the river, and the river carried it out across the whole world, a little piece for everyone.

## close

[with a gentle smile] And that is why nobody has all the wisdom, and why everybody has a little.
