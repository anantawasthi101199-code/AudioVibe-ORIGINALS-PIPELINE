## story

[warmly] This is the first story in the Mabinogion, a book of old Welsh tales, about Prince Pwyll, and the year he spent as somebody else.

[lightly] Pwyll was the prince of Dyfed, in south-west Wales. One day he went hunting in the forest, and his hounds chased a stag. [with wonder] But then he saw another pack of hounds bring the stag down first. They were dazzling white, with bright red ears, unlike any dogs he had ever seen. [slyly] Pwyll drove them away, and set his own dogs on the stag instead.

[serious] A rider in grey appeared from the trees, on a tall grey horse. You have insulted me, he said. Those were my hounds. [hushed] I am Arawn, king of Annwn. Annwn was the otherworld, a land of magic and the dead.

[thoughtful] Pwyll apologised, and asked how he could make it right. [knowingly] Arawn had an answer. There is a king who keeps attacking my land, called Hafgan. In a year's time, I must fight him. Take my place. I will give you my face and my body, and you will rule my kingdom for a year, and I will rule yours. [serious] And when you fight Hafgan, strike him only once. If you strike him a second time, he will come back to life.

[with wonder] So it was done. Pwyll rode into Annwn, wearing Arawn's face. Nobody there knew the difference. Not the courtiers. Not even Arawn's beautiful queen. [pause] And every night, for a whole year, he went to bed beside her.

## ending

[tender] But every night, he turned his face to the wall, and did not touch her, because she was another man's wife. He was a loyal friend, even when nobody would ever know.

[building tension] At the end of the year, Pwyll met Hafgan at a river ford. He struck him once, so hard that Hafgan fell. [intense] Finish me, groaned Hafgan. Strike again. [firmly] Pwyll refused. And Hafgan died, and Arawn's kingdom was safe.

[softly] Arawn and Pwyll met again in the forest, and swapped back. That night, Arawn's queen was astonished, because after a whole year of silence, her husband was warm and loving again. [warmly] When Arawn heard why, he knew he had found the truest friend a king could have.

## close

[warmly] And from then on, the two kings were friends for life, and Pwyll was known as the Lord of Annwn.
