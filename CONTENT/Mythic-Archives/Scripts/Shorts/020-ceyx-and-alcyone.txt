## story

[warmly] This is a Greek story about a husband and wife who loved each other so much that even death could not keep them apart, and how they became birds.

[tender] Ceyx was a kind king who ruled a city in Greece. His wife was Alcyone, and they were devoted to each other. Her father was the keeper of the winds, so she knew better than anyone how cruel the sea could be in a storm.

[serious] One day, Ceyx decided he had to sail across the sea, to ask the gods for advice at a distant temple. [softly] Alcyone begged him not to go. I have seen the winds when they break loose, she said. If you must go, take me with you. But he would not put her in danger. He promised he would be home within two months. [slowly] She stood on the shore and watched his ship until its sails disappeared.

[building tension] That night a storm came. The wind howled. Waves as tall as mountains crashed over the deck. The mast snapped. [intense] The ship broke apart, and Ceyx was thrown into the black water. As he struggled, he said only one name, over and over. Alcyone. [hushed] Then the waves closed over him.

[tender] At home, Alcyone did not know. Every day she prayed for his safe return, and wove a new robe for him to wear when he came home. [thoughtful] The queen of the gods, hearing her pray for a man who was already dead, felt sorry for her. [pause] So she sent a messenger to the god of sleep.

## ending

[hushed] The god of sleep lived in a dark cave where no sun ever reached, surrounded by poppies. He sent one of his sons, a shape-changing god of dreams, to Alcyone's bed. [softly] He took the form of Ceyx, pale and dripping with seawater. Do not pray for me any more, he said. I am dead.

[slowly] Alcyone woke, and ran to the shore where she had last seen him. And there, floating in on the waves, was a body. [grave] It was Ceyx. She climbed onto the stone wall above the water, and leapt.

[with wonder] But she did not fall. She flew. Her arms had become wings, and she skimmed across the waves to him, crying out. [tender] The gods turned Ceyx into a bird too, and the two of them flew away together, as kingfishers.

## close

[softly] And every winter, for seven calm days while she sits on her nest, her father holds back the winds, and the sea lies still.
