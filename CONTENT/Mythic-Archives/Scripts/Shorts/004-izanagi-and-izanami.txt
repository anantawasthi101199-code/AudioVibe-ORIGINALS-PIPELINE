## story

[warmly] This is the story of two gods who made the islands of Japan together, and how one of them followed the other into the land of the dead.

[with wonder] In the beginning, the world below the heavens was only ocean, soft and shapeless, like oil floating on water. Two young gods were sent down to make something of it. He was Izanagi, which means he who invites. She was Izanami, she who invites. [softly] They stood together on a floating bridge in the sky and dipped a long jewelled spear into the sea below. They stirred it, round and round, and drew the spear back up. Salty drops fell from its tip, and where they landed, they piled up into the first island.

[tender] The two gods stepped down onto it and married there. Then Izanami gave birth to the islands of Japan, one after another, and to the gods of the wind, the trees, the mountains and the sea.

[grave] Her last child was the god of fire. As he was born, he burned her terribly, and Izanami died. [slowly] Izanagi wept over her. He could not bear to live without her. So he went down into Yomi, the dark land of the dead, to bring her home.

[hushed] He found her in the shadows at a closed door. She would not come out. I have already eaten the food of this place, she said. But I will ask the gods of the dead to let me go. Wait. [pause] And do not look at me.

[building tension] He waited. And waited. At last he could bear it no longer. He broke a tooth off his comb, lit it like a torch, and looked.

## ending

[darkly] Izanami was rotting. Her body was crawling with maggots, and little thunder gods writhed all over it. Izanagi turned and ran. [intense] And Izanami, shamed and furious, sent the hideous hags of the underworld screaming after him. He threw down his headdress, and it became grapes that they stopped to eat. He threw his comb, and it became bamboo shoots. At the very edge of the land of the dead, he grabbed a huge boulder and rolled it across the passage, sealing it shut.

[slowly] Through the rock, Izanami cried that she would kill a thousand of his people every day. [firmly] And Izanagi answered that he would make fifteen hundred more be born.

## close

[softly] And so, every day since, a few more people are born into the world than leave it.
