## story

[warmly] This is the Chinese story of two lovers who live on opposite sides of the sky, and the one night of the year when they can meet.

[with wonder] In heaven lived a young goddess, the Weaver Girl. Her mother was the Queen Mother of heaven, and the Weaver Girl's work was the most beautiful in the sky. Every day, at her loom, she wove the clouds. Soft white clouds, glowing sunset clouds, the coloured clouds of dawn.

[softly] Down on earth lived a poor young cowherd. His parents had died, and his family had given him nothing but an old ox. [tender] He looked after it well, and talked to it, because it was his only friend. And the old ox, who had once been a god, could talk back.

[curious] One day, the ox told him that heavenly maidens came down to bathe in a lake nearby. The cowherd went to see. [gently] There he met the Weaver Girl, who had come down from heaven for a day. They talked by the water until the sun went down, and fell in love. She chose to stay on earth, and marry him.

[warmly] They were very happy. They had two children, a boy and a girl. [serious] But up in heaven, no one was weaving the clouds anymore. The sky grew grey and dull. [ominous] And the Queen Mother found out where her daughter had gone. [pause] She came down, took the Weaver Girl, and carried her back up to the sky.

## ending

[intense] The cowherd was desperate. His old ox, dying, told him to take its hide when it was gone, and wear it, and he would be able to fly. [determined] So he put his two children in baskets, hung them from a pole across his shoulders, and flew up into the sky after her.

[building tension] He was getting close. He could see her. [darkly] Then the Queen Mother took a hairpin from her hair, and drew a line across the sky between them. It became a wide river of silver stars, too wide to cross. We call it the Milky Way.

[tender] The lovers stood on either side, weeping. All the magpies in the world felt so sorry for them that they flew up into the sky together, and made a bridge with their wings. [softly] And the Queen Mother allowed it, but only once a year, on the seventh night of the seventh month.

## close

[softly] And if it rains on that night in China, people say it is the Weaver Girl, crying because the night is over.
