## story

[warmly] This is the Egyptian story of the battle that was fought every single night, so that the sun could rise in the morning.

[softly] The Egyptians believed the sun was the god Ra, sailing across the sky in a golden boat. In the evening, his boat sank below the western horizon. [hushed] And then it sailed on through the Duat, the underworld, a dark land beneath the earth, through twelve hours of night, to rise again in the east at dawn.

[ominous] But in the darkness, someone was waiting. Apep. A serpent so enormous that his coils stretched further than anyone could see. He was the enemy of the light, the spirit of chaos, older than the gods themselves. [darkly] Every night, he wanted only one thing. To swallow the sun, and leave the world in darkness forever.

[building tension] Apep hid in the river of the underworld. Sometimes he drank up its water, so the sun boat ran aground on the sandbanks. Sometimes he rose roaring out of the dark, and tried to wrap his coils around the boat. [hushed] And his eyes could put a spell on whoever looked into them, freezing them where they stood.

[determined] So Ra did not travel alone. A crew of gods sailed with him, armed and ready. Isis, the great magician, sang spells to weaken the serpent. Another huge serpent, a friendly one, curled itself around Ra to protect him. [pause] And at the front of the boat stood the strongest of them all, holding a long spear.

## ending

[intense] It was Set, the god of storms. Set feared nothing, and could not be frozen by Apep's stare. When the serpent rose up out of the river, Set drove his spear deep into him, again and again. [slowly] Apep shrieked, and sank back, wounded, into the dark water. The boat sailed on. And at the end of the twelfth hour, the sun rose over Egypt.

[serious] But Apep could never truly be killed. Every night he came back, and the battle started again. [hushed] When a storm blackened the sky, or the sun went dark in an eclipse, the Egyptians feared that Apep was winning.

[thoughtful] So the priests helped in the only way they could. In the temples, they wrote Apep's name on papyrus, or made a little figure of him from wax, and then they spat on it, stamped on it, cut it, and burned it in the fire.

## close

[softly] And so every sunrise in Egypt was a small victory, won again in the dark.
