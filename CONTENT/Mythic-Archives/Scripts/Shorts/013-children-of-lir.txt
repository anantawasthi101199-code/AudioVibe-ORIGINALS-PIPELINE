## story

[warmly] This is an Irish story about four children who were turned into swans, and the nine hundred years they waited to be human again.

[tender] Long ago, in the time when the old gods of Ireland still lived there, there was a lord of the sea called Lir. He had four children whom he loved more than anything. A daughter, Fionnuala, and three sons. When their mother died, Lir married her sister, Aoife, hoping she would love them too.

[darkly] At first, she did. But she saw how much Lir adored his children, and how little time he had left over for her, and slowly jealousy poisoned her heart.

[ominous] One summer's day, Aoife took the children in her chariot to a lake called Derravaragh. She told them to go into the water and swim. And as they splashed in the shallows, she raised a druid's wand, a staff of magic, and struck each of them. [hushed] Their arms became wings. Their bodies were covered in white feathers. In a moment, four swans floated on the lake.

[softly] Fionnuala turned to her stepmother and asked, how long must we stay like this? [slowly] Aoife answered. Three hundred years on this lake. Three hundred years on the stormy sea between Ireland and Scotland. And three hundred years on the wild western ocean. Until you hear the sound of a holy bell, ringing out across Ireland.

[tender] But even she could not take away everything. The swans kept their human voices, and they could sing more beautifully than anyone on earth. [pause] When Lir found out what she had done, his grief was terrible.

## ending

[firmly] Aoife was punished, and turned into a demon of the air, howling forever on the wind. Lir came to the lake every day to listen to his children sing. [grave] But the years passed. Lir died. Their old home fell into ruins. And the swans moved on to the freezing sea, where storms scattered them and their feathers froze to the rocks, and then to the western ocean.

[with wonder] At last, after nine hundred years, they heard it. A bell, ringing from a little stone church on the shore. A holy man, a monk, came down to the water. [softly] As he blessed them, their feathers fell away. But they did not become children again. They became four very old people, white-haired and frail. Before they died, Fionnuala asked to be buried together, with her brothers held close.

## close

[tender] And so they were laid in one grave, with Fionnuala's arms around her brothers, just as she had sheltered them under her wings.
