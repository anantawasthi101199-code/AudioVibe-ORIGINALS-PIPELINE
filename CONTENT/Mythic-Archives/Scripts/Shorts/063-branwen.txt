## story

[warmly] This is a Welsh story from the Mabinogion, the great collection of old Welsh tales, about Branwen, a princess, and the war that began because of how she was treated.

[with wonder] Branwen's brother was Brân, the king of Britain. And Brân was a giant, so huge that no house had ever been built that could hold him. [softly] One day, the King of Ireland sailed over to ask for Branwen's hand in marriage. Brân agreed, and there was a great wedding feast.

[darkly] But Branwen had another brother, a half-brother called Efnysien, who loved to cause trouble. He was furious that nobody had asked him about the marriage. So, in the night, he crept to the Irish king's horses, and cut and maimed them, so they could never be ridden again.

[serious] It was a terrible insult. To make peace, Brân gave the Irish king new horses, gold, and a magic cauldron. If a dead warrior was put into it, he would come out alive again, though he could never speak.

[thoughtful] Branwen sailed to Ireland as queen, and soon had a baby son. [grave] But the Irish never forgot the insult to their horses. They turned against her. She was sent to work in the palace kitchen, and every day the butcher struck her across the face.

[tender] For three years, Branwen suffered. [pause] And then she found a way to send for help.

## ending

[softly] She tamed a starling, a small speckled bird that came to her kitchen window. She taught it to understand her, and to know her brother's face. Then she tied a letter under its wing, and it flew across the sea to Wales, and landed on Brân's shoulder.

[intense] Brân raised an army. The ships sailed, and the giant king waded through the sea beside them. When they reached a river with no bridge, Brân lay down across it, and his men walked over him. Whoever leads, he said, must be a bridge.

[grave] There was a terrible war. The Irish put their dead warriors into the magic cauldron, and they rose up again. So Efnysien, the brother who had started it all, hid among the dead, was thrown into the cauldron, and stretched himself until it burst, and his heart burst with it. In the end, only seven men of Britain were left alive, and Brân was wounded with a poisoned spear.

[tender] The survivors brought Branwen home to Wales. She looked back across the sea at Ireland, and then at Britain. Two good islands have been destroyed because of me, she said. And her heart broke, and she died.

## close

[softly] They buried her on the bank of a river in Anglesey, under a square grave, looking out to sea.
