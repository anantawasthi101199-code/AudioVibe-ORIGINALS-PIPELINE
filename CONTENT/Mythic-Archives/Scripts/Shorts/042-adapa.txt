## story

[warmly] This is a story from ancient Babylon, about a wise man who stood in heaven, was offered everlasting life, and said no.

[softly] Adapa was a priest in Eridu, one of the oldest cities in the world, in the land that is now Iraq. He served Ea, the god of wisdom and of fresh water, who had made him the wisest man alive. [lightly] One of Adapa's jobs was to catch fish for Ea's temple. So every day he took his little boat out onto the wide, calm sea.

[building tension] One morning, the South Wind blew in, fast and fierce. Waves rose. The boat tipped over, and Adapa was thrown into the water. [intense] Furious, he cursed the wind. And his curse was so powerful that it broke the South Wind's wing.

[serious] For seven days, the South Wind did not blow. Up in heaven, Anu, the great god of the sky, noticed. Why has the South Wind stopped? he asked. [grave] When he found out, he was angry. Bring this man to me, he ordered.

[thoughtful] Before Adapa left, his own god, Ea, gave him careful advice. Dress in mourning clothes, he said. At heaven's gate you will meet two gods. Tell them you are mourning two gods who have vanished from the earth. They will be pleased, and speak up for you. [hushed] Then Anu will offer you bread and water. Do not eat or drink. It is the bread of death and the water of death. [pause] But if he offers clothes and oil, take them.

## ending

[softly] Adapa did everything as he had been told. The two gods at the gate were touched, and spoke kindly of him. Anu's anger cooled. [with wonder] And then Anu did something unexpected. Bring him the bread of life, he said, and the water of life.

[slowly] They were set before Adapa. But he remembered Ea's words. He would not eat. He would not drink. He accepted only the robe, and the oil for his skin.

[curious] Anu stared at him, and then he laughed. Why did you not eat? he asked. Why did you not drink? [grave] Now you will not live forever. [softly] And he sent Adapa back down to earth, to live and die like every other person.

## close

[thoughtful] Adapa went home to Eridu, the wisest man in the world, who had been offered forever and had politely turned it down.
