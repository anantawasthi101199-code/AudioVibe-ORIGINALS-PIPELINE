## story

[warmly] This is the Chinese story of the very first being, a giant who grew inside an egg, and who held the sky and the earth apart until they could stand on their own.

[hushed] At first, there was nothing but darkness, all of it mixed together, swirling inside something shaped like an egg. No up. No down. No light. [softly] And inside that egg, something was growing. A giant called Pangu. He slept there, curled up, for eighteen thousand years.

[building tension] Then he woke. He stretched. And the egg cracked open.

[with wonder] Everything inside began to separate. The light, clear parts drifted upwards and became the sky. The heavy, cloudy parts sank down and became the earth. [serious] But they wanted to fall back together, to become one dark mass again.

[determined] So Pangu stood up between them. He pressed his hands against the sky and planted his feet on the earth, and he pushed. [slowly] And every day, he grew ten feet taller. Every day the sky rose ten feet higher, and the earth grew ten feet thicker. For eighteen thousand years Pangu stood there, holding them apart, growing and pushing, never resting.

[thoughtful] At last, the sky was high and the earth was deep and firm. They stayed where they were. [pause] And Pangu, exhausted, lay down to rest.

## ending

[softly] He never got up again. But as he died, the giant's body became the world.

[with wonder] His breath became the wind and the clouds. His voice became the rolling thunder. His left eye became the sun, and his right eye became the moon. His blood flowed out to become the rivers, and his muscles became the fields. [gently] His hair and beard became the stars and the plants. His bones and teeth became metal and stone, his sweat became the rain that falls on the land, and his arms and legs became the mountains, standing at the four corners of the earth.

[lightly] And the tiny creatures that had been living on his body, crawling about on his skin, were blown by the new wind across the land. They became the first people.

## close

[softly] So whenever it rains, or the thunder rolls, it is a little of Pangu, still looking after the world he held up.
