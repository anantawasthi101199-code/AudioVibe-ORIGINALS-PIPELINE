## story

[warmly] This is a Greek story about a king who cut down a sacred tree, and was cursed with a hunger so great that nothing in the world could fill it.

[curious] The king was Erysichthon. He laughed at the gods and never prayed to them. And near his land stood a grove that belonged to Ceres, the goddess of the harvest, who made the wheat grow. [with wonder] In the middle of it was an enormous old oak, taller than all the trees around it. People had hung garlands on its branches, and little notes of thanks for prayers that had been answered. On summer evenings the spirits of the trees, the nymphs, danced in a ring around its trunk.

[darkly] Erysichthon wanted the wood. He ordered his servants to cut the oak down. They stood still, afraid to touch it. So he snatched an axe himself. One man tried to stop him, and the king struck him down. [intense] Then he swung at the tree. It shuddered. Its leaves turned pale. And where the axe bit into the bark, blood poured out, red as if from a wound.

[hushed] Inside the tree lived a nymph, and as the great oak crashed to the ground, her dying voice cursed him. The goddess will punish you, she said.

[ominous] She did. Ceres sent a messenger to the frozen edge of the world, where nothing grows, to fetch Hunger itself. Hunger was a thin, grey figure, all bones and hollow eyes. [slowly] That night, it crept into Erysichthon's bedroom while he slept, wrapped its arms around him, and breathed itself into his body. [pause] He dreamed of food. And when he woke, he was starving.

## ending

[building tension] He ate everything in the palace. He ordered more, and more, and still he was hungry. The more he ate, the hungrier he became. He sold his herds, his horses, his land, all to buy food. [grave] When everything else was gone, he sold his own daughter. But she prayed to the sea god, who gave her the power to change her shape, and she slipped away from every buyer. So her father sold her again and again.

[darkly] At last, there was nothing left. No money, no food, nobody left to sell. And the king, still starving, began to tear at his own arms and legs, and eat them.

## close

[softly] And the grove grew green again, while the king who had cut down its tree was gone, every last piece of him.
