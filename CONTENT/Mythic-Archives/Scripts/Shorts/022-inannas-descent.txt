## story

[warmly] This is the oldest story we have of a journey to the land of the dead, told in ancient Sumer, in what is now Iraq, about a goddess called Inanna.

[with wonder] Inanna was the queen of heaven, the goddess of love and of war, bright and fearless. [curious] One day, she decided to travel down to the great below, the land of the dead, ruled by her older sister. Nobody knows why for certain. Perhaps she wanted that kingdom too.

[thoughtful] Before she left, she dressed in all her power. A crown on her head. Blue beads around her neck. A golden ring. A jewelled breastplate, and a royal robe. [serious] And she gave instructions to her loyal servant, Ninshubur. If I have not come back in three days, she said, beat the drums, cry out for me, and go to the gods to beg for help.

[ominous] Then she walked down to the land of the dead, and knocked at its gate. [hushed] Her sister ordered the gatekeeper to open the seven gates, one at a time. At the first gate, the gatekeeper took her crown. Why? asked Inanna. Quiet, he said. These are the rules of the dead. At the second gate, he took her beads. At the third, her breastplate. On and on, gate after gate, until at the seventh, he took her royal robe. [slowly] And Inanna came into her sister's throne room naked, with nothing left at all.

[darkly] Her sister rose. The judges of the dead fixed their eyes on Inanna, the eyes of death. [pause] And Inanna fell, and became a corpse, and was hung on a hook on the wall.

## ending

[building tension] Three days passed. Ninshubur did as she was told, and went to god after god. Two refused. But the god of wisdom took dirt from under his fingernails, and made two tiny creatures, small enough to slip under a door like flies. They carried the food and water of life down into the dark, sprinkled them over Inanna's body, and she rose up, alive.

[grave] But nobody leaves the land of the dead without sending someone back in their place. Demons followed Inanna home, looking for someone to take. [darkly] She found her husband, Dumuzi, sitting on his throne in fine clothes, who had not mourned for her at all. She pointed at him. And the demons dragged him down.

## close

[softly] Later his sister begged to share his fate, so each of them spends half of every year below, and half above, in the light.
