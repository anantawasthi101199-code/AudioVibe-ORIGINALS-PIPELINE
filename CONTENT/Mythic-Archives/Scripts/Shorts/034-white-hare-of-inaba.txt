## story

[warmly] This is a Japanese story about a clever hare who was too clever, and the kind young god who helped him.

[lightly] Long ago, a white hare lived on a small island called Oki, off the coast of Japan. He wanted to cross to the mainland, to a place called Inaba. But he could not swim that far. [mischievously] So he had an idea. He went to the shore and called out to the wani, great creatures of the sea, sharks as big as boats. I bet there are more hares on my island than there are of you in the whole sea, he said. [slyly] Line up from here to the mainland, and I will count you.

[playfully] The sharks lined up in a long row across the water. And the hare hopped from back to back, counting as he went. One, two, three. [building tension] He was nearly across. Just as he reached the last shark, he could not help himself. Ha, he laughed. I tricked you. I only wanted a bridge. [intense] The last shark snapped round, grabbed him, and tore off all his fur.

[softly] The hare lay on the beach, raw and bleeding, and cried with pain.

[curious] Along the beach came eighty brothers, all gods, on their way to ask a beautiful princess of Inaba to marry one of them. [darkly] They saw the hare, and laughed. Wash yourself in the sea, they said, and then lie in the wind on top of a hill. [grave] The hare did. The salt dried on his skin and cracked it, and the pain was a hundred times worse.

[gently] Far behind the brothers came the youngest, carrying all their bags, because they treated him like a servant. [pause] He saw the hare, and stopped.

## ending

[tender] Why are you crying? he asked. The hare told him everything, the sharks, the trick, the brothers. [gently] The young god did not laugh. Go to the river, he said, and wash yourself in fresh water. Then gather the soft yellow pollen of the bulrushes, and roll in it.

[with wonder] The hare did. And the pain went away. His skin healed, and soft white fur grew back over his whole body.

[knowingly] The hare looked up at the kind young god, and made a prediction. Your brothers will never win the princess, he said. You will. [softly] And when the eighty brothers reached Inaba, the princess turned every one of them down, and chose the youngest, the one who had carried the bags.

## close

[warmly] And the white hare of Inaba is still honoured at a shrine on that same stretch of beach.
