## story

[warmly] This is a Russian story about Sadko, a musician who played so beautifully that the King of the Sea himself wanted to keep him.

[softly] Sadko lived in Novgorod, a great old trading city in the north of Russia. He was poor, and he earned his living playing the gusli, a stringed instrument held across the lap, at the feasts of rich merchants. [serious] But one winter, nobody invited him. Day after day, no feasts, no money, nothing to eat.

[thoughtful] So, sad and lonely, he walked to the shore of a great lake outside the city, sat on a rock, and played for himself. [with wonder] As he played, the water began to churn. Waves rose, though there was no wind. And out of the lake rose the King of the Sea, huge and green, with seaweed in his beard.

[warmly] You have played so beautifully, said the king, that I will reward you. Go back to the city. Bet the merchants that there are fish with golden fins in this lake. [slyly] Sadko made the bet, against the richest merchants in Novgorod, for all their shops and goods. They threw their nets into the lake, and pulled out fish with golden fins.

[delighted] Sadko won. Overnight, he was one of the richest merchants in the city. He filled ships with goods and sailed them across the seas to trade.

[ominous] But years later, as his ships sailed home, they suddenly stopped in the middle of the open sea. No wind could move them. [hushed] Sadko knew what it meant. The King of the Sea had never been paid. [pause] And he wanted Sadko.

## ending

[slowly] Sadko said goodbye to his crew, took his gusli, and climbed down onto a wooden plank on the water. The sea closed over him. [with wonder] He woke in a palace on the sea bed, made of white stone, with the King of the Sea on his throne. Play, said the king.

[building tension] Sadko played, and the king began to dance. He danced faster and faster, and up on the surface, great storms rose, and ships were sinking. [softly] Then a kindly old man appeared beside Sadko, and whispered: break your strings. Sadko snapped them, and the music stopped. The dancing stopped. The storms calmed.

[tender] The king offered him a bride from the sea, and Sadko chose the last of the maidens, the spirit of a small river. He fell asleep beside her. [warmly] And when he woke, he was lying on the bank of that very river, just outside Novgorod, as his ships came sailing home.

## close

[softly] Sadko never went back to sea, and never again played his gusli on the shore of that lake.
