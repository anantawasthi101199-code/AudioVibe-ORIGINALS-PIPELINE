## story

[warmly] This is the most famous fairy tale in Japan, about a boy who came out of a peach, and his strange little army.

[softly] Long ago, an old man and an old woman lived near a river. They were kind, but poor, and very sad, because they had never had a child. [lightly] One day, the old woman was washing clothes in the river, when she saw something enormous floating towards her. It was a peach, as big as a cooking pot, pink and golden and smelling sweet.

[delighted] She pulled it out of the water and carried it home, to share with her husband. [with wonder] But as they lifted the knife to cut it, the peach split open by itself. And inside was a baby boy, laughing up at them.

[tender] The old couple were overjoyed. They named him Momotaro, which means Peach Boy. He grew up big and strong, and kind, and they loved him with all their hearts.

[serious] But the village had a problem. Across the sea was Onigashima, Ogre Island, home to the oni, huge horned ogres with red and blue skin. They often sailed over, stole the villagers' treasure, and carried off their rice. [determined] When Momotaro was fifteen, he told his parents he was going to the island to stop them.

[tender] The old woman made him a bag of millet dumplings for the journey, the best in all Japan. [pause] And Momotaro set off.

## ending

[lightly] On the road, a dog stopped him. Give me a dumpling, and I will come with you, it said. Momotaro gave him one. A little further on, a monkey asked the same, and then a pheasant, a bright bird with long tail feathers. Each one ate a dumpling, and joined him.

[building tension] They sailed to Ogre Island. Its gate was huge and locked. [playfully] The pheasant flew over the wall and pecked at the ogres' eyes. The monkey climbed over and opened the gate. The dog charged in and bit their legs. And Momotaro rushed in after them, fighting with all his strength.

[delighted] The ogre king fell to his knees and begged for mercy. He promised never to steal again, and gave back all the treasure. [warmly] Momotaro and his three friends loaded it onto a cart, and pulled it all the way home.

## close

[tender] The old man and the old woman were waiting at the door, and they lived happily, and never went hungry again.
