## story

[warmly] This is the oldest great story ever written down, about a cruel king, a wild man made to stop him, and the friendship that changed them both.

[curious] Five thousand years ago, in a land between two rivers, in what is now Iraq, stood the walled city of Uruk. Its king was Gilgamesh. He was two parts god and one part human, taller and stronger than any man alive. [darkly] And he was a bully. He wore out the young men with endless games and labour. He took whatever he wanted. The people were exhausted, and they prayed to the gods for help.

[with wonder] The gods heard them. The mother goddess washed her hands, took a pinch of clay, and threw it down into the wilderness. From that clay grew a man called Enkidu. [lightly] He was covered in hair. He ate grass with the gazelles and drank at the waterholes with the wild animals. He tore up the hunters' traps and set the animals free.

[thoughtful] A hunter saw him and was terrified. He went to the king for help, and a woman from the temple went out to meet Enkidu. She stayed with him for a week, and taught him to be a person. She gave him bread and beer for the first time. She cut his hair. She dressed him in clothes. [softly] When he went back to the waterhole, the gazelles ran from him. He belonged to the human world now.

[building tension] Then she told him about Gilgamesh, and how cruelly he treated his people. Enkidu grew angry. He walked to Uruk, and stood in a doorway the king meant to walk through. [pause] Gilgamesh arrived. And Enkidu would not move.

## ending

[intense] They grabbed each other and fought. They wrestled through the street like two bulls. The doorframe shattered. The walls shook. [slowly] And at last, Gilgamesh pinned Enkidu to the ground. Then something strange happened. His anger simply left him. Enkidu looked up and praised him. The king pulled him to his feet, and they embraced.

[warmly] From that moment, they were inseparable. And Gilgamesh, with a true friend beside him, wanted something greater than bullying his own people. [building tension] So the two of them travelled far to the great Cedar Forest, and there they faced its terrifying guardian, Humbaba, a giant whose roar was a flood and whose breath was fire. Together, they killed him, cut down the tallest cedar, and floated it home down the river.

## close

[softly] And they made a great door from that cedar for the temple in Uruk, a door the two friends had built together.
