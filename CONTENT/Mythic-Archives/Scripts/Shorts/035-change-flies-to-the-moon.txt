## story

[warmly] This is the Chinese story of Chang'e, the woman who lives on the moon, and how she came to be there.

[softly] Chang'e was married to Yi, the great archer who had saved the world by shooting nine burning suns out of the sky. [grave] But the father of those suns was a god, and he punished Yi for killing his sons. He took away Yi's place in heaven. Yi and his wife Chang'e would have to live on earth as ordinary people. They would grow old. And one day they would die.

[determined] Yi could not accept it. He travelled far to the west, to a great mountain called Kunlun, where the Queen Mother of the West lived, a goddess who knew the secrets of life. [with wonder] She took pity on him, and gave him a small amount of a magic potion, the elixir of immortality. [serious] There is enough here for two people to live forever on earth, she told him. But if one person drinks it all, they will rise up to heaven, and become a god.

[tender] Yi took the elixir home. He wanted to share it with Chang'e, so they could live together forever. He hid it safely in the house, and waited for the right day.

[curious] But while Yi was away, Chang'e found where it was hidden. She opened it. [slowly] And she drank it, all of it.

[with wonder] At once, her body felt light. Lighter than a feather. Her feet left the floor. She floated up out of the house, and into the night sky. [pause] And she kept on rising.

## ending

[softly] She was afraid of what Yi would say when he found out. Heaven was full of gods who might mock her. So she flew to the moon, and stayed there.

[hushed] The moon was cold and silent. There was a great palace made of ice, empty and white. A single cassia tree grew there. And beneath it, one other creature lived, a jade rabbit, pounding herbs with a pestle to make more of the elixir, forever.

[tender] When Yi came home and found her gone, he looked up at the moon, and thought he saw her shape there. He set out a table in the garden, with the fruits and cakes she loved best, as an offering to her.

## close

[warmly] And every autumn, on the night of the brightest full moon, families across China still set out cakes and look up to find her.
