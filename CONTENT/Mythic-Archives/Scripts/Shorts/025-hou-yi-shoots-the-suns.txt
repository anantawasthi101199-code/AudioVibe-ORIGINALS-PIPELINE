## story

[warmly] This is the Chinese story of a time when there were ten suns in the sky, and the great archer who shot nine of them down.

[with wonder] Far in the east, beyond the sea, grew an enormous tree, taller than any mountain. In its branches lived ten suns. They were brothers, the children of the god of the eastern sky. [lightly] Every morning, one of them took his turn. He climbed into his mother's chariot and crossed the sky, giving the world its light and warmth. Then he came home, and the next day it was his brother's turn.

[softly] For thousands of years, it worked perfectly. One sun a day.

[mischievously] But the suns got bored. And one morning, for fun, all ten of them rose into the sky together.

[intense] The heat was unbearable. Crops shrivelled in the fields. Rivers dried up into cracked mud. Rocks began to melt, and forests burst into flames. [grave] People hid in caves and under the ground, and they were dying. Monsters crawled out of the scorched land and attacked them.

[serious] The good king of the people, Yao, prayed to heaven for help. And the father of the suns heard him. He did not want his sons to destroy the world. [determined] So he sent down the greatest archer in heaven, Yi, with a red bow and a quiver of white arrows. Frighten them, he told Yi. Make them go home.

[thoughtful] Yi stood on the burning earth and looked up at the ten blazing suns. He shouted at them to go home. They ignored him. [pause] So Yi drew back his bowstring.

## ending

[intense] His first arrow flew straight up into the sky, and one of the suns burst apart. Something fell, flaming, to earth. When people ran to look, they found a great crow with three legs, dead, with an arrow through it. [building tension] Yi shot again, and again. Each time a sun exploded, a three-legged crow fell, and the world grew a little cooler.

[slowly] He had shot nine, and was reaching for his next arrow, when King Yao quietly slipped one arrow out of his quiver. The people need one sun, he said. Leave them that one.

[grave] The world was saved. But the god of the eastern sky had lost nine of his sons. He was furious, and he banished Yi from heaven, to live on earth as a mortal man.

## close

[softly] And ever since, one sun crosses the sky each day, alone.
