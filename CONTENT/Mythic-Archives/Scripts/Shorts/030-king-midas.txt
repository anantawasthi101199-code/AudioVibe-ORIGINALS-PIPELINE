## story

[warmly] This is the Greek story of King Midas, who was given exactly what he wished for, and very nearly died of it.

[lightly] Midas was the king of Phrygia, a rich land in what is now Turkey. One day his people found an old man asleep in his rose garden, still drunk from the night before. They tied him up with flowers and brought him to the king. [curious] Midas recognised him at once. This was Silenus, the old teacher of Dionysus, the god of wine.

[warmly] Midas could have been angry. Instead he welcomed the old man, and threw a feast for him that lasted ten days. Then he took him home to Dionysus.

[with wonder] The god was so grateful that he offered Midas a gift. Anything you wish for, he said. [slyly] Midas did not even have to think. Let everything I touch turn to gold.

[serious] Dionysus was sad, because he knew it was a foolish wish. But a god keeps his promise. Your wish is granted, he said.

[delighted] Midas walked home, hardly able to believe it. He snapped a twig from an oak tree, and it turned to gold in his hand. He picked up a stone, and it turned to gold. He touched an apple on a tree, and it shone as if it came from a magical garden. [playfully] He ran his hands over the doorposts of his palace, and they turned to gold. He washed his hands, and the water that ran over them turned to gold too. [pause] Then he called for a feast, and sat down to eat.

## ending

[slowly] He tore off a piece of bread. It turned hard and cold in his fingers, a lump of metal. He lifted meat to his mouth, and his teeth clanged against gold. [grave] He poured wine, and it flowed into his mouth as liquid gold. He could not eat. He could not drink. Sitting at a table piled with food, he began to starve.

[tender] Midas raised his golden arms to heaven, and begged Dionysus to take the gift back. [gently] The god felt sorry for him. Go to the River Pactolus, he said, and follow it up to its spring. Put your head under the water, and wash yourself.

[softly] Midas did. As the water ran over him, the golden touch flowed out of his body and into the river.

## close

[with wonder] And the sand in the River Pactolus still glittered with gold, ever after, where the king's foolish wish had washed away.
