## opening

[warmly] You walk into a meeting, or a party, or a waiting room, and before anyone has said a word, you have already decided. That one seems nice. That one seems full of themselves. That one, you wouldn't trust with your keys.

[thoughtful] It's like a shop assistant slapping a price sticker on every item as it comes off the lorry, before anyone has looked inside the box. You didn't choose to do it. It's not because you're shallow or unkind, and it isn't some flaw of yours. If you've ever been embarrassed by how fast you judged somebody, you're not alone.

[warmly] This is called a first impression. Today we'll look at what's actually happening in those first moments, why that sticker is so hard to peel off, and what helps, whether you're the one judging or the one being judged.

## picture

[warmly] Picture your brain as a very busy doorman at a very busy door. All day long, faces arrive. He doesn't have time to interview them all. So he does a quick glance. Safe or not safe. Friend or stranger. Let in, or keep an eye on.

[curious] Now, the doorman is not asking who this person really is. He only wants a quick guess, good enough to get through the door. For our ancestors, a fast guess about a stranger on the path could keep them alive. A slow, careful one might not.

[thoughtful] So a first impression isn't a judgement of someone's character, even though it feels like one. It's a guess, a really fast one, that feels like a fact. It's not knowledge. [warmly] And it's a bit embarrassing how confident the doorman sounds, for a fellow who has only had a tenth of a second to look.

## brain

[curious] People often think we size someone up over the first few minutes of meeting them. It's actually much faster than that. In 2006, two psychologists at Princeton University in America, Janine Willis and Alexander Todorov, showed people photographs of faces, for just a tenth of a second each. That's quicker than a blink. Then they asked them to judge things like how trustworthy each person looked.

[thoughtful] Other people were given as long as they wanted with the same faces. The judgements hardly differed. After a tenth of a second, people had already reached largely the same view, and all that extra time mostly made them more sure of it.

[curious] The reason it sticks is something psychologists call confirmation bias, our habit of noticing what fits with what we already believe. Once the sticker is on, you start collecting evidence for it. If you've decided a new colleague is rude, then a short email looks rude. If you've decided they're lovely, the same email looks busy.

[measured] And here's the hard part. Those fast guesses are often wrong. Todorov and others have found that what we see in a face tells us very little about what a person is like. We agree with one another about who looks trustworthy, but agreeing doesn't make us right. So the feeling is real, and it's very often mistaken. That's nobody's failure. It's what a fast guess is like.

## real_life

[warmly] You meet your partner's friend, and they barely smile at you. Three years later, you're still a bit cool towards them. You've only just found out they were terrified of you.

[thoughtful] You start a new job, and on the first day your manager looks tired and doesn't say much. You spend weeks being careful around them, and you can't quite relax.

[curious] Or it's you on the other side of it. You've got an interview, and the moment you walk in you trip on the mat. You spend the next half hour sure that it's over, while your hands sweat, and you can hardly hear the questions.

[measured] And then the one that stings. Someone you'd written off turns out to be kind to you when you really need it, and you feel relieved and rather ashamed, both at once.

## feelings

[thoughtful] If you're someone who worries about how you come across, your inner voice can get very loud. Why did I say that? They must think I'm an idiot. Everyone else just walks into a room and seems fine. What's wrong with me?

[warmly] So you hide it. You rehearse what you'll say. You smile a bit too hard. You look relaxed on the outside, and on the inside you're replaying the first five seconds on a loop, all the way home.

[measured] Shame doesn't sit still. It takes away your warmth, your humour, the very things that would make people like you. That's why it feels so heavy. There's the moment itself, and then there's the punishing yourself for it, both at once.

## helps

[warmly] Here's the kinder way to see this. Your mind isn't trying to make you unfair. It's trying to keep you safe in a crowd of strangers, as quickly as it can. You don't need to stop the doorman. You just need to stop letting him have the last word.

[thoughtful] So pushing away the judgement isn't what helps. Slowing down what you do with it is. First, name it as a guess. Instead of "she's cold", try "my first guess is she's cold". It's a small change of words, and it's the difference between a fact and a sticker that can be peeled.

[curious] Second, go looking for one thing that doesn't fit. So instead of "he's arrogant, I knew it", try "what's one thing he's done this week that doesn't match that?" You are only asking the other side to give evidence too.

[warmly] Third, use another person. Ask a friend who knows them, "What are they actually like?" Often one sentence from outside your own head changes everything. And if you're the one who's worried about how you came across, try this. Instead of "I ruined it", try "they've probably forgotten, and they were worrying about themselves". Research on something called the liking gap suggests that after a first chat, people tend to like us more than we think.

[thoughtful] You don't have to stop judging. You don't have to be perfect. You only need to leave the door open a little longer.

## close

[warmly] So, the doorman is always going to be there, glancing up, making his guess. It's who he is. He's just not the owner of the building. You are.

[thoughtful] Here's one small thing for today. Think of one person you made your mind up about quickly. Just one. And see if you can find one small thing about them that doesn't fit. You don't need to do anything with it. Just let the sticker lift at one corner.
