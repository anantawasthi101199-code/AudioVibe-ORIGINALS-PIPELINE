## moment

[warmly] You're getting ready for something scary. A job interview, a big conversation. And in your head, there's a voice going round and round. Don't mess this up. What if you freeze? [a beat] Or you're looking for your keys, muttering out loud, where did I put them, where did I put them.

[gently] If you talk to yourself, inside or out loud, you're not weird, and you're definitely not alone. Almost everybody does it. Psychologists call it inner speech, and it's one of the ways your mind organises itself.

## why

[thoughtful] Here's what's going on. Your inner voice is like a coach standing at the side of the pitch, shouting instructions while you play. It helps you plan, remember steps, and keep going. [curious] Small children do this out loud. They talk themselves through a puzzle. As we grow up, the voice mostly moves inside.

[serious] But when we're stressed, the coach can turn harsh. And it speaks to us as I. Why do I always do this? [softly] A psychologist named Ethan Kross found something simple that helps. When people talked to themselves using their own name, or you, instead of I, before giving a stressful speech, they felt less anxious and performed better. [gently] Using your name puts a tiny bit of distance between you and the worry. It's like the coach stepping back and giving advice to a friend.

## one_thing

[warmly] Here's one thing to try. Next time you're nervous, ask yourself one question, out loud or in your head, starting with your own name. Your name, then: what do you need right now? [gently] Then listen to the answer, like you would for a friend.
