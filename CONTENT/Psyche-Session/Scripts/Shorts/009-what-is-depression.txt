## moment

[gently] You wake up, and before you've even opened your eyes, the day already feels heavy. The things you used to enjoy, music, friends, your favourite food, feel flat, like someone turned the colour down. [softly] And it's been like this for weeks.

[warmly] If that sounds like you, this is not weakness, and it is not self-pity. It is something that happens to a lot of people. Around one in six adults will go through depression at some point in their lives. [thoughtful] Depression is a real illness, and it's one of the most common there is.

## why

[thoughtful] Here's the one thing that explains the difference. Sadness is usually about something. Something happened, it hurts, and over days or weeks, the hurt eases, and good moments still get through. Depression is more like a heavy grey blanket over everything at once. It stays, and good news doesn't lift it.

[curious] Doctors look for a few signs together, most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks. A low mood, or losing interest and pleasure in things. Then changes like sleeping much more or much less, eating differently, feeling exhausted, struggling to concentrate, or feeling worthless or guilty. [gently] For some people it shows up mostly as irritability, or aches and tiredness, rather than tears.

[warmly] And here's what matters most. Depression is treatable. Talking therapies, medicines, exercise and support all help, and most people get better.

## one_thing

[warmly] So here's one thing to do today, if this has gone on for a couple of weeks. Book a GP appointment, or tell one person you trust how you've been feeling. [serious] And if you ever feel you might hurt yourself, call 999 or go to A and E, or call the Samaritans, any time, free, on one one six, one two three.
