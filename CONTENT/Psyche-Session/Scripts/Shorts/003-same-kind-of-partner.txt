## moment

[gently] New person. New name. New start. And then, a few months in, you notice it. The same arguments. The same feeling of not quite being heard. The same ending, again.

[warmly] If that keeps happening, you're not cursed, and you're not choosing badly on purpose. Many people are drawn, without realising it, to what feels familiar, even when familiar isn't what's good for them.

## why

[thoughtful] Think of how a well-worn armchair feels. [curious] It might be a bit lumpy. The springs might dig in. But it fits the shape of you, and when you sit in it, part of you relaxes and thinks, this is home.

[knowingly] Early relationships, often with the people who raised us, shape what love feels like. If love once felt like having to work hard for attention, or like walking on eggshells, then someone who makes you feel that way can feel strangely right, like home, while someone calm and steady can feel boring, or even uncomfortable. [softly] It's not that you want to be hurt. It's that the familiar feeling gets mistaken for chemistry.

## one_thing

[warmly] The next time you feel a strong spark with someone, pause and ask yourself one question: does this feel exciting, or does it feel familiar?
