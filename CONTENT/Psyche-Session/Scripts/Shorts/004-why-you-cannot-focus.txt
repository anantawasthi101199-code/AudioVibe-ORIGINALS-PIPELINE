## moment

[warmly] You sit down to write one email. Just one. And then your phone lights up. You glance at it. You reply. You go back to the email, and you read the first line three times, and nothing goes in. [a beat] Twenty minutes later, the email still isn't written.

[gently] If that's you, you're not lazy, and your brain isn't broken. Almost everyone who works around screens feels this. Researchers even have a name for part of it: attention residue.

## why

[thoughtful] Here's what's happening. When you switch from one task to another, your attention doesn't move over all at once. Part of it stays behind, still thinking about the last thing. [curious] Imagine pouring water from one glass into another. However quickly you pour, a little stays clinging to the first glass. Switch ten times, and there's hardly any water left in the glass you need.

[serious] And each interruption costs more than the moment it takes. One researcher, Gloria Mark, who has studied people at work for years, found that after being pulled away from a task, it often took more than twenty minutes to get properly back to it. [softly] So it isn't one glance. It's one glance, and then twenty minutes of half your mind somewhere else.

## one_thing

[warmly] Here's one thing to try. Next time you need to focus, put your phone in another room, not just face down, for twenty-five minutes. [gently] Only twenty-five. See how much is left in the glass.
