## moment

[warmly] Ten people tell you they loved what you did. One person says something critical. And that night, lying in bed, which one are you replaying? [gently] It's the one. Always the one.

[thoughtful] If that's you, you're not being dramatic, and you're not ungrateful for the good comments. Your brain is doing something every human brain does. Psychologists call it the negativity bias: we notice bad things more, and remember them longer, than good things of the same size.

## why

[curious] Think of your brain as a smoke alarm. [knowingly] A smoke alarm that goes off when someone burns the toast is annoying. But a smoke alarm that stays quiet during a real fire is deadly. So the safest alarm is a sensitive one, even if it overreacts.

[thoughtful] For our ancestors, missing a danger, a snake in the grass, an angry face in the group, could cost them their life. Missing a nice berry only cost them lunch. So brains that paid extra attention to bad news survived better. We inherited those brains. [softly] One critical comment sets off the alarm, while ten kind ones barely make a sound.

## one_thing

[warmly] Tonight, when the bad comment comes back, try this. Say one of the good ones out loud to yourself, word for word, as clearly as you can remember it.
