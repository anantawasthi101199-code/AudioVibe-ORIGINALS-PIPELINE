## hook

[warmly] You went for your first run in months, or tried a new class at the gym. You felt fine that evening. [with a smile] Two days later, getting down the stairs is a slow, careful business, and sitting on the toilet is an event. Everyone says it's lactic acid. Is it?

## story

[thoughtful] The lactic acid idea comes from something real. When you exercise hard, your muscles break down sugar faster than oxygen can arrive, and they make a substance called lactate. That's linked to the burning feeling in your legs during a sprint. So it seemed natural to blame it for the ache afterwards too.

[curious] But when scientists measured it, the lactate was gone. It clears from the blood within about an hour of stopping, and the muscle uses much of it as fuel. [knowingly] So by the time your legs ache on the stairs, a day or two later, there's nothing left to blame.

[thoughtful] So what is it? The ache has a name, delayed onset muscle soreness, which just means soreness that arrives late. And the clue is in the kind of movement that causes it. It's worst after muscles work while they're being stretched, like your thighs braking you when you run downhill, or lowering a heavy weight slowly.

[curious] That kind of work causes tiny areas of damage inside the muscle fibres. Then your body starts repairing them. It's a bit like roadworks after a storm. The repair crew arrive the next day, and the swelling and sensitivity that come with the repair are what you feel. [warmly] That's why it peaks one to three days after, not straight away.

[thoughtful] And here's the hopeful part. The muscle adapts. Do the same exercise again a week later and you'll be much less sore. Studies agree on this strongly, and it's why the second run of the season never hurts as much as the first.

## verdict

[warmly] So is it lactic acid? No. It's your muscles repairing themselves after work they weren't used to. [thoughtful] The NHS says it usually eases within a few days, and that gentle movement, rest, and building up exercise gradually can help. [serious] If the pain is severe, or your pee turns dark brown, that's a reason to get medical help quickly. [softly] So the next time the stairs feel like a mountain two days after a run, that's not damage done. That's your legs getting ready for the next one.
