## hook

[curious] Is a migraine just a really bad headache? [warmly] Lots of people think so. Take a paracetamol, have a lie down, it'll pass.

## story

[thoughtful] For centuries, doctors thought migraines were about blood vessels in the head swelling. That idea came from the throbbing pain. But over the last few decades, brain scans and research have shown it's much more than that. [serious] A migraine is a condition of the brain and nerves. It often comes in stages. Some people feel tired, hungry or irritable a day before. Then comes the pain, often on one side, usually with feeling sick and finding light and noise unbearable. It can last from a few hours to three days.

[curious] And about a third of people with migraine get an aura first. That's a warning, often flashing lights, zigzag lines or a blind spot in your vision, lasting up to an hour. [thoughtful] Researchers think it's caused by a slow wave of electrical activity spreading across the surface of the brain, a bit like a ripple across a pond.

[warmly] Researchers have also found a chemical messenger, called CGRP, that helps switch on migraine pain. That discovery led to newer migraine medicines, tested in large trials, which block it.

## verdict

[serious] So, is a migraine just a bad headache? No. It's a real neurological condition, and the NHS says there are treatments that help, from painkillers and anti-sickness medicines to specific migraine medicines. [gently] If headaches are affecting your life, a GP or pharmacist is the person to ask. And the NHS says to call 999 for a sudden, extremely severe headache, or one with weakness, confusion or slurred speech.
