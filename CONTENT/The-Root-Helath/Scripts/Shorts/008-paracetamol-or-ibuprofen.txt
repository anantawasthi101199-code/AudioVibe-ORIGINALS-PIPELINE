## hook

[warmly] Your head is pounding, or you've twisted your ankle, or your child is hot and grumpy with a temperature. You open the cupboard, and there are two boxes. Paracetamol, and ibuprofen. [curious] Does it matter which one you take?

## story

[thoughtful] It does, a little, because they work in different ways. Ibuprofen belongs to a group of medicines called anti-inflammatories. When you're injured, your body makes chemical messengers that cause swelling, heat and pain around the injury. Ibuprofen blocks the step that makes them. [knowingly] So it's good at pain that comes with swelling: a sprained ankle, toothache, period pain, sore joints.

[curious] Paracetamol is more of a puzzle. It's been used for well over a hundred years, and scientists still don't fully agree how it works, though it seems to act mainly in the brain and spinal cord, turning down pain signals and fever. [thoughtful] It does very little for swelling. But it's gentle on the stomach, and most people can take it, including in pregnancy.

[serious] Each has its own risks, and they're different. Ibuprofen can irritate the stomach, and can cause ulcers and bleeding. It isn't suitable for everyone, including some people with asthma, stomach ulcers, or kidney or heart problems. [grave] Paracetamol is very safe at the right amount, but taking too much can badly damage the liver, and the danger is that it hides in other things, like cold and flu remedies. Two products at once can quietly add up to too much.

[thoughtful] So back to that cupboard. For the sprained ankle, ibuprofen tackles the swelling too. For a headache or a fever, either can work, and many people find paracetamol enough.

## verdict

[warmly] So does it matter? Yes, with a catch: both work well, but ibuprofen also eases swelling, and each has its own safety limits. [thoughtful] The NHS advises checking the leaflet for how much and how often, never taking two products with the same medicine in them, and asking a pharmacist if you're not sure which suits you, especially if you're pregnant or have a long-term condition. [softly] So next time you open the cupboard, the right box depends on whether there's swelling, and what else you've already taken.
