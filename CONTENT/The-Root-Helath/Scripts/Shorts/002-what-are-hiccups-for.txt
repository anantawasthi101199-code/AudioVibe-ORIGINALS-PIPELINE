## hook

[warmly] You're halfway through a film, and it starts. Hic. Then again. You hold your breath. You sip water. Someone tries to make you jump. [curious] Hic. So what are hiccups actually for, and why won't they stop?

## story

[thoughtful] A hiccup starts with your diaphragm, the dome-shaped muscle under your lungs that pulls air in every time you breathe. It's controlled by nerves running down from your neck. When those nerves get irritated, the diaphragm gives a sudden twitch, and you gulp in air. [lightly] A split second later, the flap at the top of your windpipe snaps shut. That snap is the hic.

[curious] So what irritates the nerves? Very often, your stomach. It sits right underneath the diaphragm. When it stretches fast, from eating quickly, a big meal or a fizzy drink, it presses up against the muscle and sets it off. [knowingly] So that film-night popcorn and cola might be the reason it started.

[with wonder] Most bouts stop within minutes. But in 1922, an American farmer called Charles Osborne started hiccupping while lifting a pig, and kept going for about sixty-eight years. He still married, had children and lived a normal life.

[thoughtful] As for why we have them at all, nobody knows for sure. One idea is that it's a leftover from our very distant ancestors. Tadpoles make a similar movement to push water over their gills while keeping it out of their lungs. [warmly] Another idea is that hiccups help babies, who hiccup a lot, even in the womb, to push swallowed air back out of their stomachs while they feed. That would make it a built-in burp.

[wry] And the remedies? Holding your breath, sipping cold water, a fright. Some people swear by them, but there's little good evidence that any of them work.

## verdict

[warmly] So what are hiccups for? In adults, probably nothing. They're most likely a leftover reflex, set off by a stretched stomach. [thoughtful] The NHS says hiccups usually stop on their own within a few minutes, and that things like sipping very cold water or holding your breath may help some people. It advises seeing a GP if they last more than two days. [softly] So next time it starts halfway through the film, you can blame the popcorn, and wait it out.
