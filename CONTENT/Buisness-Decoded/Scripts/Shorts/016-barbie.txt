## story

[leaning in] More than a billion Barbie dolls have been sold. When the idea was first pitched, the men who ran the company said no mother would ever buy one.

[warmly] The idea came from Ruth Handler. In 1945, she, her husband Elliot and a friend started a small business in a garage in Los Angeles. They called it Mattel. At first they made picture frames, then used the leftover scraps of wood to make furniture for dolls' houses. Soon they were making toys.

[curious] At home, Ruth watched her daughter, Barbara, play with paper dolls. And she noticed something. Barbara did not pretend the dolls were babies. She pretended they were grown women, with jobs and clothes and adventures. [thoughtful] Every doll in the shops back then was a baby, for little girls to mother. Ruth wanted a doll that let girls imagine who they might become.

[wry] She took the idea to the company's designers, who were all men. They told her it would never sell.

[determined] Then, on a holiday in Europe, Ruth saw a grown-up doll in a shop window in Switzerland. She bought several and brought them home. Her team created their own version, and Ruth named it after her daughter. [pause] On the ninth of March, 1959, Barbie was shown to toy buyers at a big trade fair in New York. [serious] Most of them did not like it at all.

## rise

[building] But Mattel had something its rivals did not. It was already advertising on television, on a hugely popular children's programme. [knowingly] So Ruth went past the shop buyers and showed Barbie straight to the children. Girls saw her and asked for her. In the first year, about three hundred thousand were sold.

[delighted] Barbie got a boyfriend, Ken, named after Ruth's son. She had hundreds of careers, and was an astronaut in 1965, before any person had walked on the Moon. [quietly impressed] Barbie made Mattel one of the biggest toy companies in the world, and in 2023 a film about her was the biggest film of the year.

## close

[warmly] It took a mother who watched how her daughter actually played, and trusted what she saw.
