## story

[leaning in] The blue jeans in your wardrobe were invented to stop a working man's trousers from falling apart. And the inventor was a tailor in Nevada who could not afford the paperwork.

[warmly] The man whose name is on the label is Levi Strauss. He was born in 1829 in Bavaria, in southern Germany. As a teenager he sailed to New York with his family. [curious] Then, in the early 1850s, news spread that gold had been found in California. Thousands of people rushed west, hoping to get rich. Levi went too, in 1853, to San Francisco. But he did not dig for gold. He sold things to the people who did: cloth, blankets, clothes and thread, which he shipped to little shops across the American West.

[thoughtful] One of his customers was a tailor called Jacob Davis, in Reno, Nevada. One day, a woman asked Davis to make strong trousers for her husband, a labourer whose trousers kept ripping. [with a smile] Davis had an idea. He took the small copper rivets used to fix straps on horse blankets, and hammered them into the corners of the pockets, where trousers tear first.

[delighted] They did not rip. Soon every working man in Reno wanted a pair.

[serious] Davis knew others would copy him, so he wanted a patent, an official right to the invention. [pause] But a patent cost sixty-eight dollars, a lot of money then, and he did not have it.

## rise

[building] So in 1872 he wrote a letter to the cloth seller who supplied him, Levi Strauss, suggesting they go into business together. Levi said yes. On the twentieth of May, 1873, they were given the patent. [knowingly] It worked because they joined Davis's idea to Levi's money and his network of shops across the West. Their riveted trousers, in tough blue cloth called denim, were sold to miners, farmers and cowboys.

[quietly impressed] In the 1950s, film stars wore jeans on screen, and they became the clothing of young people everywhere. Today Levi's are sold in over a hundred countries, and their design has barely changed.

## close

[warmly] It took a tailor's clever fix, and a businessman who said yes to a letter.
