## story

[leaning in] At the age of forty-nine, Falguni Nayar had one of the best jobs in Indian banking. She gave it up to sell lipstick on the internet. A few years later she was one of the richest women in India.

[warmly] She was born in 1963 in Mumbai, India's biggest city, into a Gujarati family. Her father ran a small business, and at dinner the family talked about money and companies. [curious] She studied at one of India's top business schools, and joined Kotak Mahindra, a fast-growing Indian bank. She stayed nearly twenty years, rising to run its investment banking business, advising huge companies on deals worth billions.

[thoughtful] Along the way, she noticed something. Indian women were spending more on beauty products, but buying them was frustrating. In most shops, the products sat behind a counter. A sales assistant pushed whatever brand paid them most. You could not try things, compare them, or learn how to use them. [serious] And there were plenty of fake products about.

[determined] In 2012, she left banking. Friends thought she was mad. She started an online shop for beauty products and called it Nykaa, from a Sanskrit word for an actress, a woman in the spotlight. [with a smile] Her daughter and son helped her build it. [pause] Then she had to win the trust of women who had never bought make-up online.

## rise

[building] Nykaa promised that every product was genuine, bought directly from the brands. It made videos and articles showing how to use things. It sold famous brands, and helped small Indian brands grow. Later it opened real shops too. [knowingly] It worked because it treated customers as people who wanted to learn and choose for themselves, and not as people to sell to.

[quietly impressed] In 2021, Nykaa joined India's stock market, and its shares nearly doubled on the first day. Falguni Nayar became one of India's richest women, with a fortune she had built herself, starting at an age when most people are thinking about slowing down.

## close

[warmly] It took a banker who trusted what she had noticed in the shops, more than what everyone told her about starting late.
