## story

[leaning in] The idea behind Gillette came from a piece of advice. Invent something people use once, and throw away. Then they will keep coming back to buy more.

[warmly] The advice was given to King Camp Gillette, a travelling salesman, in the 1890s. His boss was the man who had invented the metal cap on top of glass bottles, which people prised off and threw away. [thoughtful] Gillette thought about that advice for years. He just could not think what to invent.

[curious] Then, one morning in 1895, he went to shave. In those days, men used a long open blade called a straight razor. It had to be sharpened all the time on a leather strap, and often taken to a professional to be ground sharp again. [wry] Gillette's razor was blunt. [with wonder] And standing there, he had his idea. A blade so thin and cheap that when it went dull, you simply threw it away, and put in a new one.

[serious] Engineers told him it was impossible. Steel that thin could not be made hard enough to stay sharp, and cheap enough to throw away. [determined] For six years, he tried, and failed. Then, in 1901, he found an engineer who finally worked out how to make the blades. [pause] In 1903, Gillette's razor went on sale.

## rise

[wry] In its first year, it sold only fifty-one razors, and a hundred and sixty-eight blades. [building] But the next year, sales soared. Gillette sold the razor handle cheaply, and made his money on the blades, which people kept buying, week after week. [knowingly] It worked because the blades were the real product, and every customer needed more. The idea became known as razor and blades, and businesses copy it to this day, from printers and ink to coffee machines and pods.

[delighted] In the First World War, the American government gave Gillette razors to millions of soldiers. They came home and kept buying the blades. [quietly impressed] In 2005 Procter and Gamble bought Gillette for fifty-seven billion dollars.

## close

[warmly] It took a salesman with a blunt razor, and a piece of advice he never forgot.
