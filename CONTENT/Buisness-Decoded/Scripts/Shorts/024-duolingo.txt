## story

[leaning in] More than a hundred million people use Duolingo every month to learn a language, often because a cartoon green owl keeps reminding them. It was built by a professor who wanted to make learning free for everyone.

[warmly] The professor was Luis von Ahn. He grew up in Guatemala, in Central America. His mother sent him to a school that taught in English, and he knew how lucky he was. [serious] Around him, he saw that learning English could change a person's life, and that it cost money most families did not have. Even taking the official English test cost a lot, and for him, it meant travelling to another country to sit it.

[curious] He moved to America, to Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh, and became a famous computer scientist. He helped invent those wobbly letter puzzles websites use to check you are a human and not a computer program. He sold one version to Google.

[determined] But he still wanted to fix language learning. In 2011, he and one of his students, Severin Hacker, started Duolingo. It would be free. And it would not feel like school. [with a smile] Short lessons of a few minutes. Points for every right answer. Little cheers and sounds. Levels to climb.

[thoughtful] People tried it, and liked it. But a free app has a problem. People download it, use it for a few days, and forget about it. [pause] So Duolingo found a way to make them come back.

## rise

[building] The streak. Every day you do a lesson, your streak grows by one. Miss a day, and it goes back to zero. People hated losing their streaks, so they kept practising. And then there was the owl, Duo, who sent cheeky reminders whenever you skipped a lesson. [delighted] Those reminders became an internet joke, with people pretending the owl was coming to find them. [knowingly] It worked because it treated learning like a game, and games are things people choose to come back to.

[quietly impressed] Duolingo joined the stock market in 2021. It still offers its lessons free, with paid extras, and teaches dozens of languages to people all over the world.

## close

[warmly] It took a professor who remembered what it cost to learn, and a little green owl that never lets you forget.
