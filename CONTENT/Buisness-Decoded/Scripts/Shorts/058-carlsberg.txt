## story

[leaning in] Many breweries guard their secrets. Carlsberg gave its greatest discovery away for free, to every brewer in the world.

[warmly] J. C. Jacobsen was a brewer's son in Copenhagen, the capital of Denmark. [curious] In the 1840s, he fell in love with the light, golden lager brewed in Bavaria, in southern Germany. To make it, he needed the right yeast, the tiny living thing that turns sugar into alcohol. [with a smile] So he travelled to Munich, and carried some yeast home in a hatbox, on a long journey by stagecoach, cooling it with water at every stop.

[determined] In 1847, he built a brewery on a hill outside Copenhagen, and named it after his five-year-old son, Carl. Carl's hill. Carlsberg.

[serious] But brewing then had a big problem. Sometimes a whole batch of beer went sour, or tasted strange, and nobody knew why. Brewers threw away huge amounts. [thoughtful] Jacobsen believed science could solve it. So in 1875, he did something unusual. He built a laboratory, filled with scientists, right there at his brewery. [pause] A few years later, one of them made the breakthrough.

## rise

[with wonder] In 1883 a scientist called Emil Christian Hansen found that the beer went wrong because the yeast was a mixture of different kinds. He found a way to grow pure yeast from a single cell. Beer made with it tasted the same every time. [delighted] And Jacobsen gave the pure yeast to any brewer who asked, for free. Lager brewers all over the world began using it. [knowingly] It worked because Jacobsen cared about good beer and good science more than about keeping a secret.

[quietly impressed] He also left most of the company to a foundation, which still controls Carlsberg today, and uses its profits to fund scientific research and art. At the Carlsberg laboratory, a scientist even invented the pH scale, which measures how acidic something is. Carlsberg is now one of the biggest brewers in the world.

## close

[warmly] It took a brewer who carried yeast home in a hatbox, and believed the answers were worth sharing.
