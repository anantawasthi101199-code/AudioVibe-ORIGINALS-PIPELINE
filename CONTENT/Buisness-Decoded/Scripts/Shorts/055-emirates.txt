## story

[leaning in] Emirates is one of the biggest international airlines in the world. In 1985 it started with two planes it did not own, and ten million dollars.

[warmly] Back then, Dubai was a small trading city on the coast of the Persian Gulf, in the United Arab Emirates. [serious] Its flights were mostly run by Gulf Air, an airline owned by several of its neighbours. And in the early 1980s, Gulf Air started cutting its flights to Dubai.

[determined] Dubai's ruling family decided it would have its own airline. They gave it ten million dollars to start, and told it there would be no more help from the government. It would have to make its own money. [curious] To run it, they hired an experienced British airline manager, Maurice Flanagan.

[thoughtful] Emirates leased its first two planes from Pakistan's national airline, with Emirates painted on the side. [delighted] On the twenty-fifth of October, 1985, its first flight took off, from Dubai to Karachi, in Pakistan.

[curious] Emirates knew it could never make money from flights in and out of Dubai alone. The city was too small. [pause] But it had something else, which was its place on the map.

## rise

[building] About a third of the world's people live within four hours' flight of Dubai, and about two thirds within eight. [knowingly] So Emirates decided to make Dubai the place where the world changes planes. Fly from London to Dubai, then on to Sydney, or Mumbai, or Lagos. It worked because Dubai sat in the middle, and the government built a huge airport to match.

[with a smile] Emirates bought enormous planes to carry as many people as possible through Dubai. It became the biggest operator in the world of the giant Airbus A380, with showers on board in first class. [quietly impressed] Today Emirates flies to well over a hundred cities, and Dubai's airport is the busiest in the world for international travellers.

## close

[warmly] It took a small city that decided to be the middle of the map.
