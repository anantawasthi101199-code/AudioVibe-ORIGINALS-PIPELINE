## story

[leaning in] Uber is one of the biggest ride companies on Earth, and it owns almost no cars. The idea began with two men standing in the cold in Paris, unable to find a taxi.

[warmly] It was the winter of 2008. Two American tech founders, Garrett Camp and Travis Kalanick, were in Paris for a technology conference. Both had already sold companies and had money to spend. One evening, they stood on a pavement, cold and tired, watching taxi after taxi go past with no light on. [curious] Camp had already been thinking about this problem back home in San Francisco, where taxis were famously hard to find. What if you could press a button on your phone, and a car simply came to you?

[thoughtful] Back then, smartphones were brand new. The first iPhone had come out only the year before. But Camp saw that a phone knows where you are, and so a phone could tell a driver where to go.

[determined] He built the first version with a small team. In 2010 it launched in San Francisco as UberCab. You opened the app, saw a little map with black cars moving around it, tapped once, and a smart black car arrived. You paid through the app, with no cash and no fuss. [with a smile] People loved it. Kalanick soon became the boss and pushed it hard.

[serious] There was a problem, though. Black cars were expensive, and there were only so many of them. [pause] Then, in 2012, Uber made the move that changed everything.

## rise

[building] It launched UberX, which let ordinary people drive their own cars for Uber. Suddenly any car could be a taxi, and that is why it worked: more drivers meant shorter waits, and shorter waits meant more riders. Uber spread city to city, country to country, at astonishing speed.

[grave] It was a bumpy ride. Taxi drivers protested, cities banned it, and in 2017 Kalanick stepped down after a string of scandals. A new boss took over. [quietly impressed] Uber joined the stock market in 2019, added food delivery, and in 2023 made a profit for a full year for the first time. It now works in around seventy countries.

## close

[warmly] It took one cold night and a phone that already knew where you were standing.
