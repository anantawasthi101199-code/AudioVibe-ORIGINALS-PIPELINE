## story

[leaning in] In April 2012, Facebook paid one billion dollars for Instagram. At the time, Instagram had thirteen employees, and had been around for less than two years.

[warmly] It began with Kevin Systrom, a young programmer in San Francisco who had studied at Stanford and worked at Google. [wry] In 2010, he built an app called Burbn, named after his favourite whiskey, bourbon. It let you check in at places, plan meetings with friends, earn points and post photos. It did far too many things. [thoughtful] Hardly anyone used it.

[curious] Kevin and his partner, Mike Krieger, a Brazilian engineer, looked closely at what the few users actually did. They ignored almost everything, except one thing. They shared photos.

[determined] So the two of them threw away almost all of Burbn. They kept only the photos, with comments and likes. [with a smile] On a holiday in Mexico, Kevin's girlfriend told him she would not post her phone photos because they did not look good enough. Phone cameras back then were blurry and dull. So he made a filter, a quick effect that warmed the colours and softened the picture, so even a bad photo looked lovely.

[delighted] They named it Instagram, from instant camera and telegram. It launched on the iPhone on the sixth of October, 2010. Twenty-five thousand people signed up on the first day. [pause] Within two months, a million people had it.

## rise

[building] Instagram was simple: take a photo, add a filter, share it. That is why it worked. Anyone could make something beautiful in seconds, on a phone they already owned. [knowingly] It also grew right as smartphones were spreading across the world.

[serious] Mark Zuckerberg, the head of Facebook, saw it as a threat. Young people were spending their time on Instagram instead. So in 2012 he bought it for a billion dollars, which many people thought was crazy.

[quietly impressed] It turned out to be one of the best deals in business history. Instagram added Stories and short videos, and today more than two billion people use it every month.

## close

[warmly] It took two founders brave enough to throw away almost everything they had built, and keep the one thing people loved.
