## story

[leaning in] In 1961, one of the two brothers who owned a small pizza shop traded his half of the business for a used Volkswagen Beetle. That shop became Domino's, the biggest pizza company in the world.

[softly] The brother who stayed was Tom Monaghan. His father died when he was four, and he spent part of his childhood in an orphanage, and then in foster homes. [determined] As a young man he wanted to go to university, and needed money. So in 1960 he and his brother James borrowed a few hundred dollars and bought a little pizza shop called DomiNick's, in Ypsilanti, a university town in Michigan.

[wry] Neither of them knew much about pizza. James soon decided he had had enough, and swapped his half for the family's Beetle, which they had been using to deliver pizzas. Tom kept the shop. And his university plans quietly faded away.

[thoughtful] He worked enormous hours, and he noticed something. Most of his customers were students, studying late at night in their halls. They did not want to come out. They wanted the pizza to come to them, and fast. [curious] So Tom built everything around delivery. A short menu, so the kitchen never slowed down. Boxes that kept pizza hot. Shops close to the universities and army bases, where hungry young people lived.

[with a smile] In 1965, he renamed the business Domino's. Its logo was a domino with three dots, one for each of his three shops. The plan was to add a dot for every new shop. [pause] That plan did not last long, because the shops began multiplying far too fast.

## rise

[building] In the 1980s Domino's made a famous promise: your pizza in thirty minutes, or it is cheaper. It was dropped in the 1990s, after concerns about drivers rushing. [grave] By 2009 sales were sliding, and customers said the pizza tasted like cardboard. [knowingly] So Domino's did something rare. It ran adverts admitting the pizza was bad, changed the recipe, and showed customers the result. It worked because people trusted a company that told the truth about itself.

[quietly impressed] It also became a technology company, letting customers order online and watch a live tracker of their pizza being made and delivered. Today there are more than twenty thousand Domino's shops around the world.

## close

[warmly] It took a young man who noticed that his customers wanted the pizza to come to them.
