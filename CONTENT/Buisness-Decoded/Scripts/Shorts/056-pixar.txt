## story

[leaning in] Steve Jobs spent years pouring money into a struggling computer company he nearly sold several times. That company made Toy Story.

[warmly] In the late 1970s, the film director George Lucas, who made Star Wars, set up a computer team to explore using computers in films. [curious] It was led by Ed Catmull, a scientist who had dreamed since he was a boy of making a whole cartoon film on a computer. [thoughtful] Soon it hired John Lasseter, a young animator who had just been let go by Disney, partly because he kept pushing the idea of computer animation.

[serious] In 1986, Lucas needed money, and sold the team. The buyer was Steve Jobs, who had just been pushed out of Apple, the company he had started. [knowingly] He named the business Pixar, after the powerful image computer it sold.

[grave] But Pixar's computers were expensive, and few people bought them. It lost money year after year. Jobs kept putting in his own money, millions and millions of dollars, and almost gave up on it.

[with wonder] To show what their computers could do, Lasseter made short cartoon films. One was about a little desk lamp playing with a ball. It was charming, and people loved it, and that little lamp still hops across the start of every Pixar film. [delighted] Another short film won an Oscar. [pause] And then Disney, the company that had let Lasseter go, came calling.

## rise

[building] In 1991, Disney agreed to pay Pixar to make three full-length computer-animated films. The first, Toy Story, came out in 1995. It was the first feature film made entirely on computers, about toys that come alive when no one is watching. [delighted] It was a massive hit. [knowingly] It worked because the computers were only the tools. What audiences fell for was the story and the characters, and Pixar cared about those more than anything.

[quietly impressed] A week later, Pixar went on the stock market, and Steve Jobs became a billionaire. Hit after hit followed. In 2006 Disney bought Pixar for seven point four billion dollars.

## close

[warmly] It took a scientist's childhood dream, an animator who was let go, and a man who refused to stop paying for it.
