## story

[leaning in] In 2018, Uber gave up in South-East Asia and handed its business there to a rival. That rival began as a class project by two students who wanted taxis to be safer.

[warmly] Anthony Tan grew up in Malaysia, in a family that ran a large car company. He was expected to join it one day. Instead, in 2011, he went to study business at Harvard, in America. There he met a classmate, Tan Hooi Ling, also from Malaysia. [serious] They talked about a problem at home. Taxis in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia's capital, had a terrible reputation. Drivers refused trips, overcharged, and some passengers, especially women travelling alone, felt unsafe getting into a stranger's car at night.

[curious] Their idea was a phone app that would match passengers with taxi drivers and show who the driver was before you got in. They entered it in a business competition at Harvard, and it came second. [determined] Anthony went home to build it. In 2012 the app launched in Kuala Lumpur as MyTeksi, using the Malay word for taxi.

[thoughtful] The early days were hard. Many taxi drivers did not own a smartphone. So the team went to taxi queues and helped drivers set them up, one by one, and showed them how the app would bring them more customers.

[building] It spread to the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and beyond, and was renamed Grab. [serious] Then Uber arrived, rich and powerful, and the two fought city by city. [pause] Grab had one big advantage over its giant rival.

## rise

[knowingly] It knew the region. It took cash, because many people there had no bank card. It offered motorbike taxis that weave through traffic jams. It worked because it was built for South-East Asia, rather than copied in from somewhere else. In 2018, Uber sold its South-East Asia business to Grab, taking a share in the company in return.

[quietly impressed] Grab added food delivery, parcels and payments, and joined the American stock market in 2021. Today it is the app millions of people across eight countries use every day.

## close

[warmly] It took two classmates who started with one question: how do we get people home safely?
