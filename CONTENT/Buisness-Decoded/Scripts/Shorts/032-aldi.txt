## story

[leaning in] There are two Aldis, not one. They have different owners, different logos and different countries. And they were split by two brothers who could not agree on cigarettes.

[warmly] Karl and Theo Albrecht grew up in Essen, a coal-mining city in western Germany. Their father was a miner, and their mother ran a small grocery shop from the front of their home. [serious] Both brothers served as soldiers in the Second World War. When they came home in 1946, Germany was in ruins, and people had almost no money.

[curious] The brothers took over their mother's shop, and they had a very clear idea. People need food, and they need it cheap. [thoughtful] So they stocked only the most basic things, a few hundred products where other shops had thousands. No fancy displays. Goods were sold straight from the boxes they arrived in. No advertising. Every penny they saved, they knocked off the price.

[delighted] Customers loved it. Shoppers who were counting every coin queued up, and the brothers opened another shop, and another. By 1960 they had about three hundred.

[thoughtful] But they were very different men, and they started to argue. [pause] The final argument was about whether their shops should sell cigarettes.

## rise

[serious] Karl said no. He thought cigarettes would be stolen, and attract shoplifters. Theo said yes. [knowingly] So in 1961, they divided the business. Theo took the shops in the north of Germany, which became Aldi Nord. Karl took the south, which became Aldi Süd. The name Aldi comes from Albrecht Discount.

[building] Each half grew enormously, and they carved up the world between them. Aldi Süd runs the shops in Britain, America and Australia. Aldi Nord runs them in much of Europe, and also owns the American grocery chain Trader Joe's. [knowingly] It worked because they never stopped doing the same simple thing: a small range, sold very cheaply, with no frills at all.

[quietly impressed] Both brothers became among the richest people in Germany, and today the two Aldis have more than ten thousand shops between them.

## close

[warmly] It took two brothers who understood that poor customers want the essentials, cheap, and nothing else.
