## story

[leaning in] In almost every country in the world, McDonald's is the biggest burger chain. In the Philippines, it is not. A smiling red bee called Jollibee is. And it all began with an ice cream parlour.

[warmly] In 1975, a young man called Tony Tan Caktiong opened a small ice cream shop in Cubao, a busy part of Manila, the capital of the Philippines. His family were Chinese immigrants, and they helped him run it. [curious] But the customers kept asking for something else. Hot food. Sandwiches. Burgers. So Tony added a few hot dishes to the menu.

[delighted] They sold so fast that the hot food soon became the main business. In 1978 Tony renamed the shop Jollibee. He liked the bee because bees are busy and hard-working, and he wanted the shop to feel jolly too.

[serious] Then, in 1981, McDonald's arrived in the Philippines. It was rich, famous and experienced. People thought it would crush little local shops like Jollibee. [thoughtful] Tony even considered selling, or partnering with a big American brand. [pause] Instead, he decided to compete with something McDonald's did not have.

## rise

[building] He made food that tasted like home. Jollibee's burger was a little sweeter, the way Filipinos liked it. Its spaghetti had a sweet tomato sauce with slices of hot dog and grated cheese, just like at a Filipino children's party. And its crispy fried chicken, called Chickenjoy, came with rice and gravy, because in the Philippines, a meal without rice hardly counts as a meal. [knowingly] It worked because Jollibee understood its customers' taste better than any foreign company could.

[delighted] Families flocked to it. Children loved the cheerful bee mascot. Jollibee became the biggest fast-food chain in the Philippines, and stayed there.

[quietly impressed] Tony's company went on to buy other restaurant brands, and opened Jollibee restaurants abroad, in places where many Filipinos live, from Dubai to London. People queue around the block when a new one opens.

## close

[warmly] It took a young man who listened when his customers asked for something he did not sell yet.
