## story

[leaning in] About three billion people use Facebook every month. In February 2004, it was a website for students at one university, built by a nineteen-year-old in his bedroom.

[warmly] The student was Mark Zuckerberg, studying at Harvard University, in America. He loved building things on the computer. [wry] Earlier that year, one of his projects, a joke website that compared students' photos, got him into real trouble with the university. But it also showed him something. Students were desperate to see who else was at their college.

[curious] Back then, Harvard gave new students a printed book of faces, a directory with every student's photo and name. Zuckerberg wanted to put that online, and let students add more themselves: their interests, their friends, their classes. [determined] With a few friends from his dormitory, he built it in a couple of weeks. On the fourth of February, 2004, TheFacebook went live.

[delighted] Within a month, around half of Harvard's students had signed up. Students at other universities heard about it and begged to join. [building] Zuckerberg opened it to Stanford, Columbia, Yale, and then dozens more. That summer he moved to California, into a rented house, and the team worked day and night to keep the site running.

[thoughtful] In 2005 they dropped the word The, and it became just Facebook. In 2006, Yahoo, then one of the biggest internet companies, offered about a billion dollars to buy it. [pause] Zuckerberg was twenty-two years old. And he said no.

## rise

[building] Instead, he opened Facebook to anyone over thirteen with an email address. He added the News Feed, a constant stream of what your friends were doing. People complained at first, then could not stop scrolling. [knowingly] It worked because it was built around real names and real friends, so the people you cared about were already there.

[serious] It went on the stock market in 2012, and bought Instagram and WhatsApp. It also faced fierce criticism over privacy, and over how it handled people's data. [quietly impressed] In 2021 the company renamed itself Meta, and its apps are used by billions of people every day.

## close

[warmly] It took a student who understood that people mostly want to know what their friends are up to.
