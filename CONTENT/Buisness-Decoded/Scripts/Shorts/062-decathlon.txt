## story

[leaning in] Decathlon is the biggest sports shop in the world. It began with one huge store in a car park outside Lille, in northern France, and one simple idea.

[warmly] In 1976, a young man called Michel Leclercq opened it. He loved sport, and he came from a family that knew retail well: his cousins had built some of France's biggest supermarket chains. [curious] Back then, buying sports gear meant going to small, specialist shops. One for football. Another for tennis. Another for fishing. Everything was expensive, and you often needed to be an expert just to walk in.

[determined] Leclercq wanted the opposite. One enormous shop, with equipment for every sport you could think of, from cycling to diving to horse riding, all under one roof, at low prices, for ordinary families who just wanted to play. [with a smile] He called it Decathlon, after the athletics contest with ten different events.

[delighted] It was busy from the start. Families came to buy football boots and left with a tent and a badminton set too.

[thoughtful] But he had a problem. He was selling other companies' brands, and they decided his prices. [pause] So Decathlon made a move that changed everything.

## rise

[building] It started designing and making its own gear. Each sport got its own Decathlon brand, run by people who did that sport themselves. Hiking and camping got Quechua. Running got Kalenji. Water sports got their own too. [delighted] One of Quechua's tents became famous because you could throw it into the air, and it popped up, ready, in two seconds. [knowingly] It worked because making its own products let Decathlon keep prices low while still controlling quality, and sell gear that beginners could afford and understand.

[quietly impressed] Today Decathlon has well over a thousand shops in dozens of countries, and it is still owned by the family that started it.

## close

[warmly] It took one man who thought sport should be cheap, simple and for everyone.
