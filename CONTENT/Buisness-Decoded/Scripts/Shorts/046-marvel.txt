## story

[leaning in] In 1996 Marvel, the home of Spider-Man and Captain America, went bankrupt. Twelve years later, it began making some of the most successful films of all time.

[warmly] Marvel started in 1939 as a small comic book publisher in New York. [curious] But its golden moment came in the 1960s, when a writer called Stan Lee and an artist called Jack Kirby began creating a new kind of superhero. [thoughtful] Most comic heroes back then were perfect. Marvel's heroes had problems. The Fantastic Four argued like a real family. Spider-Man was a nervous teenager who could not pay his rent. The Hulk could not control his temper. [delighted] Readers loved them, because they felt real.

[serious] For decades Marvel sold comics by the million. But in the 1990s, collectors stopped buying, and the company had huge debts. In 1996, it went bankrupt. [grave] To survive, it sold the film rights to its most famous characters, like Spider-Man and the X-Men, to big studios.

[thoughtful] Marvel watched those studios make films with its heroes, and keep most of the money. [determined] So it made a bold decision. It would make its own films. [pause] But it had no money to do it.

## rise

[building] In 2005, Marvel borrowed over half a billion dollars from a bank. And it offered its own remaining characters, like Captain America and Thor, as security. If the films failed, the bank would own the heroes. [knowingly] Its first film was Iron Man, a less famous hero, starring an actor many thought was a risky choice. It came out in 2008, and it was a huge hit.

[delighted] Then Marvel did something no studio had done. It linked its films together, so characters appeared in each other's stories, building towards a team-up called The Avengers. It worked because every film made you want to see the next one.

[quietly impressed] In 2009 Disney bought Marvel for four billion dollars. In 2019, Avengers Endgame became one of the highest-earning films ever made.

## close

[warmly] It took heroes with real problems, and a company willing to bet those heroes on itself.
