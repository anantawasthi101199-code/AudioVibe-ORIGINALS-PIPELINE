## story

[leaning in] On the evening of the eighth of November, 2016, India's prime minister went on television and announced that from midnight, most of the country's banknotes would no longer be money. Overnight, a phone payment app called Paytm became one of the most used apps in India.

[warmly] Paytm was built by Vijay Shekhar Sharma. He grew up in Aligarh, a small city in northern India, where his father was a schoolteacher. [softly] He was clever, and went to engineering college in Delhi at fifteen. But the lessons were in English, and he had studied in Hindi. He struggled badly at first, and taught himself English by reading magazines and books side by side with dictionaries.

[determined] In 2000 he started a company that sold content for mobile phones, like cricket scores and ringtones. Then, in 2010, he launched Paytm, short for pay through mobile. At first it let people top up their phone credit from an app. [curious] In 2014, it became a digital wallet. You loaded money into the app, and could pay shops, taxis and bills by scanning a QR code, a little square barcode, with your phone.

[thoughtful] Most Indians still paid with cash, though. [pause] And then came that November night.

## rise

[serious] The banned notes made up most of the cash in the country. Queues outside banks stretched for hours. Shops had no change. [building] The next morning, Paytm placed adverts in newspapers across India. Shopkeepers stuck its QR code on their counters. Tea stalls, vegetable sellers and rickshaw drivers started accepting payments by phone. Millions of new users joined within weeks. [knowingly] It worked because Paytm had already spent years getting shops ready, so when cash ran out, it was there.

[serious] It has had hard times since. Its shares fell on their first day on the stock market in 2021, and in 2024 regulators ordered its payments bank to stop taking new deposits. [quietly impressed] But Paytm helped change how India pays. Today, even small street stalls take payments by phone.

## close

[warmly] It took a teacher's son who was ready the morning a whole country suddenly needed him.
