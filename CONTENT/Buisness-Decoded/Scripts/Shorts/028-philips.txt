## story

[leaning in] The cassette tape and the compact disc both came out of a company that started by making light bulbs, in a small Dutch town.

[warmly] In 1891, electric light was still new and exciting. Most homes were lit by candles, oil lamps or gas. [curious] A young Dutch engineer called Gerard Philips was fascinated by electric light bulbs. His father, Frederik, was a businessman with money to invest. Together, they bought an empty factory building in Eindhoven, then a small town in the south of the Netherlands, and started making bulbs.

[serious] It did not go well at first. There were many bulb makers in Europe, and the company lost money. [determined] Then Gerard's younger brother Anton joined. Gerard was the engineer, but Anton was a born salesman. He travelled across Europe, selling bulbs to anyone who would listen. [delighted] The story goes that he won a huge order to light up a palace of the Russian tsar, and it put the company on the map.

[thoughtful] The brothers made a team. Gerard made the bulbs better and cheaper. Anton sold them everywhere. Eindhoven grew around the factory, with houses, schools, and even a football club for the workers, which became PSV Eindhoven, one of the famous Dutch football teams. [pause] Then the company did something that changed its future.

## rise

[building] In 1914 it built a research laboratory, filled with scientists. From there came radios, and electric shavers, and in 1963, the compact cassette, a little tape that could record and play music anywhere. [with a smile] In 1982, working with Sony from Japan, Philips launched the compact disc, the CD. [knowingly] It worked because the company never stopped inventing. It kept a lab of people whose job was to think about what came next.

[quietly impressed] Philips later sold its lighting and television businesses, and today it makes medical technology, like hospital scanners. And Eindhoven is still known as the City of Light.

## close

[warmly] It took one brother who could make things, and another who could sell them.
