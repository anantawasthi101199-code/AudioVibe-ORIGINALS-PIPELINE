## story

[leaning in] Ferrari sells only a few thousand cars a year, fewer than many carmakers build in a single week. And yet it is one of the most valuable car companies in the world. It all started with a man who did not really want to sell cars at all.

[warmly] His name was Enzo Ferrari. He was born in 1898 near Modena, in northern Italy. When he was ten, his father took him to a motor race, and the noise and speed of it never left him. [curious] After serving in the First World War, he became a racing driver for Alfa Romeo, one of Italy's great car brands. Then, in 1929, he set up his own racing team, Scuderia Ferrari, which raced Alfa Romeo cars. Scuderia simply means stable, as in a stable of horses.

[thoughtful] His badge became a black prancing horse on a yellow shield. The horse had been painted on the plane of a famous Italian fighter pilot, and the pilot's mother gave Enzo permission to use it for luck. The yellow was the colour of Modena.

[serious] Then Enzo fell out with Alfa Romeo and left. During the Second World War his factory was bombed. [determined] But in 1947, the first car carrying the Ferrari name rolled out of his new factory in Maranello. It was red, loud and very fast.

[thoughtful] And here was the problem. Racing cost a fortune. [pause] So Enzo made a decision that shaped the company forever.

## rise

[building] He would sell road cars to rich drivers, and use the money to go racing. Owning a Ferrari meant owning a piece of the team that won on the track. [knowingly] And he kept the numbers small on purpose. Enzo liked to say he would always build one car fewer than the market wanted. Scarcity made people want them even more, and that is why it worked.

[with a smile] In the 1960s, the American car giant Ford tried to buy Ferrari. Enzo walked away from the deal at the last moment, and Ford spent years trying to beat him at the famous Le Mans race. Ferrari joined the stock market in 2015, and today its cars often have waiting lists stretching for years.

## close

[warmly] It took a man who loved racing so much he built a car company to pay for it.
