## story

[leaning in] Most clothing brands take months to get a new design from a sketch into a shop. Zara can do it in a few weeks. That speed made a shop boy from northern Spain one of the richest people in the world.

[warmly] His name is Amancio Ortega. He was born in 1936 into a poor family in Spain. His father worked on the railways, and money was always short. At fourteen he left school and took a job as a delivery boy for a shirt maker in A Coruña, a windy port city on Spain's north-west coast. [curious] He carried parcels, learned to cut cloth, and watched which clothes people bought and which sat on the shelf.

[thoughtful] He noticed something. Fashionable clothes were expensive because they passed through so many hands: designers, factories, wholesalers, shops, each taking a cut, each adding weeks. Ortega wanted to make good-looking clothes himself and sell them himself.

[determined] In 1963 he started a small workshop with his then wife, Rosalía Mera, making quilted bathrobes and nightwear. They sewed at home at first, on the kitchen table, then hired local women to help. Then, in 1975, they opened their own shop on a busy street in A Coruña. [with a smile] They wanted to call it Zorba, after a popular film, but a bar nearby already had the name. So they rearranged the letters they had already ordered for the sign. [pause] And the shop became Zara.

## rise

[building] Here is the move that made it. Zara made most of its clothes close to home, in Spain and nearby countries, in small batches. Shop managers reported every day what customers asked for, and the designers back at headquarters drew it, made it and shipped it within weeks. New clothes arrived in the shops twice a week. [knowingly] If you liked something, you had to buy it now, because next week it might be gone. That feeling kept people coming back again and again.

[quietly impressed] Ortega's company, Inditex, now owns brands like Zara, Massimo Dutti and Bershka, and has thousands of shops around the world. It is the biggest fashion retailer on Earth.

## close

[warmly] It took a delivery boy who watched what people actually bought, and refused to wait months to give it to them.
