## story

[leaning in] One of India's biggest technology companies started out selling cooking oil. And the man who changed it was just twenty-one, and had never run a business in his life.

[warmly] In 1945, a businessman in western India started a company called Western India Vegetable Products. It made vegetable oil for cooking, from a factory in a small town called Amalner. [softly] In 1966, his son Azim was studying engineering at Stanford University in California. Then a phone call came. His father had died suddenly. [serious] Azim flew home to run the company.

[thoughtful] Many people doubted him. At the first big meeting, one shareholder even told him to sell the business to someone more experienced. Azim refused. He was quiet and careful, but determined. [determined] He began to grow the company beyond oil, into soap, lighting, and parts for machines.

[curious] Then, in 1977, something happened that changed his path. Under new rules, the American computer giant IBM pulled out of India. [thoughtful] Suddenly, Indian businesses had nowhere to buy computers. Azim saw a gap. [pause] So the cooking oil company started making computers.

## rise

[building] Wipro, as it was now called, built its own computers for Indian offices. Then it moved into software, and into running technology for companies abroad. In the 1990s, as big foreign companies looked to India for skilled, affordable software engineers, Wipro was ready. [knowingly] It worked because Azim Premji kept moving to where the next opportunity was, and built a company that was trusted to do careful work.

[quietly impressed] Wipro became one of India's largest IT companies, employing hundreds of thousands of people around the world.

[warmly] And Azim Premji did two more remarkable things. More than thirty years after leaving Stanford, he finished his degree. And he gave away a huge share of his fortune, billions of dollars, to a foundation that supports schools in India, making him one of the most generous givers in the world.

## close

[warmly] It took a young man called home too early, who kept looking for the next opportunity, and then gave much of it away.
