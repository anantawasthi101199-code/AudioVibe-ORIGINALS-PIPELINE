## story

[leaning in] In Kenya, a farmer can receive money from her son in the city in seconds, on a basic mobile phone with no internet. It began as a small trial that nobody expected to change a country.

[warmly] In the early 2000s, most Kenyans had no bank account. Banks were far away, and expensive. [serious] Many young people moved to the capital, Nairobi, to work, and they wanted to send money home to their families in the villages. So they gave cash to bus drivers, or friends travelling home, or carried it themselves on long journeys. Money got lost, or stolen.

[curious] But almost everyone was getting a mobile phone. [thoughtful] Vodafone, a big British phone company, and its Kenyan partner, Safaricom, with some funding from the British government, set up a trial. The idea was to let people repay small business loans using their phones.

[delighted] Something unexpected happened. The people in the trial started using it to send money to each other. A man in the city would pay money into the phone system, and his wife in the village would take it out as cash at a local shop.

[determined] So in 2007, Safaricom launched it properly, and called it M-Pesa. M for mobile, and pesa, the Swahili word for money. [pause] And it built the one thing that made it work for everyone.

## rise

[building] A network of agents, ordinary small shops all over Kenya, where you could turn cash into phone money, and phone money back into cash. You just sent a text message to pay someone. [knowingly] It worked because it did not need a bank, a smartphone or the internet. Only a basic phone, and a shop down the road.

[delighted] Millions joined within the first year. People paid bills, school fees and shopkeepers with it.

[quietly impressed] Researchers later estimated that M-Pesa helped lift a couple of hundred thousand Kenyan households out of extreme poverty, by making it easier to save and share money. Today most adults in Kenya use it, and it has spread to other countries in Africa.

## close

[warmly] It took a phone company that noticed what people were really doing with its trial, and built it for them.
