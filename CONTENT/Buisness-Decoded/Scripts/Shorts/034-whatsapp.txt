## story

[leaning in] In 2014, Facebook paid nineteen billion dollars for WhatsApp, an app built by about fifty people. One of its founders signed the deal outside the very building where he used to queue for food handouts.

[warmly] His name is Jan Koum. He grew up in a small village outside Kyiv, in Ukraine, in a house with no hot water. [softly] When he was sixteen, he and his mother moved to California. They were so poor they relied on government food stamps, and his mother worked as a babysitter. Jan swept floors in a grocery shop. And in the evenings he taught himself computer networking from second-hand books.

[curious] He became a good engineer, and got a job at Yahoo, then one of the biggest internet companies. There he became friends with Brian Acton, another engineer. [thoughtful] In 2009, after both had left Yahoo, Jan bought an iPhone. And he had an idea. What if, next to every name in your phone's contacts, you could see a little status? Busy. At the gym. Battery low.

[determined] He built it, and called it WhatsApp, because it sounded like what's up. [with a smile] Then Apple let apps send notifications. Suddenly, people started using their statuses to send each other messages. [pause] Jan realised what he really had.

## rise

[building] A free way to send messages over the internet. At the time, text messages cost money, especially between countries. With WhatsApp, you could chat with family on the other side of the world for nothing. Brian joined, and they had one strict rule, written on a note on Jan's desk: no ads, no games, no gimmicks. [knowingly] It worked because it was simple, fast and private, and it worked on almost any phone. Hundreds of millions of people joined, mostly by word of mouth.

[quietly impressed] In 2014, Facebook bought it. Jan Koum signed the papers leaning against the door of the old welfare office in California, where he had queued for food stamps as a teenager. Today more than two billion people use WhatsApp.

## close

[warmly] It took a boy who knew what it cost to call home, and made sure it cost nothing.
