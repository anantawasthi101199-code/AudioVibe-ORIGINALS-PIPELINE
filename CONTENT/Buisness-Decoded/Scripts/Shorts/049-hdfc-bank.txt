## story

[leaning in] India's biggest private bank grew by being careful, steady, and on purpose, a little boring. And it began with a sixty-six-year-old who wanted ordinary Indians to own their homes.

[warmly] In the 1970s, buying a house in India was almost impossible for ordinary families. Banks rarely lent money for homes. People saved for most of their working lives, and often bought only when they retired. [thoughtful] A veteran banker called H.T. Parekh thought that was wrong. In 1977, at an age when most people retire, he started India's first company that only gave home loans, called HDFC.

[serious] People said Indians would never repay home loans. [delighted] They did. HDFC lent carefully, checked every borrower, and grew steadily, helping hundreds of thousands of families buy houses.

[curious] Then, in the 1990s, India opened its economy. For the first time in decades, the government allowed new private banks to be set up. [determined] In 1994, HDFC started one: HDFC Bank. To run it, it brought home an Indian banker called Aditya Puri, who had been working for the American bank Citibank in Malaysia. [pause] Puri had a very clear idea of what kind of bank it would be.

## rise

[firmly] It would not chase risky loans to grow fast. It would check every customer carefully, use new technology from the start, and open branches in small towns that big banks ignored. [knowingly] It worked because while other banks grew fast and then got into trouble with loans that were never repaid, HDFC Bank just kept growing, quarter after quarter, with very few bad loans.

[quietly impressed] Aditya Puri ran it for twenty-six years. It became the largest private bank in India. In 2023, it merged with its parent company HDFC, the home loan pioneer, and became one of the most valuable banks in the world.

## close

[warmly] It took a banker who thought families deserved homes, and another who believed careful could beat fast.
