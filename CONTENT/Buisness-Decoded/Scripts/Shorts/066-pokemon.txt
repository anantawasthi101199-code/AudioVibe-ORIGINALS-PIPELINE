## story

[leaning in] The biggest media franchise in the world, bigger than Star Wars or Mickey Mouse by most counts, started with a boy catching beetles in the fields outside Tokyo.

[warmly] Satoshi Tajiri was born in 1965 and grew up in Machida, a town on the edge of Tokyo that still had ponds, woods and rice fields. [curious] He spent his childhood hunting insects, beetles, crayfish and dragonflies, keeping them in jars, studying them, and trading them with friends. The other children called him Doctor Bug.

[thoughtful] Then the city grew. The fields were paved over and the ponds became car parks, and the insects went with them. Tajiri found a new love instead, video games in arcades. [with a smile] As a teenager he wrote and printed a homemade magazine about games, called Game Freak, and a young artist friend drew the pictures. In 1989 the magazine became a small company that made games.

[curious] That same year, Nintendo released the Game Boy, a handheld games machine. It had a little cable that could link two Game Boys together. [a beat] Tajiri looked at that cable, and pictured insects crawling along it, from one player's machine to the other.

[determined] So he set out to make a game about catching creatures and trading them, the way he had swapped beetles as a boy. [softly] It took six years. The team ran short of money, some people left, and Tajiri sometimes worked through the night for days.

## rise

[building] Pokémon Red and Pokémon Green came out in Japan in February 1996. There were a hundred and fifty-one creatures, and no single copy of the game could catch them all, so you had to trade with friends. [knowingly] It worked because it turned a game into something you did with other people, in the playground, with the cable.

[delighted] A trading card game followed that same year, then a cartoon in 1997, and then the whole world caught it. In 2016, Pokémon Go put the creatures on phones, and sent millions of people outside, walking through real parks to find them.

## close

[warmly] It took a boy who lost his fields to the city, and found a way to give every child a pocket full of creatures to catch.
