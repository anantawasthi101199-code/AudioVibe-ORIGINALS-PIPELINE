## story

[leaning in] Somewhere in the world, a jar of Nutella is opened every couple of seconds. It exists because, after the Second World War, Italy ran short of chocolate.

[warmly] The story starts in Alba, a small town in the hills of Piedmont, in northern Italy. Pietro Ferrero ran a pastry shop there. [serious] After the war, the country was poor, and cocoa, the bean chocolate is made from, was rare and very expensive. Most families could not afford chocolate at all.

[curious] But the hills around Alba were covered in hazelnut trees. Hazelnuts were cheap and plentiful. [thoughtful] So, in 1946, Pietro mixed roasted hazelnuts with sugar and a little cocoa, and made a sweet paste that set into a solid loaf, wrapped in paper. Shopkeepers cut slices off it, and children ate it on bread. It tasted like chocolate, and it cost a fraction of the price.

[with a smile] He called it Giandujot. And it sold very well. Mothers could give their children a treat, and it was cheaper than jam.

[determined] A few years later, the family made a softer version that could be spread straight from a jar. Pietro's son, Michele, took over the business, and he had big ambitions. [pause] In 1964, he gave the spread a new name, one that would work in any language.

## rise

[building] He called it Nutella, from the English word nut, with a soft Italian ending. The first jar came out of the factory in Alba in April 1964, and soon it was on breakfast tables across Europe. [knowingly] It worked because it took a simple, comforting taste and made it easy: open the jar, spread, done.

[quietly impressed] Michele kept inventing. Kinder chocolate for children, Tic Tac mints, and the gold-wrapped Ferrero Rocher. The family kept the company private and famously secretive, and Michele became the richest man in Italy.

[warmly] Today his son Giovanni runs it. Ferrero is one of the world's biggest chocolate makers, and it buys a large share of all the hazelnuts grown on the planet.

## close

[warmly] It took a pastry maker who could not get enough chocolate, and used the hazelnuts growing on the hills outside.
