## story

[leaning in] Costco charges you a fee every year just to be allowed in the door. Then it sells you a hot dog and a drink for a dollar fifty, the same price it was in 1985. And millions of people love it.

[warmly] The idea began with an older businessman called Sol Price. In 1976, in San Diego, California, he opened a giant shop in an old aircraft hangar, and called it Price Club. It was built for small business owners. They paid a yearly fee to become members, and in return they could buy goods in bulk, at prices close to what shops themselves paid. [curious] One of the people who worked for him was Jim Sinegal, who had started out as a teenager unloading mattresses.

[determined] In 1983, Sinegal and a partner, Jeffrey Brotman, opened their own version in Seattle. They called it Costco. Ten years later, it merged with the business Sol Price had started.

[thoughtful] Here is how it works. Walk into a normal supermarket and there might be dozens of kinds of tomato ketchup. At Costco, there might be two. Costco sells only around four thousand different products, a fraction of a supermarket's range. But it sells each one in enormous amounts, at very small profit.

[knowingly] And it has a strict rule. It adds only a small markup on most of what it sells. So where does Costco make its real money? [pause] From the membership fee.

## rise

[building] Every member pays every year, whether they shop once or every week. That fee lets Costco keep its prices so low that people feel silly shopping anywhere else, and they keep renewing. [with a smile] The famous hot dog deal is part of that. It says, you can trust us. When the man who took over from Sinegal suggested raising the hot dog price, the story goes that Sinegal told him he would kill him if he did. The price stayed.

[quietly impressed] Costco also pays its staff well, and keeps them for years. Today it has hundreds of warehouses across the world, and tens of millions of paying members, almost all of whom renew every year.

## close

[warmly] It took a simple bargain: pay a little to come in, and pay less for everything after that.
