## story

[leaning in] More than four hundred billion LEGO bricks have been made, and every one of them still clicks onto the first ones made in 1958. But in 2003, the company that makes them was close to collapse.

[warmly] The story starts with a carpenter called Ole Kirk Christiansen, in a small village in Denmark called Billund. In the early 1930s the world was in a deep slump, the Great Depression. Farmers stopped ordering furniture, and Ole Kirk's workshop had no work. [curious] So he began making wooden toys from his leftover wood: little ducks on wheels, toy trucks, yo-yos. In 1934 he named his company LEGO, from two Danish words, leg godt, which mean play well.

[thoughtful] After the Second World War, he bought a machine that moulded plastic, which was expensive and unusual. He began making plastic bricks that stacked together. [pause] Most shops thought plastic toys would never catch on.

[determined] His son kept improving the brick. In 1958 the family patented the design still used today: a brick with studs on top and hollow tubes inside, so the pieces gripped each other firmly. Two years later, a fire burned down the warehouse that stored the wooden toys. [softly] The family decided to stop making them, and focus everything on the brick.

[delighted] It became one of the most loved toys in the world. [serious] But by the early 2000s, LEGO had lost its way. It had launched theme parks, clothes, video games, and so many new pieces that it lost track of what it was. [grave] In 2003 it lost a huge amount of money. Bankers worried it might not survive.

## rise

[building] In 2004 a young new boss took over, a former management consultant. He cut away much of what was not working, sold the theme parks, and brought the company back to its heart: the brick, and the fun of building. [knowingly] It worked because it stopped trying to be everything, and remembered what children actually loved.

[quietly impressed] Star Wars sets, The LEGO Movie and grown-up collectors followed. Within ten years LEGO had become the biggest toy company in the world.

## close

[warmly] It took a carpenter with no customers, a better brick, and a family brave enough to go back to the brick when everything else failed.
