## story

[leaning in] Nvidia, one of the most valuable companies in the world, was planned in a booth at a roadside diner, by three engineers who wanted to make video games look better.

[warmly] The diner was a Denny's in San Jose, California, in 1993. One of the three was Jensen Huang, who had been born in Taiwan, moved to America as a boy, and worked at a Denny's himself as a teenager, washing dishes. The other two, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, were engineers like him.

[curious] They saw that computer games were getting more ambitious, with three-dimensional worlds full of detail. But the main chip inside a computer, the processor, was not good at drawing those pictures. It does tasks one after another, very quickly. [thoughtful] Drawing a 3D scene means doing millions of small calculations all at once, for every point of light on the screen. So they decided to build a special chip that did only that: a graphics chip.

[grave] Their first chip, in 1995, flopped. Nvidia came within weeks of running out of money. [determined] They bet everything on the next one, and in 1997 it was a hit. Then, in 1999, they launched the GeForce, and called it the world's first GPU, a graphics processing unit. Gamers loved it.

[thoughtful] And then Jensen Huang made a long, expensive bet that many investors did not understand. [pause] He decided that his gaming chips should be able to do far more than games.

## rise

[building] In 2006 Nvidia released software called CUDA, which let scientists use graphics chips for any calculation that needed millions of small sums at once. [with wonder] In 2012, a team of researchers trained a new kind of artificial intelligence on two Nvidia gaming cards, and it beat every other computer at recognising pictures. [knowingly] It worked because AI turned out to need exactly what games needed: enormous numbers of small calculations, done side by side.

[quietly impressed] When AI chatbots took off at the end of 2022, every big technology company needed Nvidia's chips, and it became one of the most valuable companies on Earth.

## close

[warmly] It took three engineers in a diner, and a bet that chips built for games could do much more.
