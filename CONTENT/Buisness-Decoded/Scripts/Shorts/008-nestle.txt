## story

[leaning in] Nestlé sells food in almost every country on Earth: coffee, chocolate, baby food, pet food, bottled water. It began with one chemist in a lakeside town in Switzerland, trying to keep babies alive.

[warmly] His name was Henri Nestlé. He was born in Germany in 1814, trained as a pharmacist, and moved to Vevey, a small Swiss town on the shores of Lake Geneva. He made all kinds of things in his little workshop: lemonade, fertiliser, lamp oil. [serious] But one problem troubled him above all. In those days many babies died in their first year. Mothers who could not breastfeed had very little safe food to give their children.

[thoughtful] So, in 1867, Henri began experimenting. He mixed cow's milk with baked wheat flour and sugar, and dried it into a powder that could be mixed with water. He called it Farine Lactée, which means milky flour. [softly] He gave it to a premature baby whose mother could not feed him, and the baby lived. Word spread quickly, and soon his baby food was sold across Europe.

[curious] Just along the way, two American brothers had started a rival company in Switzerland, making condensed milk, which is milk boiled down thick and sweet, sold in tins so it would keep. For years the two companies fought for the same customers. [pause] Then they did something sensible.

## rise

[building] In 1905 they merged, and became one company. From there it grew and grew. In the 1930s Brazil had far too much coffee and asked Nestlé for help. Its scientists invented a soluble coffee powder: add hot water, and you have a cup. It was called Nescafé, and it went everywhere. [knowingly] The pattern stayed the same. Take food people already loved, make it easy to keep and easy to use, and sell it everywhere.

[serious] It has had dark chapters too. In the 1970s, people boycotted Nestlé over how it marketed baby formula in poorer countries. [quietly impressed] Today it is the biggest food company in the world, with brands like KitKat, Nespresso and Maggi.

## close

[warmly] It took a chemist trying to solve one hard problem, feeding babies who had nothing else.
