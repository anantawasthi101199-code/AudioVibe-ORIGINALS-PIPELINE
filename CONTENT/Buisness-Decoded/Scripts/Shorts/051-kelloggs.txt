## story

[leaning in] The cornflakes in a breakfast bowl were invented by accident, at a health resort, by two brothers who would end up fighting over them for years.

[warmly] In the 1890s, in a town called Battle Creek, in Michigan, there was a huge health resort called the Sanitarium. It was run by a famous doctor, John Harvey Kellogg. [curious] He believed that plain, simple food was the key to good health. His patients ate no meat, and very bland meals. His younger brother, Will Kellogg, worked for him for years, keeping the books and doing every odd job the doctor gave him.

[thoughtful] The brothers were trying to make a healthier kind of bread. One day, they boiled some wheat and then left it sitting out, because they were called away. [with a smile] When they came back, it had gone stale. They rolled it out anyway, and instead of one long sheet, it broke into thin flakes. They toasted them.

[delighted] The patients loved them. A crunchy breakfast, ready to eat. The brothers tried other grains, and in 1898 they made flakes from corn. [serious] Then they began to argue. [knowingly] Will wanted to add sugar, to make the flakes taste better, so ordinary families would buy them in shops. John refused. Sugar was unhealthy, and the flakes were meant for his patients. [pause] So in 1906, Will left, and started his own company.

## rise

[building] He called it the Battle Creek Toasted Corn Flake Company. He added a little sugar and malt, and spent a fortune on advertising, at a time when few food companies did. He handed out free samples, and printed his own signature on every box, so people knew it was the real thing. [knowingly] It worked because Will turned a health food into something easy and tasty that every family could buy.

[serious] The brothers fought in court for years over who could use the family name. Will won. [quietly impressed] His company, Kellogg's, became one of the biggest food companies in the world, adding Rice Krispies and many more. He gave much of his fortune to a foundation for children's health and education.

## close

[warmly] It took a batch of stale wheat, and a younger brother who wanted everybody to enjoy it.
