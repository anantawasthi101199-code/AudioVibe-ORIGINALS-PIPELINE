## story

[leaning in] Walk into a Sephora shop and you can try almost anything you like, on your own, with nobody pushing you to buy. In 1969, that was a strange and new idea.

[warmly] Back then, buying perfume or make-up in France meant going to a counter. The products sat behind glass. A sales assistant stood guard, and often worked for one brand, pushing whatever she was paid to sell. [serious] If you wanted to smell a perfume, you had to ask. It could feel awkward, even a little intimidating.

[curious] A young Frenchman called Dominique Mandonnaud thought there was a better way. In 1969, in the city of Limoges, in central France, he opened a small perfume shop with a different rule. [delighted] Customers could walk in, pick up any bottle, spray it, smell it, and put it back. No pressure. Staff were there to help if you asked, and only if you asked.

[thoughtful] French shoppers loved it. People stayed longer, tried more, and bought more. Mandonnaud opened more shops across France, under his own brand. [determined] Then, in 1993, he bought a rival chain of perfume shops called Sephora, and joined the two together under that name. [with a smile] The word came from the Greek for beautiful. [pause] Four years later, a giant came knocking.

## rise

[building] In 1997, LVMH, the luxury group that owns Louis Vuitton and Dior, bought Sephora. With their money, Sephora spread across the world, opening in New York in 1998. Its shops had black and white stripes, long open aisles, and walls of products from hundreds of brands, from famous names to tiny new ones. [knowingly] It worked because it let customers explore and play, and treated buying make-up like fun instead of a test.

[quietly impressed] Sephora added a loyalty club, and online shopping, and became the biggest beauty shop chain in the world, with thousands of shops in dozens of countries.

## close

[warmly] It took one man who took the glass away from the counter, and let people try for themselves.
