## story

[leaning in] Royal Enfield is the oldest motorcycle brand in the world still being made. It started in England. Today it is Indian, and it nearly died twice.

[warmly] The company began in Redditch, an English town, making bicycles and then rifle parts for the British government. That is why its old slogan said: made like a gun. [curious] It built its first motorcycle in 1901, and in 1932 it made the Bullet, a strong, simple machine with a thumping engine.

[thoughtful] After India became independent in 1947, its army needed tough motorbikes to patrol rough mountain borders. It ordered hundreds of Bullets from England. [determined] Then, in 1955, an Indian company and Royal Enfield set up a factory in Madras, now Chennai, to build Bullets in India.

[grave] In England, the original company struggled, and in 1970 it closed. [with a smile] But in Madras, the factory kept going, making the same 1950s Bullet, year after year, almost unchanged. Indian riders loved its heavy thump. Soldiers and police rode it. It became a symbol.

[serious] But by the 1990s, the Indian company was struggling too. The bikes broke down, and young buyers wanted lighter, modern bikes from Japan. In 1994, an Indian truck company called Eicher bought it. [grave] Sales kept falling. [pause] In 2000, a young member of the family that ran Eicher was told to look at it, and probably close it down.

## rise

[determined] His name was Siddhartha Lal, then about twenty-six, and a motorbike lover. He asked for time to save it. He fixed the quality problems, improved the factory, and kept the classic look and the famous thump, but made the bikes reliable. [building] In 2009, the new Classic 350 came out, and it was a sensation. [knowingly] It worked because he kept everything people loved about the old Bullet, and fixed everything they hated.

[quietly impressed] Today Royal Enfield sells hundreds of thousands of motorbikes a year, in India and around the world, and it even makes some in other countries, including bikes sold back in Britain, where it all began.

## close

[warmly] It took an old factory that refused to stop, and a young man who asked for one more chance.
