## story

[leaning in] The biggest online shopping app in South-East Asia was paid for by video games.

[warmly] The story starts with Forrest Li. He was born in China, studied in Shanghai, and then went to Stanford University in America. While there, he watched how internet companies were changing the world. He married, and moved with his wife to Singapore, a small, wealthy island country at the southern tip of Malaysia.

[curious] In 2009, he started a company called Garena. It brought popular online games to players across South-East Asia, a region of hundreds of millions of young people who loved gaming. [delighted] It was a big success. Then, in 2017, its own game studio released Free Fire, a fast survival game built to run on cheap phones. It became one of the most downloaded mobile games in the world.

[thoughtful] But Forrest Li was watching something else. Across the region, people were buying their first smartphones. Few of them had shopped online before. Many did not have credit cards. Big online shops from other countries had arrived, but they were built for computers, and for richer customers.

[determined] So in 2015, Garena launched an app called Shopee. It was built for phones from day one. [pause] And Forrest Li made a bold decision about the money.

## rise

[building] He used the profits from his games to pay for free delivery, and let sellers join without paying fees. [with a smile] Shopee filled television with catchy, cheerful adverts, and ran huge sales on special dates like the ninth of September. Small sellers flooded in, and so did buyers. [knowingly] It worked because the money from games let Shopee spend for years before it made a profit, longer than its rivals could afford to.

[quietly impressed] The company renamed itself Sea, and joined the New York Stock Exchange in 2017. Today Shopee is the biggest online marketplace in South-East Asia, and Sea runs games, shopping and digital payments for millions of people.

## close

[warmly] It took one company's hit games, paying for a shop that people across a whole region would learn to use on their phones.
