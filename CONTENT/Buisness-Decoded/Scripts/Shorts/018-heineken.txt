## story

[leaning in] In 1864, a twenty-two-year-old in Amsterdam persuaded his mother to buy a brewery. Today the beer with his name on it is sold in almost every country on Earth.

[warmly] His name was Gerard Adriaan Heineken. His family was wealthy, and his mother had money to invest. [curious] At that time, Amsterdam had a problem. Many people drank strong gin, too much of it, and it caused real harm. Gerard believed that good, lighter beer would be better for the city, and a good business too.

[determined] So he convinced his mother to buy De Hooiberg, which means The Haystack. It was one of the biggest breweries in Amsterdam, and it had been making beer for nearly three hundred years.

[thoughtful] Gerard did not want to make the heavy dark beers the Dutch usually drank. He had tasted the lighter, golden lager brewed in Bavaria, in southern Germany, and he wanted to make that. [curious] Lager needs cold to brew, and a very particular yeast, the tiny living thing that turns sugar into alcohol and gives beer its taste. So he built a new brewery, with cool cellars, and hired a scientist.

[with wonder] In 1886 that scientist, who had studied under the famous Louis Pasteur, isolated a pure strain of yeast for Gerard's beer. It made the taste the same, batch after batch. [pause] Heineken still brews with it today.

## rise

[building] The beer began to win prizes, and was shipped abroad. When America ended its ban on alcohol in 1933, Heineken had beer on its way across the Atlantic almost at once. [knowingly] Then Gerard's grandson, Freddy Heineken, turned it into a brand. He made the green bottle and the red star famous, and even tilted the letter e in the name so it looked like it was smiling. It worked because the beer tasted the same everywhere, and looked the same everywhere.

[quietly impressed] Heineken is now the second-biggest brewer in the world, and its family still holds a controlling stake.

## close

[warmly] It took a young man with a good argument, and the scientist who made sure every bottle tasted exactly like the last.
