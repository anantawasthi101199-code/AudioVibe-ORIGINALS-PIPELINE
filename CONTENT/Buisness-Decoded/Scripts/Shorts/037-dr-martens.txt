## story

[leaning in] The chunky boots that punks, rock stars and students wear around the world were invented by a German army doctor with a sore ankle.

[warmly] His name was Klaus Märtens. In 1945, at the end of the Second World War, he hurt his ankle skiing in the Alps. [curious] His army boots were stiff and hard, and every step hurt. So he designed his own boot, with a soft sole that had pockets of air trapped inside, so it cushioned the foot like walking on a pillow.

[thoughtful] With a friend, he began making the boots from old rubber, left over from the war, in the city of Munich. [with a smile] Their first big customers were not soldiers or workers. They were older women, who liked the comfort for walking around town.

[determined] In 1959 the two men advertised the sole in foreign magazines, looking for a company to make the boots abroad. [curious] In England, a family of bootmakers called the Griggs saw the advert. They had been making boots in a village in Northamptonshire for generations. They bought the rights.

[thoughtful] The Griggs gave the boot its own look. Yellow stitching around the edge. A grooved two-coloured sole. A little loop at the back of the heel. [pause] And on the first of April, 1960, they made the first pair, and named it after the date.

## rise

[building] The 1460. One, four, sixty. It was cheap and tough, and it was sold as a work boot to postmen, factory workers and police officers. [delighted] Then the young started wearing them. In the 1960s a famous rock guitarist wore them on stage. Then came skinheads, and punks, and later grunge bands. Each new group of rebels picked them up. [knowingly] It worked because the boot was tough, honest and a bit rough, and it fitted every kind of person who wanted to look like they did not care about looking nice.

[serious] The business nearly collapsed in the early 2000s, and most making moved abroad. [quietly impressed] But the boots came back into fashion, and in 2021 Dr. Martens joined the London Stock Exchange. The original factory in Northamptonshire still makes some by hand.

## close

[warmly] It took a doctor who needed a softer step, and bootmakers who knew how to make it look like it meant business.
