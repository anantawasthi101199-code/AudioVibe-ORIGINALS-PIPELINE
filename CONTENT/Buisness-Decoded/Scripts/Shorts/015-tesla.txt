## story

[leaning in] In December 2008, Tesla was weeks away from running out of money. Twelve years later, it was worth more than any car company in history.

[warmly] It was founded in 2003 by two engineers in California, Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning. [curious] They had a bold idea. Electric cars back then were slow, ugly little things with a short range. But laptop batteries had become small and powerful. What if you packed thousands of them into a car? Could an electric car be fast, beautiful, and fun to drive?

[thoughtful] In 2004 a rich young entrepreneur called Elon Musk put millions of dollars into the company and became its chairman. He had just made a fortune selling a payments company called PayPal. [determined] Tesla's first car was the Roadster, a sleek two-seater sports car built on the body of a British sports car, packed with nearly seven thousand laptop-style batteries. It could go from zero to sixty miles an hour in under four seconds. [delighted] Celebrities lined up to buy one.

[grave] But building it cost far more than planned. The founders left the company, and Musk took charge. Then, in 2008, the world's banks crashed and money dried up everywhere. [serious] Musk poured in nearly all the money he had left. [pause] On Christmas Eve 2008, at the last possible moment, he closed a new round of investment, and Tesla survived.

## rise

[building] Next came the Model S, a family car that was also fast and had a range long enough for real journeys. Then the cheaper Model 3. Building it at scale nearly broke the company again, and Musk famously slept on the factory floor. [knowingly] It worked in the end because Tesla made electric cars that people wanted for themselves, rather than out of duty, and built its own charging network so drivers could actually travel.

[quietly impressed] In 2020 Tesla became the most valuable car company in the world, and in 2023 its Model Y was the best-selling car on the planet.

## close

[warmly] It took engineers who saw a laptop battery and imagined a sports car, and the nerve to survive two near-collapses.
