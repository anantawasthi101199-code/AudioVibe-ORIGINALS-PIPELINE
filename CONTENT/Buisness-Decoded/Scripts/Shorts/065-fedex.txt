## story

[leaning in] Every night, while most of America sleeps, hundreds of planes land at one airport in Memphis, Tennessee, unload millions of parcels, and take off again before dawn. That system began as a student's essay.

[warmly] Fred Smith was a student at Yale University in the 1960s. He wrote a paper arguing that, in a world of computers and electronics, businesses would need urgent parts delivered overnight, and the ordinary airlines, which carried parcels in the hold of passenger planes, were too slow for it. [with a smile] The story goes that he got a C. Smith himself has said he cannot remember the grade.

[serious] He served as a Marine in Vietnam, came home, and in 1971 started Federal Express, using money from his family and from investors. [curious] His idea was strange. Instead of flying parcels straight from one city to another, every single parcel would fly to one place first, Memphis, in the middle of the country. There, overnight, they would be sorted, and flown out again to where they were going.

[building] On the night of the seventeenth of April, 1973, fourteen small jets took off, carrying one hundred and eighty-six parcels to twenty-five cities. [softly] It was a tiny start, and it lost money fast. Fuel prices soared that year.

[pause] At one low point, with the company almost out of cash, the story goes that Smith flew to Las Vegas with the last five thousand dollars, played blackjack, and came back with about twenty-seven thousand. It paid the fuel bill, and the planes kept flying.

## rise

[building] Slowly the parcels grew. In 1977 the American government loosened the rules on air cargo, and FedEx could fly much bigger planes. [knowingly] The Memphis idea, which everyone calls hub and spoke, worked because one central sorting place lets you connect every city to every other city with just one flight in and one flight out, instead of thousands of separate routes.

[quietly impressed] Its adverts promised that a parcel would absolutely, positively get there overnight, and it did. Today FedEx moves millions of parcels a day in more than two hundred countries, and Smith led it for half a century.

## close

[warmly] It took one student who could picture every parcel in America flying to the same city in the dark, and enough nerve to keep the planes in the air when the money ran out.
