## story

[leaning in] Rolex is the most famous watch brand on Earth, and it has no owners selling shares or chasing quick profits. It belongs to a charity. To see how, we go back to a young man in London who had lost his whole family.

[softly] His name was Hans Wilsdorf. He was born in Germany in 1881, and by the age of twelve both of his parents had died. He was raised by relatives and sent to boarding school. [curious] As a young man he took a job with a watch exporter in Switzerland, the country famous for watchmaking, and learned the trade.

[thoughtful] Then he moved to London. In 1905, at just twenty-four, he started a watch company there with his brother-in-law. Back then, a serious man carried a pocket watch on a chain. Watches worn on the wrist were seen as delicate jewellery for women, and nobody trusted them to keep good time. [determined] Wilsdorf believed the opposite. He was sure people would one day want their watch on their wrist, and he was going to make one accurate enough to trust.

[with a smile] In 1908 he invented a name, Rolex, short, easy to say in any language, and small enough to fit on a watch face. In 1910 a Rolex became the first wristwatch to win an official Swiss certificate for accuracy. After the First World War he moved the company to Geneva, in Switzerland.

[building] Then, in 1926, he made a watch with a case sealed tight against water and dust, and called it the Oyster. [pause] A year later, he found the perfect way to prove it.

## rise

[delighted] A young swimmer, Mercedes Gleitze, swam the cold English Channel wearing one. Afterwards the watch was still ticking perfectly. Wilsdorf bought the whole front page of a British newspaper to tell everyone. [knowingly] It worked because he did not just say the watch was tough. He showed it.

[softly] Wilsdorf's wife died in 1944. They had no children. So he set up a foundation, and when he died in 1960 he left it the company. [quietly impressed] That foundation still owns Rolex today and uses part of its money for charity, while Rolex makes around a million watches a year, each one built to last a lifetime.

## close

[warmly] It took an orphan who trusted the wrist when everyone else trusted the pocket.
