## story

[leaning in] Ryanair flies more passengers than any other airline in Europe. Its first plane had just fifteen seats, and it nearly went bust within a few years.

[warmly] It was founded in 1984 by an Irish businessman, Tony Ryan, who had made his money leasing aircraft to other airlines. [curious] Back then, flying in Europe was expensive. A few big airlines, often owned by governments, kept fares high. A return flight between Ireland and London could cost as much as a week's wages.

[determined] In 1985, Ryanair's first flight took off from Waterford, a small city in Ireland, to London, in a tiny propeller plane. Soon it was flying bigger planes from Dublin, and offering lower fares than the national airline. [delighted] Passengers rushed to buy tickets.

[grave] But it was losing money, fast. It had too many kinds of plane, too many routes, too many costs. [serious] Tony Ryan asked a young accountant who worked for him, Michael O'Leary, to sort it out. [pause] O'Leary flew to America to study an airline that was making money while charging very little.

## rise

[building] It was Southwest Airlines, in Texas. And Ryanair copied its recipe. One type of plane only, the Boeing 737, so that pilots, parts and mechanics could all be shared. Planes turned around at the gate in about twenty-five minutes, so they spent more time in the air earning money. Smaller airports further from the city, where landing fees were lower. [wry] And no free food or drinks. You paid extra for bags, for seats, even, at one time, for printing your boarding pass.

[knowingly] It worked because many people cared about one thing more than comfort: the price. Ryanair made flying so cheap that people who had never flown began hopping across Europe for a weekend.

[serious] O'Leary, who became its boss, loved a fight and said outrageous things, and the airline has often been criticised for its fees and its service. [quietly impressed] But today it carries around two hundred million passengers a year.

## close

[warmly] It took an airline in trouble that decided price was the only thing that mattered, and cut everything else.
