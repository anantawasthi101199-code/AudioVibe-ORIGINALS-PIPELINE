## story

[leaning in] For years, the richest person in Africa has been a Nigerian who started out trading sugar and cement with money borrowed from his uncle.

[warmly] Aliko Dangote was born in 1957 in Kano, a big, old trading city in northern Nigeria. His family were traders, and his great-grandfather had once been one of the richest men in West Africa. [with a smile] As a boy at school, Aliko bought sweets in bulk and sold them to the other children for a profit.

[determined] In 1977, when he was about twenty-one, he borrowed money from his uncle and started trading. He bought goods like rice, sugar and cement abroad, shipped them into Nigeria, and sold them. [delighted] He was good at it, and grew fast.

[thoughtful] But he noticed something. Nigeria was a huge country, with a fast-growing population, and it imported almost everything. Even basic things like cement and sugar came from overseas, which made them expensive, and left the country depending on others. [serious] Importing was profitable. But it would never build anything lasting. [pause] So Dangote decided to stop importing, and start making things in Nigeria.

## rise

[building] He built sugar refineries, flour mills and salt factories. Then cement. Nigeria and its neighbours were building roads, houses and offices everywhere, and they all needed cement. Dangote built giant cement plants, first in Nigeria, then in other countries across Africa. [knowingly] It worked because he made what his own continent kept buying from abroad, closer to the customers, and cheaper.

[quietly impressed] Dangote Cement became the biggest cement company in Africa, and Aliko Dangote became Africa's richest person. [determined] Then he took on his biggest project. Nigeria pumps huge amounts of oil, but for years it imported most of its petrol. So, near Lagos, Dangote built one of the largest oil refineries in the world, which began producing fuel in 2024.

## close

[warmly] It took a trader who decided it was better to build than to buy.
