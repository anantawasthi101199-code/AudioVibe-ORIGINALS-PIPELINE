## hook

[leaning in] In 2007, two young engineers in Bangalore, in southern India, quit their jobs at Amazon. They put in about four hundred thousand rupees of their own savings, roughly ten thousand dollars at the time, and started selling books online from a small flat.

[curious] Eleven years later, in 2018, the American retail giant Walmart paid about sixteen billion dollars for most of their company. [a beat] It was the biggest deal of its kind India had ever seen.

[warmly] Hey, I'm Adrian, and this is Business Decoded. Today, the story of Flipkart: how two friends taught India to shop online, the clever fix that won people's trust, and the long battle against the company they used to work for.

## beginnings

[matter-of-fact] Two people to know. Sachin Bansal and Binny Bansal. [with a smile] They share a surname, but they are not related. Both studied engineering at IIT Delhi, one of India's top universities, and both later worked at Amazon's office in Bangalore.

[warmly] So let's go back to the very beginning. India in 2007. Only a small share of Indians used the internet, and most of them did so in internet cafés or at work. Very few people had a credit card. And shopping online seemed risky. You had to type in your card details and trust that a parcel would really turn up.

[thoughtful] But Sachin and Binny had seen how Amazon worked from the inside. They believed India would come online sooner or later, and they wanted to be there first. In October 2007, they started Flipkart. They began with books, because books are simple to sell online. A book is the same book wherever you buy it, so customers do not need to touch it first, and it does not break in the post.

[curious] The early days were very small. The two of them did everything. They built the website. They answered customer calls. They went to bookshops to buy the books that customers had ordered, packed them, and sometimes delivered them themselves. [with a smile] The story the company tells is that its very first order was a book called Leaving Microsoft to Change the World, bought by a customer in Andhra Pradesh, and it took a while to track down a copy.

[warmly] Word spread. People liked that the books were cheap and actually arrived. By the end of the first year, Flipkart was getting more and more orders every day. [pause] But a bigger problem was waiting.

## struggle

[serious] But it didn't stay easy for long. To grow, Flipkart needed people who had never shopped online to start. And most Indians did not trust it. They did not have a credit card, and those who did were afraid of fraud.

[determined] So in 2010 Flipkart made a decision that changed online shopping in India. Cash on delivery. You ordered online, and you paid the delivery person in cash when the parcel arrived at your door. [knowingly] If it never came, you lost nothing. If it came, you could see it before handing over any money.

[thoughtful] It worked brilliantly for customers. But it made life very hard for Flipkart. Delivery companies were not used to collecting cash, and some were slow to pass it back. Money was stuck in the system for days or weeks. [grave] So Flipkart built its own delivery business, called Ekart, with its own staff and vans. It was expensive. But it meant Flipkart controlled the most important part of the experience: the moment the parcel arrived.

[curious] They added more. Thirty-day returns, so people could send things back. Phones, then electronics, clothes, and household goods. They raised money from investors, and they spent a lot of it on discounts to win customers. [serious] Year after year, the company lost money. That is common for fast-growing internet businesses, but it meant Flipkart always needed more investment to keep going.

[grave] Then, in 2013, the biggest rival of all arrived. Amazon opened in India. And in 2014, its founder Jeff Bezos announced he would invest two billion dollars there, standing on top of a decorated truck in Bangalore to hand over a giant cheque. [pause] Sachin and Binny's old employer had come to beat them.

## rise

[building] And then came the move that changed everything. Flipkart decided to become the place where India went for its biggest shopping days.

[curious] In October 2014 it held its first Big Billion Day sale, with huge discounts on phones, televisions and more. So many people visited that the website crashed, and some items sold out in seconds. [with a smile] The founders publicly apologised to customers for the problems. But the sale took in hundreds of crores of rupees in a single day, and the whole country was talking about it.

[thoughtful] Here's why the battle with Amazon went the way it did. Flipkart understood the Indian customer in a way a newcomer could not at first. It worked with phone makers to launch new models only on Flipkart. It made the app fast on cheap phones and patchy connections. And it built delivery to small towns that big companies had ignored. [knowingly] An everyday comparison: it was like the local shop that knows every customer by name competing with the giant supermarket that has just opened down the road.

[determined] Flipkart also bought other companies. In 2014 it bought Myntra, an online fashion shop. And in 2016 it bought PhonePe, a small payments app, which went on to become one of India's most used ways of sending money by phone.

[serious] It was a hard fight. Both sides lost billions of dollars on discounts, deliveries and advertising. The founders stepped back from running the company day to day, and new managers came in. [pause] And then, in 2018, an offer arrived that changed Flipkart's story for good.

## ending

[warmly] In May 2018, Walmart, the biggest retailer in the world, agreed to buy about seventy-seven per cent of Flipkart for around sixteen billion dollars. Sachin Bansal sold his shares and left. Binny stayed on for a short time and later left too. Both became very rich men.

[thoughtful] Today Flipkart is one of India's biggest online shops, still fighting Amazon every festive season, with hundreds of millions of registered customers.

[reflective] What did it take? Starting small, with books, so the first customers had nothing to fear. Cash on delivery, which let people trust a website they had never met. And building its own deliveries, so that the parcel at the door was always Flipkart's own promise.

[warmly] It began with two friends, a small flat in Bangalore and a pile of books. [softly] And it ended with Walmart paying billions for what they built.
