## hook

[low, steady] It is the evening of Saturday, the twenty-sixth of August, 1978, in New Delhi. It is the monsoon. The roads are wet, and the light is going.

[warmly] Two teenagers leave their home in the naval officers' houses at Dhaula Kuan, in the south-west of the city. Geeta Chopra is sixteen. Her brother Sanjay is fourteen. They are on their way to All India Radio, the national broadcaster, on Parliament Street, in the centre of the city.

[thoughtful] At eight o'clock, Geeta is due to take part in a programme called Yuva Vani, the voice of youth. Their father has said he will pick them up from the radio station at nine, when it is over.

[softly] At eight o'clock, the programme starts. Geeta is not in the studio. [pause] At nine, their father is waiting outside, and neither of his children comes out.

## context

[warmly] Geeta and Sanjay were the children of Captain Madan Mohan Chopra, an officer in the Indian Navy, and his wife. Geeta was a student at the Jesus and Mary College, part of Delhi University. Sanjay was at Modern School, one of the city's well-known schools. [thoughtful] Geeta had been on the radio before. Speaking on Yuva Vani was something she was proud of.

[measured] Their home at Dhaula Kuan was some way from the centre of Delhi. That evening, the two of them set out in good time. In Delhi in 1978, it was quite normal for young people to ask for a lift from a passing car, especially in daylight and on the busy main roads. Many people did it every day.

[thoughtful] Their father went to Parliament Street at nine. When the children did not appear, he went into the radio station, and was told that Geeta had never arrived. He went home, hoping they had come back another way. [pause] They had not.

## investigation

[serious] Earlier that evening, at about half past six, people on the roads of central Delhi had seen something wrong. A man driving behind a small yellow car saw a struggle inside it, and a girl and a boy calling for help. He noted the car's number, and he reported it to the police.

[measured] Other people saw the same car that evening, and some of them reported it too. But the reports went to different police stations, and each station seemed to think it belonged to another. Nobody went after the car that night. [thoughtful] That failure would later be examined, and it would become part of what made the city so angry.

[grave] On the twenty-eighth of August, two days after they left home, a young man grazing cattle found the bodies of Geeta and Sanjay in thick scrub, off Upper Ridge Road, in the Ridge, a wooded area on the edge of New Delhi.

[serious] They had both been killed. What was done to them in those hours is set out in the court record. It does not need to be told here.

[thoughtful] Delhi was shocked. Students marched. Questions were asked in Parliament. Newspapers asked how two children could be taken in the middle of the city, past witnesses, while the police passed the reports between stations.

[measured] The police traced the yellow car. It had been stolen. They identified two men: Kuljeet Singh, known as Ranga, and Jasbir Singh, known as Billa. Both had criminal records. Their photographs were printed in newspapers across the country. [pause] And for nearly two weeks, nobody could find them.

## turning_point

[low, steady] On the eighth of September, 1978, two men jumped onto a train, the Kalka Mail, which runs from Howrah, near Calcutta, to the north. In the rush, they climbed into a carriage reserved for soldiers.

[measured] The soldiers in that carriage had seen the newspapers. They looked at the two men, and they recognised them. [building] The soldiers held them on the train, and when it reached Delhi, they handed Ranga and Billa over to the police.

[thoughtful] It was not a detective's breakthrough. It was a carriage full of ordinary people who had read about two children, and had remembered two faces. [measured] The men were questioned, the evidence was gathered, and the case went to trial.

## aftermath

[serious] In 1979, Ranga and Billa were convicted of murder in a Delhi court and sentenced to death. Their appeals went to the Delhi High Court, and to the Supreme Court of India, and failed. The President of India rejected their pleas for mercy.

[grave] On the thirty-first of January, 1982, Kuljeet Singh and Jasbir Singh were hanged in Tihar Jail, in Delhi.

[thoughtful] The case changed some things. The failure of the police that first evening led to inquiries, and to changes in how reports of a crime in progress were passed between stations. [measured] And for many families in Delhi, the habit of asking for a lift stopped being something their children did.

[warmly] In 1978, the Indian Council for Child Welfare created two national awards for bravery by children, and named them after Geeta and Sanjay. The Geeta Chopra Award goes to a girl, and the Sanjay Chopra Award to a boy, for a child who has shown courage in a moment of danger. [thoughtful] They are given each year with the National Bravery Awards. In the evidence gathered after their deaths, witnesses described two children who fought back.

[softly] So that is how their names are said now. Every year, in front of the country, as a reward for courage. [gently] Geeta, who was on her way to speak on the radio, and Sanjay, who had gone along to keep her company.
