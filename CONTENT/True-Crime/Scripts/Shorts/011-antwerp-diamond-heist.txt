## who_and_what
[steady] It's Monday morning, the seventeenth of February, 2003, in Antwerp, in Belgium. Two floors under the Antwerp Diamond Centre, a guard opens the door to the vault, and stops.

[measured] The floor is covered in diamonds, gold, jewels and paperwork, ankle deep. The doors of the strongboxes hang open. More than a hundred of them have been emptied.

[measured] Antwerp is the world's diamond city. Most of the world's rough diamonds pass through a few streets near its railway station, and this vault was meant to be one of the safest places on earth. It had a lock with a hundred million possible combinations, heat sensors that could feel a body, a light sensor, a magnetic alarm on the door and a sensor that could feel the walls being drilled. Ten layers of security in all.

[thoughtful] The man the police came to suspect was Leonardo Notarbartolo, an Italian. For about two years he had rented an office in the building, posing as a diamond dealer. That gave him a pass, and a box of his own in the vault, and time to look around.

[measured] Over the weekend, the police said, a small team went down and beat every layer, one at a time. They left with diamonds and jewels later valued by investigators at more than a hundred million dollars. [pause] It looked like a perfect job. It was undone by a farmer.

## ending
[thoughtful] That same Monday, August Van Camp was walking his land by a motorway outside Brussels, in a patch of woods where people kept dumping rubbish. He found bin bags of it. Inside were envelopes from the Diamond Centre, diamond certificates, tape from its security cameras, and a half-eaten salami sandwich.

[measured] He called the police. Among the papers was a receipt that led them towards Notarbartolo, and DNA from the sandwich was later matched to him. He was arrested, and in 2005 a Belgian court convicted him, and sentenced him to ten years in prison. Other members of the team were convicted too.

[measured] Years later, Notarbartolo told a journalist his own version, saying the haul was much smaller and the job was an inside fraud. That claim was never proved.

[thoughtful] The diamonds were never found. Dozens of dealers who had trusted that vault lost what was in their boxes, and most of it has never come back.
