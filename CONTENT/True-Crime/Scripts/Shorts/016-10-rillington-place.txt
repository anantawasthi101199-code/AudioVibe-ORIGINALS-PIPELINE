## who_and_what

[steady] On the 9th of March 1950, at Pentonville Prison in London, a twenty-five-year-old van driver named Timothy Evans was hanged for the murder of his baby daughter. He had said, at the end of his trial, that the man who did it was living downstairs.

[measured] Timothy Evans came from Wales. He could barely read or write. In 1948 he moved with his young wife, Beryl, into the top floor of a small, run-down terraced house at 10 Rillington Place, in Notting Hill, in west London. Their daughter, Geraldine, was born that October. On the ground floor lived an older couple. The husband had been a wartime special constable, a volunteer policeman, and he was quiet and very respectable.

[steady] In November 1949, Beryl and Geraldine disappeared. Evans went to the police in Wales and gave a series of statements that contradicted each other, at one point confessing, at another blaming the man downstairs. Police searched the house and found Beryl and Geraldine dead in a washhouse in the back yard. Both had been strangled. In January 1950, Evans was tried at the Old Bailey for Geraldine's murder. The man downstairs gave evidence against him. The jury took forty minutes to convict.

## ending

[thoughtful] In March 1953, the downstairs tenant moved out. The new tenant was putting up a shelf in the kitchen when he found a papered-over alcove, and in it were the bodies of three women. The former special constable was arrested. More remains were found under the floor and in the garden. He was convicted and hanged that July. Before he died, he confessed to killing Beryl Evans.

[measured] It took years of campaigning by Timothy's mother and sister, and by writers and lawyers. In 1966 an official inquiry found that Evans had probably not killed his daughter, the murder he was hanged for, and that October he was given a royal pardon. The case was one of the reasons Britain abolished hanging for murder, in 1965.

[steady] Timothy Evans was reburied by his family in a cemetery in east London. Rillington Place no longer exists. The council renamed it, then knocked it down and built something else in its place.
