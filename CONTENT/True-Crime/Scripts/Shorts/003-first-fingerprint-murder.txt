## who_and_what

[low, steady] It's June 1892, in Necochea, a small town on the coast of Argentina. A woman called Francisca Rojas runs to her neighbours, screaming. Her two young children, a boy of six and a girl of four, have been killed in their home. [serious] She is hurt too, with a wound to her throat. And she says she knows who did it: a neighbour, a man who had wanted to marry her.

[thoughtful] The police arrest the neighbour. They question him harshly for days. [measured] He insists he is innocent, and says he was somewhere else that night, and others back him up.

[curious] The case reaches a police inspector called Eduardo Álvarez. Álvarez had been trained in a brand-new method, developed by a police officer in the city of La Plata called Juan Vucetich. The idea was that the lines on every person's fingertips make a pattern that belongs to them alone, and never changes.

[serious] Álvarez searched the house. On the wooden frame of a door, he found a small brown mark. A thumbprint, made in blood. [pause] He cut that piece of wood out of the door frame.

## ending

[building] Álvarez took fingerprints from the neighbour, and from Francisca Rojas, and compared them with the mark under a magnifying glass. The neighbour's didn't match. [grave] Francisca Rojas's did.

[measured] Faced with it, she confessed. She had killed her own children, and wounded herself to make it look like an attack.

[thoughtful] It was the first murder ever solved by a fingerprint. Within a few years, Argentina had become the first country to use fingerprints to identify criminals, and the rest of the world followed. [softly] The innocent neighbour went free, cleared by a mark on a door frame.
