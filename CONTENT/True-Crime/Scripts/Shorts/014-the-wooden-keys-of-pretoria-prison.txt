## who_and_what
[steady] On the 11th of December 1979, three men in a maximum security prison in South Africa took a set of wooden keys from their hiding places, and started walking towards the first of ten locked doors.

[measured] Tim Jenkin and Stephen Lee were young white South Africans. They had turned against apartheid, the system of racial separation that ruled their country, and they had worked in secret for the banned African National Congress, the ANC. They set off small leaflet bombs on city streets, harmless devices that scattered anti-apartheid pamphlets. In 1978 they were caught, and convicted under the Terrorism Act. Jenkin got twelve years, and Lee got eight. The third man was Alex Moumbaris, also serving twelve years for ANC work.

[thoughtful] Pretoria Central's section for white political prisoners was said to be impossible to break out of. Jenkin decided it wasn't. He worked in the prison carpentry shop, and that gave him wood. He studied the keys hanging at the warders' belts by eye, and, in time, he got close enough to draw them. He made copies of wood, in secret, and tested them on the doors at night. Some broke off in the locks and had to be fished out. He made more than a dozen keys in all, working by trial and error for about eighteen months.

[measured] That evening they slipped out of their cells, opened door after door, and came to a street exit where they could not make a key turn. They forced it. At the last one, there was a guard post close by. They walked out into the warm evening, and the first thing they did was take a taxi.

## ending
[thoughtful] It was a major embarrassment for the prison service. By the time the alarm was raised, they were well away. With help from friends in the ANC, the three of them reached Swaziland, then Mozambique, and went on to Tanzania and finally London. Police never caught them. No one was hurt.

[measured] Jenkin went on working for the ANC from London. In the 1980s he helped to build a secret encrypted communication system, Operation Vula, that let its leaders in exile talk to activists inside the country, and it reached Nelson Mandela in prison. In 1994, apartheid ended. Jenkin's book about the escape became a film in 2020.

[steady] The keys are what stay with you. A man sat at a workbench, in a jail built to break him, and carved a piece of wood to the shape of a lock he had only ever seen across a room.
