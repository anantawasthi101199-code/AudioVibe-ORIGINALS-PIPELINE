## who_and_what

[steady] On the evening of the 8th of December 1963, at a hotel by Lake Tahoe, on the border of California and Nevada, two men knocked on the door of room 417 and said they had a delivery.

[measured] Inside was Frank Sinatra Junior. He was nineteen, the son of the most famous singer in America, and he was trying to make his own way as a singer, touring with a big band. That night he was eating chicken in his room with a trumpet player from the band, before the show. When the door opened, one of the men had a gun. They tied up the trumpet player, put a blindfold on Frank Junior, and drove him away through a snowstorm towards Los Angeles.

[steady] The kidnappers were young men in their twenties, led by an old school friend of Frank Junior's sister, a man called Barry Keenan. He had planned it for months. He believed, he said later, that he could take the money and pay it back, and that nobody would be hurt.

[measured] Frank Sinatra Senior flew to Nevada, and the FBI moved in. The kidnappers rang him and demanded two hundred and forty thousand dollars. Frank Senior paid it, in used bills, left at a petrol station between two school buses. On the night of the 10th of December, one of the kidnappers lost his nerve and let Frank Junior go, by a road in the Bel Air district of Los Angeles. He walked until a security guard found him and drove him home.

## ending

[thoughtful] All three men were arrested within days, after one of them confessed to his brother, and most of the ransom was found. At their trial in 1964, their lawyers argued that the kidnapping had been a publicity stunt arranged by Frank Junior himself. The jury rejected that, and convicted all three. Their long sentences were later reduced, and they were out of prison within a few years.

[steady] The rumour stayed. For the rest of his life Frank Sinatra Junior had to live with people whispering that he had faked his own kidnapping, though the court, the FBI and the kidnappers themselves said he had not. He kept singing, and later led his father's orchestra on tour. He died in 2016, at the age of seventy-two, on the road, a few hours before a show.
