## who_and_what

[low, steady] It's early on the morning of the twelfth of June, 1962, on Alcatraz, a rocky island in the bay off San Francisco. A guard does the morning count, walking past the cells. [measured] In three of them, a man seems to be asleep, his head on the pillow. He calls out. One doesn't wake. [serious] He reaches in and touches the head, and it rolls onto the floor. It's made of plaster, soap and toilet paper, painted, with real hair glued on.

[thoughtful] Alcatraz was America's toughest prison, built for men who had escaped from other prisons. The water around it was cold, with strong currents. Frank Morris, a bank robber, and two brothers, John and Clarence Anglin, had been planning for months.

[curious] Using spoons they had sharpened, and a drill made from the motor of a vacuum cleaner, they had slowly dug out the crumbling concrete around the small air vents at the back of their cells, hiding the holes behind cardboard painted to look like the wall. [measured] Behind the cells was a narrow passage. They climbed up it to the roof. In a hidden workshop on top of the cellblock, they had made a raft and life jackets from more than fifty stolen raincoats, glued together.

## ending

[thoughtful] That night, they climbed through the vent, up onto the roof, down a pipe to the ground, over the fence, and launched the raft from the shore into the dark water of the bay.

[serious] Pieces of a raincoat raft and a life jacket were later found in the water. The FBI investigated for seventeen years and concluded they had most likely drowned. But no bodies were ever found, and the United States Marshals, who hunt escaped prisoners, keep the case open. [measured] The year after, Alcatraz was closed for good.

[softly] Whether Frank Morris and the Anglin brothers drowned that night or reached the shore has never been settled. [thoughtful] Their dummy heads are still on display, on the island they left behind.
