## who_and_what

[low, steady] It's late morning on the fourth of August, 1892, a sweltering day in Fall River, a mill town in Massachusetts, in America. The family's maid, Bridget Sullivan, is resting in her room at the top of the house. [measured] At around ten past eleven, she hears the voice of the younger daughter of the house calling up the stairs. Come down quick. Father's dead.

[thoughtful] Andrew Borden was seventy, one of the richest men in town, careful with money and not much liked. He lived with his second wife, Abby, and his two grown-up daughters from his first marriage, Emma and Lizzie. Lizzie was thirty-two. The family was not a happy one, with quarrels about money and property.

[serious] Andrew had been killed with a hatchet as he lay on the sitting room sofa. Then a neighbour went upstairs and found Abby, killed in the same way, in the guest room. She had died about an hour and a half before her husband.

[thoughtful] Emma was away. Bridget had been outside cleaning the windows, then upstairs. Lizzie said she'd been in the barn. [measured] Police found a hatchet head in the cellar, its handle broken off. A few days later, a friend saw Lizzie burn a dress in the kitchen stove. Lizzie said it was stained with paint. She was arrested and charged with both murders.

## ending

[thoughtful] Her trial in June 1893 was followed across America. Much of the evidence was weak. There was no blood found on her, no clear weapon, and the judge ruled that some of what she had said earlier could not be used. [serious] After about an hour and a half, the jury found her not guilty.

[measured] Lizzie stayed in Fall River and bought a large house, but many in the town shunned her for the rest of her life. She died in 1927. No one else was ever charged.

[softly] Who killed Andrew and Abby Borden has never been settled. [gently] They were buried in Fall River, side by side, in the summer heat of 1892.
