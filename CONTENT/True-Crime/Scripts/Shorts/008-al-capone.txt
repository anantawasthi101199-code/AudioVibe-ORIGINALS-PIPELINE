## who_and_what

[low, steady] It's the seventeenth of October, 1931, in a federal courtroom in Chicago. The most famous gangster in America is sitting at the defence table in an expensive suit, waiting for the jury. [measured] They are not here to decide whether he killed anyone. They are here to decide whether he paid his taxes.

[thoughtful] Al Capone grew up in a poor family of Italian immigrants in Brooklyn, New York, and left school at fourteen. In the 1920s, alcohol was banned across America, a time called Prohibition, and gangs made fortunes selling it illegally. [serious] Capone moved to Chicago and rose to run one of the biggest of those gangs, with breweries, gambling dens and speakeasies, secret bars, across the city. He paid off police officers and politicians. Rivals were shot in the street.

[grave] On Valentine's Day, 1929, seven men from a rival gang were lined up against the wall of a garage and shot. Everyone believed Capone was behind it. [measured] But witnesses would not talk, and nothing tied him to it. He was never charged.

[thoughtful] So the government tried another way. In 1927, the Supreme Court had ruled that money made from crime still had to be declared and taxed. Capone had never filed a tax return in his life. [pause] He owned nothing in his own name, and paid for everything in cash. The tax investigators had to prove he had an income at all.

## ending

[measured] In 1930, an investigator called Frank Wilson was searching through old files late at night and found a ledger from a gambling house, with payments marked for "Al". He tracked down the bookkeeper who had written it, and persuaded him to testify.

[serious] At the trial, the jury heard about Capone's spending: the custom suits, the furnished houses, the thousands of dollars on meat for one estate. The jury found him guilty of tax evasion. He was sentenced to eleven years in prison, the longest sentence ever given for that crime at the time. [thoughtful] He served part of it on Alcatraz, an island prison in San Francisco Bay, and came out in 1939, seriously ill. He died in 1947.

[softly] No one was ever convicted of the killings in that garage. [gently] Seven men were lined up against a brick wall on Valentine's Day, and a tax ledger is the closest anyone came to an answer.
