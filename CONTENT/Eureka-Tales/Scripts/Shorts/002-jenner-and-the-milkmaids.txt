## mystery

[curious] Why did milkmaids, the women who milked cows, so often escape smallpox, one of the deadliest diseases on Earth? [warmly] This is the story of how Edward Jenner made the first vaccine. It begins in the English countryside, in Gloucestershire, in the 1700s.

## story

[serious] Smallpox killed millions. It covered people in painful sores, and killed roughly one in three of those who caught it. Many survivors were left scarred or blind.

[thoughtful] Edward Jenner was a country doctor in the village of Berkeley. He kept hearing a piece of local wisdom. Milkmaids often caught cowpox, a mild disease from cows that gave them a few sores on their hands. And the ones who did seemed never to catch smallpox.

[curious] Jenner wondered if it was true. In May 1796, a milkmaid called Sarah Nelmes came to him with cowpox sores, caught from a cow called Blossom. [building] He took a little fluid from one of her sores, and scratched it into the arm of an eight-year-old boy, James Phipps, the son of his gardener. The boy had a mild fever, and recovered.

[intense] Then, weeks later, Jenner did something no doctor would be allowed to do today. He deliberately exposed James to smallpox. [pause] And James didn't get ill.

## close

[warmly] Jenner called it vaccination, from the Latin word for cow. [quietly impressed] It spread around the world, and in 1980, smallpox became the first human disease ever wiped out, starting with a milkmaid's hand and a cow called Blossom.
