## mystery

[curious] An engineer stood next to a machine that wasn't hot, and the chocolate bar in his pocket melted. Why? [warmly] This is the story of how Percy Spencer invented the microwave oven. It begins in America, in 1945, in a factory making radar for the war.

## story

[thoughtful] Percy Spencer had left school at twelve, but taught himself so much about electronics that he became one of the top engineers at a company called Raytheon. Its factory made magnetrons, devices that produce microwaves, a kind of invisible radio wave, for radar systems that spotted enemy planes.

[curious] One day, standing in front of a working magnetron, Spencer noticed that a chocolate bar in his pocket had turned to a sticky mess. [lightly] Other people had felt a strange warmth near the machines before. Spencer wanted to know why.

[delighted] So he tried an experiment. He put some popcorn kernels in front of the magnetron. They started popping all over the lab. The next day, the story goes, he tried an egg. It shook, and then exploded, all over a colleague who was leaning in to look.

[thoughtful] Spencer worked out what was happening. The microwaves made the water in food jiggle back and forth incredibly fast. And all that jiggling made heat, cooking the food from the inside.

## close

[warmly] Raytheon built the first microwave oven in 1947. It was nearly as tall as a person and cost thousands of dollars. [quietly impressed] Twenty years later, small ones for the kitchen arrived, and today most homes in many countries have one, all because of a melted chocolate bar.
