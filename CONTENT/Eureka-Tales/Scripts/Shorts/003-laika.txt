## mystery

[curious] In 1957, the first living thing ever sent into orbit was not a person. It was a small stray dog. What really happened to her? [warmly] This is the story of Laika. It begins on the streets of Moscow, in the year the space age started.

## story

[thoughtful] In October 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik, the first satellite, a metal ball that beeped as it circled the Earth. The Soviet leader wanted another triumph, fast, in time for a big anniversary less than a month later. [serious] So engineers rushed to build Sputnik 2, with room inside for a passenger.

[softly] Scientists chose stray dogs from the streets, because they thought dogs used to cold and hunger would cope best. One was a small, calm, part-husky mongrel, about three years old. They named her Laika, which means barker.

[thoughtful] For weeks she was trained, kept in smaller and smaller cages to get used to the tiny cabin, and spun in machines to feel the force of a launch. [grave] Everyone involved knew there was no way to bring her home. Sputnik 2 was built to go up, but not to come back.

[intense] On the third of November, 1957, the rocket launched. Laika reached orbit, the first animal ever to circle the Earth.

## close

[softly] For years, the Soviet Union said she had lived calmly for several days. In 2002, one of the scientists revealed the truth: the cabin overheated, and Laika died within hours. [tender] Today a statue of her stands in Moscow, a small dog standing on top of a rocket.
