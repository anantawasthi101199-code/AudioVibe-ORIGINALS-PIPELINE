## hook

[steady] On the morning of the 9th of February 1855, people across southern Devon opened their doors and found a line of hoof-like prints in the fresh snow, running for many miles, across rooftops and over high walls. This is the case of the Devil's Footprints.

## story

[measured] It had been one of the hardest winters of the century. The river Exe, in the south west of England, had frozen in places. Snow fell on the night of the 8th, then stopped. In the morning, in Exmouth, Topsham, Dawlish, Teignmouth and a string of other villages, people found the marks. They were reported as being about four inches long and nearly three wide, and shaped like small horseshoes. They went in a single line, one in front of the other, about eight inches apart.

[thoughtful] That line was the problem. Most animals leave two rows of prints. This trail went up to a wall fourteen feet high and carried on, on the other side. It seemed to walk along rooftops, stop at a haystack and carry on beyond it. In one place it went up to a small hole in a fence, and through. Reports in The Times and the Illustrated London News put the total length at forty to a hundred miles, on both sides of the estuary, which is about two miles wide.

[steady] In the villages, people talked of the Devil, and some would not go out after dark. Groups of men with guns and dogs followed the trail into the woods. In Dawlish, the hounds are said to have come back howling. A local vicar, the Reverend George Musgrave, told his congregation it might be a kangaroo escaped from a private zoo. He later admitted he said so mainly to calm them down.

## question

[measured] The best modern answer is that there was no single trail at all. Researchers such as the historian Mike Dash, who collected the original letters, point out that no one person followed it the whole way. It was probably several animals, on one night, with witnesses joining up the marks. Wood mice hop in snow, and leave a pair of prints that can look like a small hoof. Birds may account for the rooftops. A thaw and a refreeze can change a print's shape. What that still does not cover is why so many separate, sober people, across so many miles, all saw the same neat line. What walked through Devon that night?
