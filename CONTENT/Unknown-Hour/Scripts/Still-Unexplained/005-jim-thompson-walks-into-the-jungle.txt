## hook

[measured] On Easter Sunday in 1967, one of the most famous Americans in Asia went out for a short walk in the Malaysian hills, and in spite of one of the largest searches the country had ever seen, not one trace of him was ever found.

[steady] His name was Jim Thompson. He was the man who brought Thai silk back to life, and this is the story of the afternoon he vanished in the Cameron Highlands, a cool, green hill resort in the middle of Malaysia.

## story

[warmly] Thompson was an architect from Delaware who had served in the Office of Strategic Services, America's wartime spy service, and came to Bangkok at the end of the Second World War. He stayed. He fell in love with Thai silk, which was then woven by hand by only a few families. He brought them new dyes, found them buyers in New York, and made it famous. By 1967 he was sixty-one, rich, admired, and the owner of a beautiful teak house in Bangkok that is now a museum.

[steady] That Easter weekend he went on holiday with friends to a bungalow called Moonlight Cottage, high in the Cameron Highlands. On Sunday the 26th of March, after lunch, his friends went to lie down. At about three in the afternoon, according to their statements, they heard his footsteps on the gravel outside. He was thought to be going for a short walk. He had left his cigarettes and his lighter on the veranda, and also the pills he took for his gall bladder. He was never seen again.

[measured] The search began that evening and lasted for weeks. Several hundred people took part: police, soldiers, British Gurkha troops, local Orang Asli trackers who knew the forest better than anyone, and dogs. Thompson knew the jungle. He had trained for it in the war. And the hills around the cottage were full of paths. They found nothing. No footprint, no scrap of cloth, no body.

## question

[thoughtful] The explanation many investigators found most likely was the simplest one: that he got lost or hurt in the forest, perhaps fell, or was struck by a car on a hill road and hidden by a frightened driver. Others pointed to his past in American intelligence, and believed he was taken, or chose to disappear. No evidence for any of these has ever been found.

[steady] What the simple answer cannot explain is why, in those few square miles of hillside, searched for weeks by hundreds of people who knew every path, the forest gave back nothing at all. On the veranda of Moonlight Cottage, his cigarettes were still waiting for him.
