## hook

[steady] In the 12th century, two English chroniclers wrote down the same strange story: two children with green skin had appeared at the edge of a Suffolk village, speaking no language anyone could recognise. This is the tale of the Green Children of Woolpit.

## story

[measured] Our two sources are William of Newburgh and Ralph of Coggeshall, both monks, and both careful men. William admits that he did not believe it at first, and that the weight of witnesses wore him down. Ralph says he had it from a local knight, Sir Richard de Calne, who took the children in.

[thoughtful] Woolpit took its name from the wolf pits, big ditches dug to trap wolves. At harvest time, the reapers found a boy and a girl beside one, in strange clothes, and with skin the colour of leaves. They were frightened and wept. For days they refused all food, until they saw some freshly cut bean stalks. They tore them open, looking in the stalks for the beans, and then ate the beans raw.

[steady] The boy was the younger, and he grew sickly and died. The girl lived. Slowly, on an ordinary diet, the green faded from her skin. She learned English, and was baptised. Asked where she came from, she said it was a land where the sun never rose, with only a twilight, and that she and her brother had been following their father's cattle, then heard a sound of bells, and came out into the blinding light. She lived as a servant in the knight's house, and William says she later married a man from King's Lynn.

## question

[measured] The best-known explanation came from a researcher, Paul Harris, in 1998. He suggested they were Flemish orphans. Flemish immigrants, from what is now Belgium, had been settling in eastern England and were persecuted in the 1170s. Lost, half-starved children might have a greenish tinge from a form of anaemia called chlorosis, and their speech would sound foreign. Critics point out that a knight of that time would very likely have recognised Flemish. The children have also been explained as a folk tale, and people at the time wondered whether they came from a land under the ground. The fact that it was written by two separate men in sober Latin still stands. So where had that girl been, when she remembered a country with no sun?
