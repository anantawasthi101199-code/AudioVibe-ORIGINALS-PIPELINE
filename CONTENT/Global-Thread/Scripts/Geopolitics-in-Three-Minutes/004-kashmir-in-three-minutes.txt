## hook

[measured] India and Pakistan have fought three wars over one Himalayan region since 1947, and the line that divides it is about seven hundred and forty kilometres long.

[steady] This is the story of Kashmir, a region of high mountains and a green, lake-filled valley, claimed in full by both India and Pakistan, and divided between them.

## explain

[steady] In 1947 Britain left India and divided it into two countries: India, and Pakistan, created as a homeland for the subcontinent's Muslims. More than five hundred princely states, ruled by their own princes under British protection, had to choose a side. Jammu and Kashmir had a Hindu ruler, Maharaja Hari Singh, and a mostly Muslim population. He waited. In October 1947, armed tribesmen from Pakistan crossed into Kashmir. The maharaja asked India for help, and signed a document joining his state to India. Indian troops flew in, and the first war began.

[measured] In January 1949 the United Nations arranged a ceasefire. A UN resolution called for a vote of the people of Kashmir, once troops had been withdrawn. Each side said the other had to withdraw first, and the vote was never held. Think of two families who both hold a deed to the same house, and a judge who said, let the people living in it decide, once you both step outside. Neither stepped outside. Wars followed in 1965 and, on a smaller scale, at Kargil in 1999. In 1972, in the Simla Agreement, both countries agreed to settle their differences bilaterally, between themselves, and the ceasefire line became the Line of Control.

[steady] India's government says the 1947 accession was legal and final, that Jammu and Kashmir is an integral part of India, and that Pakistan backs militant groups who attack there. In 2019 India ended the region's special self-governing status. Pakistan's government says Kashmiris have a right, under UN resolutions, to decide their own future, and that it gives them moral and diplomatic support. Pakistan calls the area it governs Azad Kashmir, free Kashmir. India calls it Pakistan-occupied Kashmir. China controls a third part, Aksai Chin.

## close

[measured] In April 2025, gunmen killed twenty-six people, most of them tourists, at Pahalgam in Indian-administered Kashmir. India blamed Pakistan, which denied any role. In May the two countries struck each other's territory for four days, before agreeing to a ceasefire. India also put on hold a 1960 treaty that shares the waters of the Indus river system, on which millions of farmers in Pakistan depend.

[steady] As of November 2026 there have been reports of fresh exchanges of fire across the Line of Control, and the treaty remains on hold. The water of the Indus still rises in the Himalayas and flows past soldiers on both sides of the line.
