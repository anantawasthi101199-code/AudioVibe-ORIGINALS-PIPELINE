## opening

[steady] In July 2021, the president of Russia published a long essay saying that Russians and Ukrainians were one people. Seven months later, his army crossed the border.

[measured] This is the first chapter of the story of Russia and Ukraine. It goes back more than a thousand years, because both countries trace themselves to the same medieval state, and they tell its story in opposite ways. It covers that state, the Cossacks, the Russian Empire, a short-lived Ukrainian republic, and a famine that killed millions.

[thoughtful] It matters to you even if you have never been to either country. Ukraine is one of the world's great grain exporters, this war moved the price of bread and heating, and it is the largest war in Europe since 1945. To follow any of the talks, you need to know why history itself is one of the battlefields.

[steady] So let's go back to the river where it starts.

## roots

[measured] In the late 800s, a state grew up along the river Dnipro, with its capital at Kyiv, today the capital of Ukraine. We call it Kyivan Rus. It was ruled by a family of princes with Scandinavian roots, and its people spoke Slavic languages. In 988 its ruler, Volodymyr, took Christianity from Constantinople. Moscow at that time did not exist. It first appears in the written record in 1147, as a small settlement.

[thoughtful] In 1240 Mongol armies destroyed Kyiv, and the lands of Rus broke apart. Here the two stories divide. In the north east, Moscow slowly grew under Mongol overlordship, and later called itself the heir of Kyiv. In the south west, the lands around Kyiv passed to Lithuania and then Poland. Think of a family house lost in a fire, where two branches of the family each say the deeds belong to them.

[steady] On the open grasslands to the south, a new kind of society appeared in the 1500s: the Cossacks, free soldiers and farmers, many of them runaway peasants. In 1648 a Cossack leader, Bohdan Khmelnytsky, led a huge uprising against Polish rule. It included terrible massacres of Jews and Poles. In 1654, needing an ally, he made an agreement with the Tsar of Russia at a town called Pereiaslav. Russia later said this reunited one people. Ukrainian historians say it was a military alliance between two parties, which Russia turned into rule.

[measured] Through the 1700s and 1800s the Russian Empire absorbed most of what is now Ukraine. The empire called the Ukrainian lands Little Russia. In 1863 a government order said a separate Ukrainian language had never existed and could not exist, and restricted books in it. In 1876 a further order went further still. Meanwhile, a Ukrainian poet, Taras Shevchenko, born a serf, wrote the verses that became the heart of a national identity.

[thoughtful] In 1917 the Russian Empire collapsed in revolution. In Kyiv a council declared a Ukrainian People's Republic, and in January 1918 it declared full independence. It lasted only a few chaotic years of war, fought among Germans, Poles, the Red Army and rival Russian forces. By 1921 most of it was absorbed into what became the Soviet Union. It was brief, but Ukrainians today point to it as proof that their wish for a state is not new.

[steady] Then came the darkest moment in this chapter. In 1932 and 1933, under the Soviet leader Joseph Stalin, farms had already been forced into state collectives, and officials took grain from Ukrainian villages to meet impossible targets. Ukraine's borders were closed so hungry villagers could not leave to look for food. Ukrainians call this the Holodomor, which means death by hunger. Demographers in Ukraine estimate about three point nine million deaths there.

## sides

[steady] These same events are told very differently in Kyiv and in Moscow, so here is each in its own terms.

[measured] Moscow says Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians share one origin in Kyivan Rus, one faith and one history. President Vladimir Putin says modern Ukraine was largely an invention of Soviet leaders, who drew its borders. Russia says the famine of the early 1930s was a shared tragedy that also struck Russia and Kazakhstan, and that it was not aimed at any one nation.

[measured] Kyiv says Ukraine is a separate nation with its own language, its own history and its own unbroken wish to govern itself. Ukrainian leaders say Kyiv was a great city when Moscow was a forest, and that Russia took the name and the story of Rus for itself. Ukraine says the Holodomor was a genocide, a deliberate attempt to break the Ukrainian nation. Its parliament passed a law saying so in 2006, and many other countries, along with the European Parliament, have since used the same word.

[thoughtful] Historians still argue over whether the famine meets the legal definition of genocide. The record leaves less doubt on the closing of the borders, the grain taken and the villages that starved.

## now

[steady] As of November 2026, that argument about history is being fought with weapons. Russia began its full-scale invasion on the 24th of February 2022. The UN human rights office says it has verified the deaths of more than fifteen thousand civilians since then, and says the true number is higher. Both armies keep their own losses close, and independent estimates run to hundreds of thousands of soldiers killed and wounded on each side.

[measured] The United States has arranged a new round of working-level talks between the two sides in Abu Dhabi, planned for the 10th and 11th of November. The American envoy, Steve Witkoff, says they will cover post-war security, de-escalation and humanitarian issues. Moscow says Ukraine must give up all of the eastern region called the Donbas, including land Russia has not taken. Kyiv says it will not hand over land it holds, and President Volodymyr Zelensky says any deal needs strong security guarantees.

[thoughtful] What to watch is whether either position moves in that room. The two delegations will be arguing over the map of today. Behind them is the older argument over whose story that map tells, and it still begins on a river, in Kyiv, with a prince named Volodymyr, a name that Ukraine's president now carries.
